Akagera National Park

Rwanda's savannah Big Five park: rhinos, lions and the Lake Ihema boat safari.

Akagera National Park is Rwanda's only savannah national park and its most important destination for a traditional African wildlife safari. Located in eastern Rwanda along the Tanzanian border, the park combines open plains, rolling hills, woodland, wetlands and a chain of lakes into a landscape that looks very different from the volcanic mountains and rainforests of western Rwanda.

Akagera is also one of Africa's strongest conservation recovery stories.

The park suffered severe wildlife losses during decades of political instability, habitat pressure and poaching. Since 2010, however, the Rwanda Development Board and African Parks have worked together to rebuild the ecosystem. Lions returned in 2015, eastern black rhinos in 2017, and southern white rhinos in 2021, followed by another major introduction of 70 white rhinos in 2025.

Today, Akagera supports thriving populations of elephants, buffalo, lions, leopards, eastern black rhinos and southern white rhinos, alongside giraffes, zebras, antelope, hippos and almost 500 recorded bird species.

The result is a safari destination that can now be combined naturally with Rwanda's mountain-gorilla experience in Volcanoes National Park.

What makes Akagera National Park different?

Akagera is different from Rwanda's other major wildlife destinations because the experience is built around savannah wildlife rather than primates or montane forests.

Volcanoes National Park is defined by mountain gorillas, golden monkeys and the Virunga volcanoes. Nyungwe is dominated by rainforest, chimpanzees and forest primates.

Akagera is about open savannah, elephants and buffalo, lions and leopards, black and white rhinos, giraffes and zebras, wetlands and lakes, boat safaris, birdwatching, night game drives and Rwanda's conservation success story.

This makes Akagera particularly valuable for travelers who want to experience two completely different sides of Rwanda on the same trip.

A traveler can track mountain gorillas in the misty Virungas and, a few days later, watch elephants crossing an open savannah or hippos gathering along Lake Ihema.

Where is Akagera National Park?

Akagera is located in eastern Rwanda, close to the country's border with Tanzania.

The park is named after the Akagera River, which forms part of the eastern boundary and feeds into a network of lakes and wetlands.

The park covers approximately 1,122 km² according to African Parks' current park information. Its relatively low elevation and warmer climate create conditions that are very different from Rwanda's highland environments.

This geographic contrast is one of the reasons Akagera works so well within a Rwanda safari itinerary.

The landscape of Akagera

Akagera is not simply a large grassland. It is a complex mosaic of open savannah, woodland, rolling hills, wetlands, papyrus swamps, lakes, riverine habitats and low-lying plains.

Visit Rwanda describes the park as a combination of savannah, woodland, wetland and a dozen lakes.

The wetland system is particularly important. Akagera is recognized by African Parks as Central Africa's largest protected wetland and Rwanda's last remaining refuge for savannah-adapted wildlife.

This makes the park valuable not only as a safari destination but also as a major freshwater and wetland ecosystem.

The lakes of Akagera

A chain of lakes runs through the park, creating permanent or semi-permanent water habitats. Among the most important are Lake Ihema, Lake Rwanyakazinga, Lake Shakani, Lake Hago and Lake Mihindi.

These lakes support hippos, crocodiles, waterbirds and other aquatic wildlife.

They also create one of Akagera's biggest advantages over conventional savannah parks: you can experience wildlife from the water as well as from a safari vehicle.

Lake Ihema boat safari

The Lake Ihema boat safari is one of Akagera's signature experiences.

Instead of following wildlife tracks in a 4x4, travelers move quietly across the lake while looking toward the shoreline and islands.

The experience can include sightings of hippos, Nile crocodiles, elephants, buffalo, waterbuck, antelope, African fish eagles, kingfishers, herons and other wetland birds.

Visit Rwanda specifically highlights Lake Ihema for its hippo populations, crocodiles and abundant waterbirds.

For birdwatchers, the boat safari is particularly valuable because many species are easier to observe from the water than from a moving vehicle.

Wildlife in Akagera National Park

Akagera's wildlife recovery is one of the defining stories of the park.

African Parks currently describes the park as supporting thriving populations of lion, leopard, elephant, rhino, buffalo, zebra, giraffe and antelope, alongside almost 500 bird species.

This means Akagera can now provide a genuine Big Five safari experience.

However, travelers should understand that Big Five status does not mean every species is equally easy to see. Elephants and buffalo may be encountered relatively regularly. Lions can provide excellent sightings. Rhinos are carefully monitored and protected. Leopards remain considerably more elusive.

The quality of the safari therefore comes from the entire ecosystem rather than simply completing a five-animal checklist.

The Big Five in Akagera

Lion

Lions were hunted out of Akagera during the 1990s. A founder population was reintroduced in 2015, with additional lions introduced in 2017. The population has since expanded through successful breeding.

Today, lions are an established part of the Akagera ecosystem. They are most often searched for around productive prey areas and open grasslands.

Early morning and late afternoon game drives are particularly useful because predators are generally more active during cooler periods.

Leopards

Leopards are present in Akagera, but they should be considered one of the park's more difficult sightings.

Unlike giraffes or buffalo, leopards can disappear almost completely into woodland and dense vegetation. They are primarily nocturnal and crepuscular hunters, so patience and an experienced guide are important.

Travelers should therefore enjoy a leopard sighting as a bonus rather than treating it as a guaranteed part of a short safari.

Elephants

Elephants are one of Akagera's important conservation success stories. The species had suffered severe losses from illegal hunting, but elephants were reintroduced to the park in 1975. Today they form an established component of the ecosystem.

African Parks reported identifying more than 100 individual elephants through its elephant identification work, with the total park population estimated at around 142 in the cited 2025 survey.

Elephants can be encountered around woodland, open country and water.

Buffalo

Cape buffalo are widespread across suitable areas of Akagera. Large herds can be particularly impressive when encountered on open plains.

They also become an important prey base for lions, helping support the park's recovering predator population.

Black rhinos

Eastern black rhinos returned to Akagera in 2017, when 18 animals were reintroduced after a 10-year absence from Rwanda. Five additional black rhinos were moved from European zoos in 2019 to strengthen genetic diversity.

This population is particularly important because Akagera represents Rwanda's only protected population of eastern black rhinos.

Rhino sightings are never guaranteed, but their presence adds significant conservation importance to the safari.

White rhinos

Akagera's white rhino story is more recent. In 2021, 30 southern white rhinos were introduced from South Africa. Then, in June 2025, another 70 southern white rhinos arrived in one of the largest international rhino translocations of its kind.

The population has continued to develop, with births recorded following the introductions. As of September 2026, African Parks reports that Akagera's white rhino population stands at just over 100 animals.

This is important when planning current safari content: Akagera is not simply a destination where white rhinos were recently introduced. It is now developing into a significant secure population.

Giraffes

Giraffes are among the more visually distinctive animals in Akagera. They are particularly striking against the park's open plains, woodland and rolling hills.

Their height allows them to feed on vegetation above the reach of many other herbivores, making them an important part of the savannah browsing community.

Zebras

Zebra are another common safari target. They are often encountered in open grassland alongside other grazing species.

Their presence also contributes to the predator-prey dynamics that support Akagera's recovering lion population.

Hippos and crocodiles

The lakes and wetlands provide habitat for large populations of hippos. Lake Ihema is particularly well known for hippo viewing. Nile crocodiles can also be seen along the shoreline and around suitable sections of the lake.

The boat safari therefore provides a different wildlife experience from the game drive. Instead of searching for animals across kilometres of savannah, travelers concentrate on the water's edge, islands and shallow areas.

Antelope and other herbivores

Akagera supports a broad selection of antelope and other grazing and browsing species. Depending on location and season, travelers may encounter impala, topi, waterbuck, bushbuck, oribi, eland, duiker, reedbuck and kob.

The diversity of herbivores is important because it supports the park's predator populations while also creating varied wildlife-viewing opportunities.

Birdwatching in Akagera

Akagera is one of Rwanda's most important birding destinations. African Parks reports almost 500 bird species, while Visit Rwanda similarly highlights approximately 490 species.

The combination of wetlands, open savannah, woodland, lakes, papyrus and riverine habitat creates a wide range of bird habitats.

Among the species associated with Akagera are shoebill, African fish eagle, papyrus gonolek, kingfishers, herons, egrets, storks, raptors and waterbirds.

The shoebill is particularly sought after by birdwatchers. It is not a guaranteed sighting, but the wetland environment makes Akagera one of Rwanda's important places to search for this unusual species.

Game drives in Akagera

Game drives are the foundation of the Akagera safari. Morning drives are particularly useful for predators and active herbivores. Afternoon drives allow travelers to explore different sections of the park before sunset.

The open nature of many parts of Akagera can provide excellent visibility, while woodland and wetland areas create more challenging wildlife-viewing conditions. A good guide therefore makes a significant difference.

The objective is not simply to drive around until an animal appears. It is to understand tracks, habitat, recent sightings, animal behavior and changing conditions.

Night game drives

Akagera also offers night game drives, creating a completely different safari experience. After sunset, species that are difficult to observe during daylight become active.

Depending on conditions and luck, travelers may encounter lions, leopards, spotted hyenas, genets, civets, bushbabies and other nocturnal wildlife.

Visit Rwanda specifically highlights guided night drives as an opportunity to search for nocturnal wildlife, including lions and leopards.

A night drive should not be treated simply as another game drive. The atmosphere changes completely once the sun disappears. Sounds become more important, the beam of the spotlight replaces natural daylight and animals that were hidden during the afternoon can suddenly become visible.

Fishing in Akagera

Fishing is another activity associated with Akagera's lakes. Lake Shakani is particularly associated with sport fishing.

This can provide a useful break from conventional game drives for travelers staying several nights.

Fishing should, however, be understood as an activity within a protected ecosystem and undertaken according to the park's current regulations.

Akagera's conservation turnaround

Akagera is one of the clearest examples of how long-term protected-area management can rebuild a severely damaged ecosystem.

When African Parks entered into a management partnership with the Rwanda Development Board in 2010, the park faced major conservation and community challenges. Since then, management has focused on anti-poaching, ranger deployment, wildlife monitoring, habitat management, species reintroductions, tourism development and community engagement.

The results have been substantial. Lions returned. Black rhinos returned. White rhinos were introduced. Wildlife populations recovered. Tourism increased.

African Parks reports that Akagera recorded no loss of high-value species to poaching since 2010.

Tourism and conservation

Akagera demonstrates why tourism can be more than simply a way of seeing wildlife. Tourism revenue contributes directly to the park's ability to fund conservation and local employment.

African Parks reports that tourism generated 97% of the park's annual revenue in 2024, while 2025 became the first full year in which Akagera no longer required RDB operational funding.

The park now employs more than 300 full-time staff, with the majority coming from surrounding communities.

This creates an important link: more responsible tourism → more revenue → stronger park management → stronger wildlife protection → greater long-term conservation value.

Akagera and local communities

Conservation cannot succeed if neighboring communities receive no benefit from a protected area. Akagera's management therefore includes community development and education programmes.

African Parks reports that more than 3,000 community members visit the park annually through environmental education programmes, while eco-club activities reach more than 2,300 students across 76 programmes.

The park also operates community-oriented enterprises such as the Gishanda Fish Farm. These programmes help connect conservation with tangible local benefits.

Best time to visit Akagera National Park

Akagera can be visited throughout the year, but the experience changes significantly with the seasons.

Dry season

The drier months can be particularly productive for traditional wildlife viewing. As water becomes less abundant, wildlife movements can become more concentrated around remaining water sources. Vegetation may also be less dense, improving visibility.

This can be particularly useful for travelers whose main priority is finding large mammals.

Wet season

The landscape becomes greener and more dramatic. Bird activity can be excellent, and migratory species add additional interest during relevant periods.

The trade-off is that wildlife can become more dispersed because water is available in more places. Some tracks can also become more challenging.

For photographers and birders, however, the greener season can be extremely rewarding.

How many days do you need in Akagera?

2 nights

A short safari can still provide a meaningful introduction. You can combine a game drive, the Lake Ihema boat safari and departure. This works particularly well for travelers combining Akagera with Volcanoes National Park.

3 nights

Three nights is a much better option. It allows time for several game drives, the boat safari, more relaxed wildlife viewing and an optional night drive.

4 nights

Four nights suits travelers who want to explore the park more thoroughly. It gives you more opportunities for predators, rhinos, elephants, birding, night wildlife, photography and different sections of the park.

How to get to Akagera National Park

Akagera is one of Rwanda's more accessible safari destinations. The park can be reached by road from Kigali.

Visit Rwanda's current itinerary information describes the drive between Akagera and Kigali as approximately three hours, depending on the exact starting and ending points.

This relatively easy access is one reason Akagera works so well as a short safari extension from Kigali. It is also practical for travelers arriving in Rwanda specifically for gorilla trekking.

Where to stay in Akagera

Accommodation can be found in and around the park, ranging from comfortable safari lodges to more basic camps. The right choice depends on budget, desired level of comfort, location, whether you want a private safari atmosphere and whether you want easy access to particular park activities.

Staying inside or close to the park is generally preferable for travelers who want to maximize wildlife-viewing time. An early start matters on safari.

Being close to the park reduces unnecessary road transfers and gives you more time in the ecosystem.

Akagera and Volcanoes National Park

These two parks should not be viewed as competitors. They provide completely different experiences.

Akagera is a savannah and wetlands ecosystem whose signature wildlife is Big Five and savannah species, with game drives and a boat safari as the main activities and a landscape of lakes, plains and woodland. Volcanoes is montane forest where the signature wildlife is mountain gorillas, the main activity is gorilla trekking and the landscape is volcanoes and rainforest.

Both parks are excellent for birding. Akagera's main specialist experience is its rhino and Big Five safari, while the specialist experience at Volcanoes is gorilla trekking. Two to four nights is an ideal stay for either park.

A combined itinerary creates a much more complete picture of Rwanda's biodiversity.

Akagera vs Serengeti

Akagera should not be marketed as a smaller version of the Serengeti. The two parks offer different scales and safari experiences.

The Serengeti is famous for enormous open plains, large migratory herds and a much larger landscape. Akagera is more compact and combines savannah with extensive wetlands and lakes. Its strength is variety.

A traveler can experience a game drive, a boat safari, a night drive, rhino conservation and birding without leaving the same protected area. That combination is a major part of Akagera's identity.

Akagera vs Masai Mara

The Masai Mara is particularly famous for high-density predator viewing and the Great Migration.

Akagera offers a quieter and more varied landscape, with wetlands and lake-based wildlife viewing adding another dimension. Akagera is also particularly interesting for travelers who want to understand wildlife restoration and conservation as part of the safari itself.

The Masai Mara is generally the stronger choice for travelers whose primary goal is migration or classic East African predator scenes. Akagera is stronger for travelers who want a compact Rwanda safari combining savannah, wetlands and conservation.

Akagera National Park FAQs

Is Akagera National Park worth visiting?

Yes, particularly if you want a traditional savannah safari in Rwanda.

Akagera offers something that Rwanda's mountain and rainforest parks cannot: open wildlife landscapes with lions, elephants, buffalo, giraffes and rhinos.

The park is also unusually diverse because it combines savannah with lakes, wetlands and woodland. For travelers already planning a gorilla trek, adding Akagera creates a much broader wildlife experience rather than repeating the same type of environment.

Can you see the Big Five in Akagera?

Yes. Akagera now supports lion, leopard, elephant, buffalo and rhino, and Visit Rwanda explicitly promotes the park as a destination where visitors can hope to see the Big Five.

However, the phrase "Big Five" should not be interpreted as a guarantee.

Elephants, buffalo and some other large mammals can be relatively straightforward to encounter, while leopards are naturally elusive. Rhinos are also carefully managed and protected, so sightings depend on location, conditions and luck.

Are there rhinos in Akagera?

Yes. Akagera has both eastern black rhinos and southern white rhinos.

Eastern black rhinos were reintroduced in 2017, with additional animals introduced in 2019. Southern white rhinos arrived in 2021, followed by another 70 animals in June 2025.

As of September 2026, African Parks reports that the white rhino population is just over 100 animals.

This makes rhino conservation one of the most important reasons to pay attention to Akagera beyond its conventional safari appeal.

Can you see lions in Akagera?

Yes. Lions were reintroduced to Akagera in 2015 after having disappeared from the park.

The population has since reproduced successfully, and lions are now an established component of the ecosystem. A morning or late-afternoon game drive is usually the best time to search for them.

As with any wild predator, however, sightings remain dependent on animal movements.

Is Akagera good for a safari from Kigali?

Very much so. One of Akagera's major advantages is its accessibility from Kigali.

The drive is approximately three hours according to Visit Rwanda's current itinerary information. This makes it possible to have a genuine wildlife safari without committing to the longer transfers required for some other East African destinations.

It is particularly practical for travelers with limited time.

How many days should I spend in Akagera?

Two nights is enough for a short introduction.

Three nights is a better choice for most travelers because it allows several wildlife-viewing opportunities and time for the Lake Ihema boat safari.

Four nights or more is appropriate for serious photographers, birders or travelers who want to explore the park at a slower pace.

The more time available, the less the safari depends on what happens during one particular game drive.

Is the Lake Ihema boat safari worth doing?

Yes. The boat safari is one of Akagera's most distinctive activities because it changes the way you experience the ecosystem.

Instead of searching across grassland from a vehicle, you observe hippos, crocodiles and waterbirds from the lake while also watching the shoreline for elephants and other wildlife.

It is particularly valuable for photographers and birdwatchers. For a first Akagera safari, the boat cruise should normally be included if time allows.

Can you see hippos in Akagera?

Yes. Hippos are strongly associated with Akagera's lakes and wetlands. Lake Ihema is particularly well known for its hippo populations.

A boat safari provides one of the best ways to observe them because you can approach their aquatic habitat while maintaining the appropriate distance.

Can you see crocodiles in Akagera?

Yes. Nile crocodiles occur in the park's aquatic environments and can be observed particularly well during boat trips. They may be seen lying along lake edges or partially submerged in the water.

The boat experience gives travelers a better perspective on crocodile behavior than a conventional game drive through the savannah.

Is Akagera good for birdwatching?

Yes. Akagera has recorded almost 500 bird species, making it one of Rwanda's most important birding destinations.

Its combination of wetlands, lakes, woodland and savannah creates habitat for both waterbirds and terrestrial species. The shoebill is one of the most sought-after sightings.

Birding can be incorporated into a normal safari, but serious birders should allocate additional time because finding specialist species requires patience.

Can you see a shoebill in Akagera?

Yes, Akagera is one of the places in Rwanda where birders can search for shoebills. However, the species is not guaranteed.

Shoebills are associated with particular wetland habitats and can be difficult to locate. A specialist guide and enough time are important if the bird is one of your primary targets.

Does Akagera offer night game drives?

Yes. Night game drives are available and provide access to a completely different range of wildlife activity.

Nocturnal species become active after dark, while some predators are more comfortable hunting once temperatures fall. Visit Rwanda specifically highlights night drives as an opportunity to search for nocturnal wildlife including lions and leopards.

Is Akagera good for families?

Yes. Akagera can be particularly suitable for families because it offers several types of activity rather than relying entirely on long game drives.

Children can experience elephants and giraffes, boat safaris, hippos and crocodiles, birds and savannah landscapes.

Private vehicles are particularly useful for families because the itinerary can be adapted around children's energy levels and interests. Age restrictions and activity-specific rules should still be confirmed when booking.

Is Akagera suitable for first-time safari travelers?

Yes. Akagera is one of the more accessible ways to experience an African savannah safari.

It combines relatively straightforward access from Kigali with classic wildlife, comfortable accommodation options and a variety of activities.

It is especially suitable for travelers who want to experience safari but do not have enough time for a long East African overland itinerary.

Is Akagera a good alternative to the Serengeti?

It depends on the traveler.

Akagera is not an alternative to the Serengeti if the goal is the Great Migration or vast open plains extending across a huge ecosystem.

It can be an excellent alternative for travelers who want a shorter, more compact safari with wetlands, boat trips, Big Five wildlife and conservation-focused experiences.

The two destinations should therefore be positioned according to search intent rather than treated as interchangeable parks.

Can you combine Akagera with gorilla trekking?

Yes, and this is one of Rwanda's strongest safari combinations.

Akagera provides savannah wildlife. Volcanoes National Park provides mountain gorilla trekking. The contrast between the two experiences is substantial enough that the combination does not feel repetitive.

A five- to seven-day Rwanda itinerary can therefore include both without requiring travelers to visit multiple countries.

Akagera or Volcanoes National Park: which is better?

Neither is universally better.

Choose Akagera if your priorities are Big Five wildlife, game drives, the boat safari, rhinos, savannah, birding and wetlands.

Choose Volcanoes if your priorities are mountain gorillas, golden monkeys, volcano hiking, montane rainforest and Dian Fossey and gorilla conservation.

If budget and time allow, combining them is usually more rewarding than choosing one.

What is the best time to visit Akagera?

Akagera is open year-round.

The drier periods are generally attractive for conventional game viewing because wildlife can become more concentrated around water and vegetation can be less dense.

The wetter periods offer greener landscapes, excellent birding opportunities and dramatic scenery. The best month therefore depends on whether your priority is wildlife concentration, photography, birding, landscape or overall weather.

Is Akagera a luxury safari destination?

It can be. The park has accommodation ranging from comfortable camps to higher-end safari lodges.

A private 4x4, private guide and premium lodge can create a genuinely high-quality safari experience. However, Akagera's greatest luxury is not simply the room.

It is the ability to combine high-quality accommodation with relatively easy access to a recovering wild ecosystem.

What makes Akagera important for conservation?

Akagera demonstrates how a heavily depleted ecosystem can recover through sustained management.

Since 2010, conservation efforts have included improved law enforcement, wildlife monitoring, community engagement and major species reintroductions. Lions returned. Black rhinos returned. White rhinos were introduced. Wildlife populations increased.

The park also became financially self-sustaining through tourism and other revenue-generating activities. For travelers interested in conservation, Akagera offers a safari where the restoration story is part of the destination itself.

Is Akagera safe for safari?

Akagera is a professionally managed protected area with a dedicated conservation and law-enforcement system.

Visitors should still follow their guide's instructions and park regulations because they are entering a genuine wild environment containing large mammals and predators.

Self-driving is possible, but first-time visitors may benefit from using a professional guide who understands the park's wildlife, roads and seasonal conditions.

Can you self-drive in Akagera?

Yes. Visit Rwanda states that visitors can explore the park by vehicle, including self-drive, although it recommends using a guide.

For travelers unfamiliar with African safari driving, a professional guide is generally preferable. A guide can interpret tracks, identify animals, understand recent sightings and manage the timing of game drives.

How long should an Akagera safari be compared with a gorilla trek?

A gorilla trek itself can be organized as a relatively short experience, while Akagera benefits from multiple game drives because wildlife sightings are unpredictable.

For a Rwanda wildlife trip, a useful structure is often 2–3 nights Akagera + 2–3 nights Volcanoes.

This allows enough time for both savannah wildlife and mountain gorilla experiences without making either destination feel like a rushed stop.
