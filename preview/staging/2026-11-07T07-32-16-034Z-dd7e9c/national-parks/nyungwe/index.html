Nyungwe National Park

Rwanda's rainforest: chimpanzee trekking, the canopy walkway and Albertine Rift endemics.

Nyungwe National Park is Rwanda's great rainforest destination, covering approximately 1,019 km² of dense Afromontane forest, bamboo-covered slopes, wetlands and grasslands in the country's southwest. It is one of Africa's most important montane rainforest ecosystems and a major biodiversity stronghold within the Albertine Rift.

Unlike Akagera's savannah or Volcanoes National Park's volcanic mountain environment, Nyungwe is defined by ancient rainforest, chimpanzees, forest primates, endemic birds, waterfalls, hiking trails and the famous canopy walkway.

The park contains around 85 known mammal species, including 13 primate species, and more than 1,100 recorded plant species. African Parks also records 345 bird species, while UNESCO's World Heritage documentation records 317 species within the inscribed property.

Nyungwe was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2023 because of its exceptional biodiversity and importance for the conservation of Afromontane ecosystems.

For travelers, however, Nyungwe is not simply a place to look at trees.

It is a destination for chimpanzee trekking, primate encounters, birdwatching, forest hiking and experiencing one of Rwanda's most biologically important landscapes.

What makes Nyungwe National Park different?

Nyungwe offers a completely different safari from the other major Rwanda destinations.

Akagera is Rwanda's savannah and Big Five destination. Volcanoes is Rwanda's mountain-gorilla destination. Nyungwe is the country's rainforest and primate destination.

Its greatest attraction is therefore not a single animal or landmark. It is the ecosystem itself.

You enter a dense mountain forest where visibility can be limited, sounds travel through the canopy and wildlife may appear only briefly before disappearing into vegetation.

That makes Nyungwe a more immersive and exploratory safari experience.

Where is Nyungwe National Park?

Nyungwe is located in southwestern Rwanda, close to the country's border with Burundi and within the Albertine Rift region.

The forest occupies a mountainous landscape with elevations ranging approximately from 1,480 metres to 2,950 metres above sea level.

This elevation range helps explain the extraordinary variety of habitats found within the park. The forest includes:

  • montane rainforest
  • bamboo
  • high-altitude wetlands
  • peat bogs
  • moorlands
  • grasslands
  • thickets

UNESCO identifies Nyungwe as one of the largest remaining montane forest areas in the Albertine Rift and notes that the habitats within the property remain more than 95% intact.

Nyungwe and the Albertine Rift

Nyungwe forms part of the Albertine Rift, one of Africa's most biologically important regions.

The Albertine Rift contains exceptionally high levels of endemism, meaning that many species found there occur nowhere else or have extremely restricted distributions.

This is one reason Nyungwe is more important than its size alone might suggest. The forest protects habitats for globally threatened mammals, birds, amphibians and plants.

African Parks reports that at least 265 of Nyungwe's 1,100 recorded plant species are endemic to its mountains, while 30 of its 345 bird species are Albertine Rift endemics.

For a safari website, this is an important distinction. Nyungwe should not be marketed simply as a rainforest where you can see chimpanzees. It is a major biodiversity and endemism destination.

Nyungwe as a UNESCO World Heritage Site

Nyungwe National Park became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2023.

UNESCO recognized the park under the natural heritage criterion for its exceptional biodiversity and importance to the conservation of threatened species and ecosystems.

The World Heritage property includes three component forests:

  • Nyungwe Natural Forest
  • Cyamudongo Natural Forest
  • Gisakura Natural Forest

Together, the inscribed property covers more than 101,900 hectares.

The designation gives Nyungwe an important position within Rwanda's conservation story and strengthens its value for travelers interested in nature beyond conventional wildlife viewing.

The rainforest landscape

Nyungwe's forest is ancient and exceptionally diverse.

The canopy creates a cool, humid environment compared with Rwanda's lower savannah areas. On the ground, visitors encounter:

  • enormous trees
  • vines
  • ferns
  • moss
  • orchids
  • bamboo
  • dense undergrowth
  • streams
  • wetlands

The forest changes dramatically with altitude. Lower areas can feel warmer and more humid, while higher sections become cooler and more open.

This variation contributes to the park's unusual mixture of species and vegetation.

Nyungwe's importance for Rwanda's water

Nyungwe is not only a wildlife habitat. It is also a critical water catchment for Rwanda.

African Parks states that the forest feeds two of Africa's major river systems, the Congo and Nile, and provides a significant portion of Rwanda's freshwater.

This gives the forest importance far beyond tourism. Protecting Nyungwe protects forests, wildlife, wetlands and water resources simultaneously.

Chimpanzee trekking in Nyungwe

Chimpanzee trekking is Nyungwe's signature wildlife experience. The eastern chimpanzee is one of the park's most important species and is globally threatened.

A chimpanzee trek is fundamentally different from a conventional game drive. There are no open plains where animals can easily be spotted from hundreds of metres away.

Instead, trekkers move through dense forest searching for signs of chimpanzee activity. Your guide may follow:

  • vocalizations
  • movement through the canopy
  • feeding signs
  • nests
  • tracks
  • recently disturbed vegetation

Even when chimpanzees are nearby, dense foliage can make them difficult to see. That uncertainty is part of the experience.

What is a Nyungwe chimpanzee trek like?

A typical chimpanzee trek begins early because chimpanzees are active during the morning and may move through the forest as the day progresses.

After briefing and meeting the guides, visitors enter the forest and begin searching for the troop. The duration can vary considerably. Some days produce relatively quick sightings. Other days require several hours of walking.

Terrain is also an important factor. Nyungwe is mountainous, and forest trails can become slippery after rain.

This means chimpanzee trekking is a genuine forest hike rather than a simple wildlife walk.

How difficult is chimpanzee trekking in Nyungwe?

The difficulty depends on:

  • location of the chimpanzees
  • recent weather
  • trail conditions
  • elevation
  • fitness
  • how long the group needs to search

The terrain can be steep and muddy. Travelers should therefore be comfortable walking for several hours over uneven ground.

Good hiking shoes are important. A lightweight waterproof jacket is also useful because rain can develop quickly in rainforest environments.

How close can you get to chimpanzees?

Chimpanzee encounters are managed under park regulations designed to protect both visitors and wildlife. The objective is not to approach the animals aggressively.

Chimpanzees are wild animals, and their behavior can change quickly. A responsible trek prioritizes the welfare of the troop while allowing visitors to observe natural behavior.

The experience is therefore very different from a zoo encounter. You are entering their forest rather than asking them to perform for you.

Nyungwe's other primates

Chimpanzees are the most famous primate in Nyungwe, but they are far from the only ones. African Parks records 13 primate species in the park. Species associated with Nyungwe include:

  • eastern chimpanzees
  • Rwenzori black-and-white colobus
  • L'Hoest's monkeys
  • owl-faced monkeys
  • blue monkeys
  • red-tailed monkeys
  • vervet monkeys
  • olive baboons
  • mangabeys

The exact species encountered depends heavily on location, season and troop movements.

This is why dedicated primate walks can be more rewarding than simply visiting Nyungwe for a single chimpanzee trek.

Rwenzori black-and-white colobus

One of Nyungwe's most remarkable primate experiences is the Rwenzori black-and-white colobus.

The park supports an exceptional population of these monkeys. African Parks reports that a single community of approximately 600 Rwenzori colobus has been observed in Nyungwe, described as the largest primate group of its kind ever recorded on the continent.

Large groups moving through the canopy can create a spectacular wildlife encounter. The contrast between their black-and-white coats and the deep green forest makes them particularly striking for photographers.

L'Hoest's monkeys

L'Hoest's monkey is another important primate associated with Nyungwe. The species is closely associated with the Albertine Rift and forest environments.

Unlike large open-country mammals, these monkeys can disappear quickly into dense vegetation.

A good guide therefore matters because identifying movement and calls can help visitors locate troops before they vanish into the forest.

Golden monkeys in Nyungwe?

Travelers sometimes confuse Rwanda's two main primate destinations. Golden monkey trekking is primarily associated with Volcanoes National Park, not Nyungwe.

Nyungwe's major primate attractions are chimpanzees, colobus and its diverse forest-monkey community.

This distinction matters when travelers compare Rwanda's primate experiences. Chimpanzee trekking in Nyungwe and golden monkey trekking in Volcanoes are separate activities in separate parks, and itineraries should not treat them as interchangeable.

The Nyungwe canopy walkway

The Nyungwe Canopy Walkway is one of Rwanda's most recognizable forest experiences. The suspension bridge is approximately 160 metres long and 70 metres above the forest floor.

It provides a completely different perspective on Nyungwe. Instead of walking beneath the trees, you move through the forest canopy. From the walkway, visitors may see:

  • treetops
  • birds
  • butterflies
  • forest vegetation
  • monkeys moving through the canopy
  • distant forested hills

The experience is reached through a guided forest trail. Visit Rwanda notes that the walkway can be accessed through the Igishigishigi Trail and by hikers using the Imbaraga or Umuyove trails.

Is the canopy walkway difficult?

The bridge itself is not the main physical challenge. The difficulty comes from the hike required to reach it. The forest trails can be steep, and rain can make them muddy.

Visit Rwanda recommends suitable footwear and a lightweight raincoat, and notes that boots and raincoats can be rented at park headquarters.

Travelers who are uncomfortable with heights should also know that the bridge is suspended high above a forested ravine. For those comfortable with heights, however, it is one of the most memorable ways to experience Nyungwe.

Nyungwe forest hiking

Nyungwe contains an extensive network of forest trails. Hiking allows visitors to experience the ecosystem without focusing exclusively on one species. Depending on the trail, visitors may encounter:

  • primates
  • birds
  • butterflies
  • orchids
  • ancient trees
  • waterfalls
  • streams
  • forest viewpoints

The forest is particularly rewarding for travelers who enjoy slow observation. You may spend several minutes watching a small movement in the canopy that would be completely missed during a fast-paced wildlife drive.

Igishigishigi Trail

The Igishigishigi Trail is particularly associated with the canopy walkway. The route passes through montane forest before reaching the elevated bridge.

It is a useful choice for first-time visitors who want to combine a forest hike with the canopy experience.

Imbaraga Trail

The Imbaraga Trail provides a longer forest hiking experience and can also be used to access the canopy walkway.

It is more suitable for travelers who want the hike itself to be part of the experience rather than simply treating the canopy bridge as an isolated attraction.

Umuyove Trail

The Umuyove Trail is another route associated with the canopy walkway. It provides opportunities to experience Nyungwe's vegetation and wildlife before reaching the elevated section.

Trail choice should ultimately depend on available time, fitness and current trail conditions.

Kamiranzovu swamp

Kamiranzovu is one of Nyungwe's most important wetland environments. The swamp sits within the forest and contributes to the ecological diversity of the park.

UNESCO identifies Kamiranzovu as particularly important for the Grauer's swamp-warbler, an Albertine Rift endemic, with the swamp supporting its second-largest estimated population.

This makes the area especially interesting for specialist birders and travelers interested in wetland ecology.

Waterfalls in Nyungwe

Nyungwe contains numerous streams and waterfalls. These become particularly attractive destinations for hikers because they provide a visual change from dense forest.

Waterfalls also illustrate how the forest's high rainfall and steep terrain interact.

Some trails can become slippery after rain, so travelers should not underestimate the physical conditions simply because the distances may appear short on a map.

Birdwatching in Nyungwe

Nyungwe is one of Rwanda's most important birding destinations. African Parks currently lists 345 bird species, including 30 Albertine Rift endemics. UNESCO's World Heritage assessment records 317 species within the inscribed property.

Both figures should be understood in context rather than presented as a contradiction. The different figures reflect different datasets, boundaries and recording approaches.

The important point is that Nyungwe has an exceptionally rich bird community and is particularly important for Albertine Rift endemic birds.

Birds to look for

Depending on location and conditions, birders may search for species including:

  • Great Blue Turaco
  • Rwenzori Turaco
  • Red-collared Mountain Babbler
  • Albertine Rift endemics
  • forest raptors
  • sunbirds
  • flycatchers
  • barbets
  • forest hornbills

Nyungwe is particularly valuable for birders who want species that are difficult to encounter elsewhere in East Africa.

Butterflies and forest invertebrates

Nyungwe's biodiversity extends far beyond mammals and birds. UNESCO records at least 290 butterfly species, including 47 Albertine Rift endemics.

For visitors who slow down and pay attention, butterflies can become one of the most noticeable elements of a forest walk.

This is another reason Nyungwe works well for nature travelers who enjoy interpretation rather than only large-animal sightings.

Plants and botanical diversity

Nyungwe contains more than 1,100 recorded plant species. African Parks reports that at least 265 are endemic to its mountains. The vegetation includes:

  • montane forest
  • bamboo
  • orchids
  • ferns
  • mosses
  • flowering plants
  • high-altitude vegetation

For botanists and specialist nature travelers, this botanical diversity is one of the park's greatest attractions.

The Hill's horseshoe bat

Nyungwe is also important for less familiar wildlife. The Hill's horseshoe bat is a critically endangered species associated with the park.

UNESCO identifies it as a species found nowhere else in the world.

This illustrates an important aspect of Nyungwe's conservation value. The park protects not only charismatic species such as chimpanzees but also highly specialized species that receive much less international attention.

Conservation and management

Nyungwe has been protected for many decades. UNESCO records that the area was formally protected from 1933, initially as a forest reserve, before becoming a national park in 2005.

In 2020, African Parks entered into a 20-year management partnership with the Government of Rwanda for Nyungwe. The partnership focuses on:

  • biodiversity protection
  • ecological monitoring
  • law enforcement
  • community development
  • sustainable tourism
  • financial sustainability

This management structure is important because Nyungwe faces pressures associated with its location in a densely populated country.

Nyungwe and local communities

Conservation around Nyungwe cannot be separated from surrounding communities. African Parks' management approach includes community development initiatives and support for livelihoods around the park.

UNESCO also notes Rwanda's tourism revenue-sharing system, through which 10% of park revenue is allocated to a national fund supporting projects in neighboring communities, while an additional fund addresses wildlife-related damage.

This creates an important connection between tourism and conservation. Visitors are therefore contributing not simply to a forest experience but to a larger system intended to keep the ecosystem valuable to people as well as wildlife.

Best time to visit Nyungwe

Nyungwe can be visited throughout the year. However, conditions vary significantly.

Drier periods

Drier weather can make forest trails easier to walk. This is particularly useful for travelers planning several hiking activities.

Chimpanzees can still be difficult to find because their movements are influenced by food availability and forest conditions rather than simply rainfall.

Wetter periods

Rain is an inherent part of the rainforest experience. The forest becomes especially lush, streams and waterfalls can be more dramatic, and bird activity can be rewarding.

The disadvantage is that trails can become muddy and slippery. Visit Rwanda specifically notes that the canopy walkway is open year-round but that afternoon rain is more common.

For serious hikers, weather should therefore be considered alongside wildlife priorities.

What should you wear for Nyungwe?

Nyungwe requires different clothing from an open savannah safari. Recommended clothing includes:

  • sturdy hiking shoes
  • lightweight long trousers
  • breathable layers
  • lightweight waterproof jacket
  • insect protection
  • hat
  • small backpack
  • drinking water

Long trousers are particularly useful because trails can pass through dense vegetation. Waterproof footwear or gaiters can also be useful after rain.

The priority is comfort and protection rather than safari fashion.

How to get to Nyungwe

Nyungwe is located in southwestern Rwanda and is normally reached by road from Kigali or as part of a larger Rwanda circuit.

The journey is considerably longer than the transfer from Kigali to Akagera. This is one reason Nyungwe works particularly well as part of a multi-day Rwanda itinerary rather than as a rushed day trip.

Travelers can also combine Nyungwe with destinations around Lake Kivu. Visit Rwanda currently promotes multi-day Nyungwe and Lake Kivu experiences, reflecting the practicality of combining the rainforest with Rwanda's western lake region.

Is Nyungwe a traditional safari park?

Not exactly. Nyungwe is a forest safari destination rather than a traditional savannah game-drive park.

You should not arrive expecting large herds of elephants, lions and zebras. Instead, the experience is based on:

  • chimpanzees
  • monkeys
  • colobus
  • birds
  • forest plants
  • hiking
  • canopy experiences
  • waterfalls
  • biodiversity

This distinction should be clear when choosing between Rwanda's destinations.

A traveler searching for a classic Big Five safari should choose Akagera. A traveler interested in rainforest wildlife and primates should choose Nyungwe.

Nyungwe vs Volcanoes National Park

These two destinations are both primate-focused, but they are not interchangeable.

Nyungwe is montane rainforest whose signature animal is the chimpanzee. It holds 13 primate species, and its main activity is chimpanzee trekking. It offers a canopy experience with no equivalent in Volcanoes, extensive hiking, exceptional birding, dense forest and wetlands, and UNESCO World Heritage status. The ideal stay is 2-4 nights.

Volcanoes is montane rainforest and bamboo whose signature animal is the mountain gorilla. Its primates include golden monkeys and gorillas, and its main activity is gorilla trekking. There is no equivalent canopy experience, hiking is volcano hiking, birding is excellent, the landscape is volcanoes and forest, and there is no UNESCO natural designation. The ideal stay is also 2-4 nights.

The strongest Rwanda itinerary can include both. A traveler can experience chimpanzees in Nyungwe and mountain gorillas in Volcanoes, producing two very different primate encounters.

Nyungwe vs Kibale Forest

For travelers considering Uganda and Rwanda together, Nyungwe and Kibale Forest are natural comparison destinations.

Kibale is particularly famous for chimpanzee trekking and its extraordinary primate diversity. Nyungwe combines chimpanzees with a broader mountain-rainforest experience, including colobus, endemic birds, canopy walks and UNESCO recognition.

Neither should automatically be described as better. The better choice depends on:

  • chimpanzee priorities
  • itinerary length
  • Rwanda vs Uganda routing
  • birding interests
  • canopy walkway interest
  • accommodation preference
  • combination with other destinations

Nyungwe National Park FAQs

Is Nyungwe National Park worth visiting?

Yes, particularly for travelers interested in rainforest, primates, chimpanzees, birding and hiking.

Nyungwe offers an experience that is fundamentally different from Akagera. You are not coming primarily for large herds or open savannah predators. You are entering one of Africa's most important Afromontane forests and searching for animals within a complex three-dimensional ecosystem.

For nature enthusiasts, birders and primate travelers, this can make Nyungwe one of the most rewarding destinations in Rwanda.

What is Nyungwe National Park famous for?

Nyungwe is best known for:

  • chimpanzee trekking
  • its rainforest ecosystem
  • 13 recorded primate species
  • the canopy walkway
  • exceptional birding
  • Albertine Rift endemics
  • forest hiking
  • UNESCO World Heritage status

Its importance extends beyond tourism because it is also a major water catchment and biodiversity refuge.

Can you see chimpanzees in Nyungwe?

Yes. Chimpanzee trekking is one of Nyungwe's principal tourism experiences.

However, chimpanzees are wild and move through a large forest environment. Even when trackers know that a troop is nearby, dense vegetation can make the animals difficult to see.

This is why chimpanzee trekking should be approached as a wildlife-searching experience, not a guaranteed photo opportunity.

How long does chimpanzee trekking in Nyungwe take?

The duration can vary depending on where the chimpanzees are located and how far the group needs to walk. Some encounters may be relatively quick, while others require several hours of searching through forest terrain.

Rain, elevation, trail conditions and chimpanzee movements can all influence the experience.

Travelers should therefore avoid scheduling an important flight immediately after trekking unless sufficient time has been allowed for delays.

Is Nyungwe chimpanzee trekking difficult?

It can be. Nyungwe is mountainous rainforest rather than flat savannah. Trails can be steep, muddy and slippery, particularly after rain.

A reasonable level of fitness is useful, and travelers should wear proper hiking shoes.

Those who are less confident hikers should tell their operator in advance so that the itinerary and expectations can be planned appropriately.

What other primates can you see in Nyungwe?

Nyungwe contains 13 primate species, making it one of Africa's important primate forests.

Depending on location and conditions, visitors may encounter colobus, L'Hoest's monkeys, blue monkeys, red-tailed monkeys and other forest primates. The large Rwenzori black-and-white colobus groups are particularly impressive.

This makes Nyungwe valuable even when chimpanzees are difficult to locate.

Can you see gorillas in Nyungwe?

No. Mountain gorilla trekking in Rwanda takes place in Volcanoes National Park, not Nyungwe.

Nyungwe's principal primate experiences are chimpanzees, colobus and other forest monkeys.

Travelers who want both gorillas and chimpanzees can combine Volcanoes and Nyungwe in one Rwanda itinerary.

Can you see golden monkeys in Nyungwe?

Golden monkey trekking is associated with Volcanoes National Park in the Virunga Mountains.

Nyungwe should instead be promoted for chimpanzees, colobus and its wider forest-primate diversity.

This distinction is particularly important when designing Rwanda safari itineraries.

Is the Nyungwe canopy walkway worth it?

Yes. The canopy walkway provides an experience that cannot be replicated from the forest floor.

At approximately 70 metres above the ground, visitors gain a view across the treetops and forested valleys. It is especially worthwhile for travelers who enjoy photography, birds and landscapes.

The hike to reach the walkway is also part of the experience.

Is the Nyungwe canopy walkway safe?

The walkway is a managed tourism attraction and is accessed through guided trails.

However, it is suspended high above the forest, so travelers who are uncomfortable with heights should consider this before booking. Rain can also make the approach trails slippery.

Good footwear and following guide instructions are important.

How high is the Nyungwe canopy walkway?

The walkway is approximately 70 metres above the forest floor and about 160 metres long.

Its elevated position provides views into the forest canopy that are impossible to obtain from normal hiking trails.

Is Nyungwe good for birdwatching?

Yes. Nyungwe is one of Rwanda's strongest specialist birding destinations. African Parks records 345 bird species, including 30 Albertine Rift endemics.

The forest environment makes it particularly valuable for travelers interested in species that are difficult to find in open savannah landscapes.

Serious birders should allow more than a single morning because locating forest specialists can require patience.

Can you see a shoebill in Nyungwe?

Nyungwe is not one of Rwanda's principal shoebill destinations. The park's birding strength is its forest species and Albertine Rift endemics.

Travelers specifically targeting shoebills should consider wetland destinations where the species is more strongly associated with the habitat.

This is another reason not to treat all Rwanda birding destinations as interchangeable.

What is the best time to visit Nyungwe?

Nyungwe can be visited year-round. Drier conditions can make hiking more comfortable and trails easier.

Wet periods can produce lush vegetation, flowing streams and excellent forest atmosphere, but trails can become muddy.

Because Nyungwe is rainforest, travelers should be prepared for rain regardless of the month.

How many days should I spend in Nyungwe?

Two nights can work for travelers whose main goal is chimpanzee trekking.

Three nights is more comfortable because it allows chimpanzee trekking plus the canopy walkway or another forest activity.

Four nights is better for birders, photographers, hikers and travelers who want to explore multiple trails.

Can Nyungwe be combined with Volcanoes National Park?

Yes. It is one of the strongest combinations for a Rwanda primate safari.

Nyungwe provides chimpanzees, rainforest, colobus and the canopy walkway, while Volcanoes provides mountain gorillas, golden monkeys and volcanoes.

The two destinations therefore complement rather than duplicate one another.

Can Nyungwe be combined with Akagera National Park?

Yes. This creates a three-ecosystem Rwanda safari:

  • Akagera: savannah, wetlands and Big Five wildlife
  • Nyungwe: rainforest and chimpanzees
  • Volcanoes: mountain gorillas and volcanic landscapes

For travelers with enough time, this is arguably the most comprehensive way to experience Rwanda's major wildlife ecosystems.

Is Nyungwe good for families?

It can be, but the suitability depends on the children's age and the planned activities. Long forest hikes and chimpanzee trekking can be physically demanding.

The canopy walkway may also be unsuitable for children who are uncomfortable with heights.

Families should therefore select activities according to age, fitness and individual interests rather than assuming every Nyungwe experience is suitable for every child.

Is Nyungwe suitable for older travelers?

Yes, provided the itinerary is adapted to fitness and mobility. Not every experience requires strenuous hiking.

However, chimpanzee trekking and many forest trails can involve steep or muddy terrain. Travelers with mobility limitations should discuss their requirements before booking so the operator can determine which experiences are practical.

Is Nyungwe a Big Five safari destination?

No. Nyungwe is a rainforest and primate destination. It does not compete with Akagera for Big Five safari searches.

Its principal wildlife value comes from chimpanzees, monkeys, forest birds, endemic species and the exceptional diversity of the rainforest ecosystem.

This distinction matters when comparing Rwanda's safari destinations.

What makes Nyungwe important for conservation?

Nyungwe protects one of Africa's most important Afromontane forest ecosystems. It contains threatened species, numerous Albertine Rift endemics and major freshwater catchments.

Its UNESCO World Heritage designation recognizes the global importance of its biodiversity. The park's management partnership with African Parks also focuses on long-term conservation, sustainable tourism and community development.

Is Nyungwe a rainforest?

Yes. Nyungwe is primarily a montane rainforest, although the park also contains bamboo, peat bogs, moorlands, wetlands, grasslands and thickets.

Its elevation ranges from approximately 1,480 to 2,950 metres, creating substantial environmental variation within the protected landscape.

  • small backpack
  • binoculars

Is Nyungwe worth visiting without chimpanzee trekking?

Yes. Chimpanzee trekking is the park's headline experience, but it is not the only reason to visit. Travelers can also experience:

  • the canopy walkway
  • forest hiking
  • birdwatching
  • colobus tracking
  • waterfalls
  • botanical diversity
  • scenic forest landscapes

For birders and hikers, Nyungwe can be rewarding even when chimpanzees are not the primary objective.
