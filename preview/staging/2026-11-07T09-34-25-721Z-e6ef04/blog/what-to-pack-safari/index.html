Clothing that works

Khaki, olive, grey - never white (dust) or navy/black (tsetse). Three to four shirts, convertible trousers, fleece plus packable down (mornings hit 5C June-August), wide-brim hat, buff. Laundry at most lodges returns next day - pack half what you think.

Footwear and gear

Broken-in walking shoes plus sandals. Binoculars per person - non-negotiable, sharing ruins sightings. Camera with 100-400mm zoom, dust bags, power bank. Headlamp for camps and night walks.

Medical and documents

DEET repellent, sunscreen, antihistamines, rehydration salts, prophylaxis per doctor. Passport copies in phone plus paper; insurance details printed - camps ask for them.

Leave home

Camouflage (restricted in some countries), drones (banned in most parks), flash expectations, rigid itineraries. Trekkers add route-specific kit: Kilimanjaro list, gorilla gear.

FAQs

How strict are bush-flight luggage limits?

Strict: 15-20kg in soft duffels, weighed at check-in. Excess rides separately for a fee or stays behind - lodges store hard cases free.

What colors must we avoid?

White shows dust instantly; dark navy and black attract tsetse flies. Khaki, olive, stone and grey work everywhere.

Do lodges do laundry?

Almost all, usually overnight return. Pack for 3-4 days regardless of trip length - except intimates.

Binoculars or just camera?

Both: binoculars for finding, camera for keeping. One pair per person minimum.

Power and charging?

Camps charge via solar/mains with downtime midday; bring a power bank and multi-plug. Confirm inverter access on camping trips.

Kids packing extras?

Junior binoculars, wildlife checklist books, headlamp, extra snacks. Guides love briefing sharp-eyed kids.

How do deposits and payments work?

A deposit of 20 to 30 percent confirms camps, permits and flights; the balance is due 60 days before travel. We accept bank transfer and major cards (card fees disclosed upfront). Full terms arrive in writing before you pay anything.

We accept bank transfer and Visa or Mastercard; card payments add the processor's 2-4 percent, disclosed line by line. Splitting the balance across two cards is possible at proposal stage, and paying early in green season occasionally unlocks camp flexibility worth a few percent - always against a written schedule, never against an informal promise.

Are your guides qualified?

Our driver-guides average a decade in their parks, hold national guiding licenses plus first-aid certification, and are assigned by specialty - Migration positioning, photography, birding or family pacing.

How do we get the best price without hurting the trip?

Travel green season, bring four or more travelers to split the vehicle, choose tented camps over lodges, and drive where flying saves little. Never cut guide quality, Migration-zone positioning or insurance.

The four levers that actually work: green season (published rates 20-40 percent below high season with fewer vehicles at sightings), four or more travelers splitting the vehicle, tented camps over permanent lodges of the same operator, and road transfers under two hours instead of flight sectors. The three that do not: cutting guide quality, cutting Migration-zone positioning, and dropping insurance - the last one being a false economy at US$100-300 for a multi-thousand-dollar trip.
