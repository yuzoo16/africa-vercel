8-day Lemosho itinerary

Day 1 - Londorossi Gate to Mti Mkubwa (2,895m)

Morning: registration, crew and gear check. Afternoon: 3-4 hr rainforest hike with lunch boxes. Evening: camp briefing, dinner, early night.

Day 2 - Mti Mkubwa to Shira 1 (3,500m)

Steep forest-to-moorland climb (5-6 hrs). First Kibo views. Camp on the Shira plateau rim.

Day 3 - Shira 1 to Moir Hut (3,900m)

Gentle acclimatization day across the plateau with optional Cathedral Peak scramble (4-5 hrs).

Day 4 - Moir to Barranco via Lava Tower (4,630m)

Classic walk-high-sleep-low: lunch at Lava Tower, descend to Barranco camp (3,976m). Longest day (6-7 hrs).

Day 5 - Barranco to Karanga (4,035m)

Barranco Wall scramble after breakfast (hands needed, exposure brief), then a short valley afternoon - recovery by design.

Day 6 - Karanga to Barafu (4,673m)

Short morning push to base camp; lunch, gear prep, sleep by 7pm.

Day 7 - Summit: Uhuru Peak (5,895m) to Mweka (3,100m)

Midnight start; Stella Point at sunrise, Uhuru 1-2 hrs later; descend to Mweka for celebration dinner. 12-14 hrs total.

Day 8 - Mweka to Mweka Gate, certificates, transfer

3-4 hr forest descent, summit certificates, drive to Arusha/Moshi hotel.

7-day Lemosho itinerary

As the 8-day but Barranco to Barafu direct (skipping Karanga): Day 5 becomes the long Barranco Wall plus Karanga Valley traverse to Barafu (8-9 hrs). Suits fit trekkers with hill experience. Success near 85% with good crews.

6-day Lemosho itinerary

Skips Moir and Karanga: Shira 1 to Barranco in one long day, Barranco to Barafu direct. Only for pre-acclimatized, very fit climbers - success drops toward 75%. We recommend against it for first high-altitude trekkers.

Difficulty, scenery, crowds, cost

Difficulty: moderate - no technical sections; Barranco Wall is a scramble, summit night is endurance. Scenery: the full transect - forest, plateau, alpine desert, glaciers. Traffic: moderate; busier than Rongai, quieter than Machame. Cost: from $3,400 (8-day, join-in basis); private climbs higher. Best months: January-March and June-October. Try the success estimator.

Lemosho FAQs

Why is Lemosho success highest?

Length and profile: 8 days with walk-high-sleep-low days at Lava Tower give bodies time to adapt. Acclimatization beats fitness for summit odds.

Is 6-day Lemosho a bad idea?

For most: yes. It compresses the two best acclimatization days out. Only pre-acclimatized (e.g. post-Meru) fit climbers should consider it.

How crowded is Lemosho?

Moderate: western start spreads groups; trails converge with Machame at Barranco. Rongai is quieter, Machame busier.

What does it cost?

Quality 8-day climbs from about $3,400 per person join-in; private and luxury tiers higher. Below $2,500 means cut crew wages or days - ask for the breakdown.

Can beginners climb Lemosho?

Yes with 8 days and 8-12 weeks of hill training: fit first-timers summit routinely. Altitude affects everyone - go slow, hydrate, report symptoms.

When should we book?

Full-moon summit weeks and dry-season months fill 4-6 months ahead. Shoulder dates offer availability and value.

What gear is critical?

Broken-in boots, -10C bag, 4-layer system, headlamp with spare batteries, 3L hydration. See the packing list; rentals available in Arusha.

Diamox - yes or no?

A personal medical decision with your doctor: many climbers use prophylactic acetazolamide; it does not replace slow ascent. Discuss contraindications (sulfa allergy) professionally.

Safari after the climb?

Highly recommended: legs recover on game drives. Most climbers add 4-7 safari days - 5-day safari is the classic pairing.
