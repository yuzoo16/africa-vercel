Best time to book

The northern circuit runs on two seasons. The one you pick changes what you see, what the camps cost and how far ahead you need to commit.

Dry season: June to October

Tanzania's cooler, drier high season is the easiest window for wildlife. Water shrinks to the Tarangire River and the remaining pools, vegetation thins and animals gather where they can still drink, so sightings come faster and the crater floor is at its most open. It is also peak price and peak crowd: July and August are the busiest weeks in the parks, and the better camps go first.

Green season: November to May

Short rains begin in November and the landscape greens within weeks, with rates down and the parks nearly empty. January to March is calving season on the southern Serengeti, where predators concentrate around the newborn herds and the photography is at its sharpest. March and April bring the long rains, when lodges cut hardest, visitor numbers fall to their lowest and the light is superb for photography. Outside the Christmas and New Year window, this is where the value sits.

What actually drives the timing

The migration moves all year and it does not follow a fixed map: the southern Ndutu plains for calving from January, the central Seronera through the green months, and the northern Mara from July to October. Camp zone follows the herds, which is why your month matters more than your itinerary length.

How far ahead to book

June to August and the peak migration months want six to twelve months of notice for the camps worth choosing. Green-season departures usually go through on two to four months. Christmas, New Year and Easter compress early whatever the season.

FAQs

What is NOT included that surprises first-timers?

International flights, visas, travel insurance (required), premium drinks, the balloon flight where offered, and tips. Our quotes line-item park fees and permits so the total you compare is the total you pay.

What happens if it rains?

Game drives run rain or shine - vehicles have roofs and ponchos, animals remain active, and green-season light is superb for photography. Only true downpours pause activity, and camps flex schedules around them.

What insurance do we need?

Comprehensive travel insurance is required, including medical evacuation (AMREF/Flying Doctors cover, about 30 dollars, is recommended for remote circuits). Bring policy details - camps and operators ask for them.

Can we customise the route?

Freely - add nights, upgrade camps, swap parks or append beach and trekking. Request a proposal and we re-quote line by line.

Do we need a visa?

Most visitors need one: Tanzania offers visas on arrival for many nationalities, Kenya uses an eTA applied for online before travel, Uganda an e-visa. We confirm the current rule for your passport before you fly.

One decision per country, and they differ. Tanzania sells visas on arrival for most passports - US$50, US$100 for United States citizens - or by eVisa in advance. Kenya has issued only the eTA since 2024, at US$32.50 online at least three days before departure. Uganda runs an e-visa printed before you fly, and the US$100 East African Tourist Visa folds Kenya, Uganda and Rwanda into a single stamp. Six months of passport validity covers all three. Send us passport details at proposal stage and we confirm every rule for your nationality before you commit to flights.

Traveling solo - how do single supplements work?

All itineraries run solo. Expect a 20 to 50 percent supplement where lodges charge per room; vehicles cost the same whether one or six ride. Ask for the honest supplement math per trip.

Is a balloon flight worth adding?

Once, yes: a dawn flight over the Serengeti or Mara with a bush breakfast runs 550 to 600 dollars and minimum ages apply. Book at proposal stage - seats sell out months ahead in season.

How far ahead should we book?

Peak Migration and gorilla-permit months need 6 to 12 months. Green season often works with 2 to 4 months notice.

Is this trip suitable for children?

With a private vehicle and pools at stops, yes for most routes. Gorilla trekking has a strict minimum age of 15.
