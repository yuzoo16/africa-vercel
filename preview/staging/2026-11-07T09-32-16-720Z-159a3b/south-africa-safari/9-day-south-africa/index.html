Getting around South Africa

South Africa splits into two halves that do not connect by road: the Cape, which starts at the international arrival, and the bush, which is a short domestic flight away. The Garden Route is the overland link between them, and it only works as a stop rather than a corridor.

Best time to book

South Africa runs on two seasons. The one you pick changes what you see, what the camps cost and how far ahead you need to commit.

Dry season

May to September is the dry, cool window in the bush: thinner vegetation around Kruger, animals at the waterholes and comfortable temperatures. It is also the busiest period in the private reserves.

Green season

The Cape runs on the opposite calendar. November to March is its warm, dry summer, which is when Table Mountain, the peninsula and the winelands are at their best - so a trip that combines both needs the seasons reconciled.

What actually drives the timing

The private reserves around Sabi Sand are year-round, and leopard sightings do not follow a season. Green months bring lush scenery, newborn animals and excellent birding at lower rates, and the Cape shoulder months of April and October are quiet and mild.

How far ahead to book

June to September and the December to January Cape window want six to nine months of notice, particularly for the smaller Sabi Sand lodges. Shoulder-season departures often go through on two to four months.

FAQs

Traveling solo - how do single supplements work?

All itineraries run solo. Expect a 20 to 50 percent supplement where lodges charge per room; vehicles cost the same whether one or six ride. Ask for the honest supplement math per trip.

Is a balloon flight worth adding?

Once, yes: a dawn flight over the Serengeti or Mara with a bush breakfast runs 550 to 600 dollars and minimum ages apply. Book at proposal stage - seats sell out months ahead in season.

How far ahead should we book?

Peak Migration and gorilla-permit months need 6 to 12 months. Green season often works with 2 to 4 months notice.

Is this trip suitable for children?

With a private vehicle and pools at stops, yes for most routes. Gorilla trekking has a strict minimum age of 15.

How much should we tip?

As a guideline: 15 to 25 dollars per day for your driver-guide (per group, not per person), 10 to 15 dollars per day into camp communal tip boxes, and 20 to 30 dollars for a gorilla porter. Restaurants 10 percent where service is not included.

Can dietary needs be handled in bush camps?

Yes - vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free and most allergies are routinely catered with advance notice. Remote camps need 30 days notice for specialist supplies; tell us at proposal stage.

Camps cook from scratch, so most requests are a kitchen question rather than a supply one. Vegetarian, vegan and gluten-free are routine; halal and kosher products have to be imported in the remotest valleys, which is where the 30-day notice applies - Skeleton Coast, northern Uganda, the deeper Okavango camps. Menus are planned per group before the first grocery run, so tell us at proposal stage rather than on arrival. Guides carry your dietary notes on every drive, and camps refill filtered water wherever possible instead of trucking in bottles.

Can older travelers or limited mobility join?

Often yes: game drives are vehicle-based, many lodges have ground-floor rooms with walk-in showers, and we grade walking and trekking honestly. Tell us constraints and we design around them - except gorilla trekking, which demands real hill fitness.

What is included in the from-price?

Private 4x4 and driver-guide, accommodation, park and concession fees, listed transfers and most meals. International flights, visas, insurance and tips are excluded.

Can we add a beach extension?

Yes - Zanzibar, Diani and Mozambique links are routine. Say the word and the flights slot into your proposal.
