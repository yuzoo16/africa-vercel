14 days on the Uganda circuit: Murchison Falls, Kibale, Queen Elizabeth, Bwindi. Every day below is a real day on the ground, not a transfer with a game drive attached.

Bwindi gets 9 nights

What 14 days buys

14 days is the unhurried format. With this many nights the itinerary can absorb a closed road, a slow morning or a sighting that runs long, and still finish with everything covered.

Getting around Uganda

Uganda is a road trip with long, honest transfer days: Entebbe west to the chimpanzee forests, south through Queen Elizabeth to the gorilla forests of Bwindi, and north to Murchison on the Nile. The roads are good but the distances are real, so the itinerary is built around keeping the driving days short.

Best time to book

Uganda runs on two seasons. The one you pick changes what you see, what the camps cost and how far ahead you need to commit.

Dry season

June to September and December to February are the drier windows, when the forest trails are firmest underfoot and the gorilla trekking is at its most comfortable. It is also the busy period for permits.

Green season

The long rains run roughly March to May and the short rains in October and November, when trails soften and the forest turns intensely green. Gorillas are tracked year-round regardless - the forest is wet at every season.

What actually drives the timing

Gorilla permits are the fixed point: they are limited, issued by UWA, and they drive the dates more than the weather does. Chimpanzees are active every day of the year, so Kibale is season-proof.

How far ahead to book

Gorilla permits want six to twelve months of notice for the dry months, because each trekker needs a permit and the daily quota is small. Green-season permits often go through on two to four months.

FAQs

Is this trip suitable for children?

With a private vehicle and pools at stops, yes for most routes. Gorilla trekking has a strict minimum age of 15.

How much should we tip?

As a guideline: 15 to 25 dollars per day for your driver-guide (per group, not per person), 10 to 15 dollars per day into camp communal tip boxes, and 20 to 30 dollars for a gorilla porter. Restaurants 10 percent where service is not included.

Can dietary needs be handled in bush camps?

Yes - vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free and most allergies are routinely catered with advance notice. Remote camps need 30 days notice for specialist supplies; tell us at proposal stage.

Camps cook from scratch, so most requests are a kitchen question rather than a supply one. Vegetarian, vegan and gluten-free are routine; halal and kosher products have to be imported in the remotest valleys, which is where the 30-day notice applies - Skeleton Coast, northern Uganda, the deeper Okavango camps. Menus are planned per group before the first grocery run, so tell us at proposal stage rather than on arrival. Guides carry your dietary notes on every drive, and camps refill filtered water wherever possible instead of trucking in bottles.

Can older travelers or limited mobility join?

Often yes: game drives are vehicle-based, many lodges have ground-floor rooms with walk-in showers, and we grade walking and trekking honestly. Tell us constraints and we design around them - except gorilla trekking, which demands real hill fitness.

What is included in the from-price?

Private 4x4 and driver-guide, accommodation, park and concession fees, listed transfers and most meals. International flights, visas, insurance and tips are excluded.

On our quotes the from-price covers the private 4x4 and driver-guide, accommodation, park and concession fees, listed transfers and most meals; it excludes international flights, visas, insurance, tips and premium drinks. Balloon flights, night drives and gorilla permits appear as optional line items you switch on.

Can we add a beach extension?

Yes - Zanzibar, Diani and Mozambique links are routine. Say the word and the flights slot into your proposal.

How do deposits and payments work?

A deposit of 20 to 30 percent confirms camps, permits and flights; the balance is due 60 days before travel. We accept bank transfer and major cards (card fees disclosed upfront). Full terms arrive in writing before you pay anything.

We accept bank transfer and Visa or Mastercard; card payments add the processor's 2-4 percent, disclosed line by line. Splitting the balance across two cards is possible at proposal stage, and paying early in green season occasionally unlocks camp flexibility worth a few percent - always against a written schedule, never against an informal promise.

Are your guides qualified?

Our driver-guides average a decade in their parks, hold national guiding licenses plus first-aid certification, and are assigned by specialty - Migration positioning, photography, birding or family pacing.

How do we get the best price without hurting the trip?

Travel green season, bring four or more travelers to split the vehicle, choose tented camps over lodges, and drive where flying saves little. Never cut guide quality, Migration-zone positioning or insurance.

The four levers that actually work: green season (published rates 20-40 percent below high season with fewer vehicles at sightings), four or more travelers splitting the vehicle, tented camps over permanent lodges of the same operator, and road transfers under two hours instead of flight sectors. The three that do not: cutting guide quality, cutting Migration-zone positioning, and dropping insurance - the last one being a false economy at US$100-300 for a multi-thousand-dollar trip.
