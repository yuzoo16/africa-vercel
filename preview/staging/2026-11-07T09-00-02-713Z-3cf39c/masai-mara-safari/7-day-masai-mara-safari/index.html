Best time to book

Kenya runs on two seasons. The one you pick changes what you see, what the camps cost and how far ahead you need to commit.

Dry season

June to October is the classic window: dry, comfortable and productive, and July to October is when the migration reaches the Mara. Wildlife concentrates around the remaining water, vegetation thins, and the parks are at their busiest in August.

Green season

The short rains arrive in November and the long rains in March to May. The landscape greens, rates drop and the crowds thin, and January and February are strong months for birding as the northern migrants arrive.

What actually drives the timing

The Mara has no single best month: migration from July to October, predator viewing year-round, and quieter, greener, cheaper parks from November to May. Lake Nakuru's flamingo numbers shift with the lake level rather than the calendar.

How far ahead to book

The migration months and July to August want six to twelve months of notice for the better camps. Green-season departures usually need two to four months, and the Christmas window compresses early.

How do we get the best price without hurting the trip?

Travel green season, bring four or more travelers to split the vehicle, choose tented camps over lodges, and drive where flying saves little. Never cut guide quality, Migration-zone positioning or insurance.

The four levers that actually work: green season (published rates 20-40 percent below high season with fewer vehicles at sightings), four or more travelers splitting the vehicle, tented camps over permanent lodges of the same operator, and road transfers under two hours instead of flight sectors. The three that do not: cutting guide quality, cutting Migration-zone positioning, and dropping insurance - the last one being a false economy at US$100-300 for a multi-thousand-dollar trip.

What is the best month for this trip?

Dry-season months give the easiest wildlife viewing; green-season months cut cost 20 to 35 percent. Tell us your window and we confirm the wildlife logic.

What vaccinations and malaria prevention do we need?

Yellow fever certificates are required when arriving from endemic countries; malaria prophylaxis is recommended for most safari regions - discuss options with your doctor 6 to 8 weeks out. We brief every trip on water, sun and insect precautions.

CDC guidance for the region is precise. Yellow fever vaccination is recommended for all safari travelers and becomes a certificate requirement when you arrive via an endemic country such as Rwanda or Uganda. Routine boosters, hepatitis A, typhoid and hepatitis B round out the travel clinic list. Malaria transmission covers every game park below roughly 2,500 meters, the parasite is chloroquine resistant, and the preventatives that work are atovaquone-proguanil, doxycycline, mefloquine or tafenoquine, started before arrival and finished after. Book the clinic six to eight weeks out, and pack repellent with 20 percent DEET plus long sleeves for dusk.

What photography gear should we bring?

A 100 to 400mm zoom covers 90 percent of sightings; add a wide second body for landscapes. Beanbags beat tripods in vehicles. We provide charging setups and dust management briefings - tell us your kit and targets.
