Permits: 800 vs 1,500 dollars

Uganda charges 800 dollars per person; Rwanda 1,500. A couple saves 1,400 dollars choosing Uganda - enough for chimpanzee permits, a savanna extension, or a lodge upgrade. Rwanda's premium buys shorter transfers and deeper luxury lodge inventory.

Transfers: 9 hours vs 2.5

Bwindi sits 8-9 hours by road from Entebbe (or a 1-hour scheduled flight); Volcanoes sits 2.5 hours from Kigali. Rwanda suits 3-day add-ons; Uganda rewards 5-plus days.

Combinations

Uganda pairs gorillas with Kibale chimps, Queen Elizabeth lions and Murchison Falls in one visa and one trip. Rwanda pairs with Akagera savanna or Lake Kivu - thinner, pricier, faster.

Our recommendation

First trek, longer trip, or budget under luxury: Uganda. Short on time with premium budget: Rwanda. Either way, permits first, lodges second - availability rules both countries. See trekking guide.

FAQs

Is the gorilla experience different?

No - same species, same one-hour regulations, same 95-percent-plus success. Terrain varies by sector more than by country.

The differences that do show: Uganda's Bwindi families are often bolder at close range (tourism habituation runs longer there), Rwanda's groups sit on open volcanic slopes - better light, easier photos, more altitude walking - and Mgahinga's group ranges across bamboo. Same expectations of behaviour, different backdrop.

How far ahead for permits?

Uganda peak months 6-12 months; Rwanda similar for premium lodges. Shoulder months often work at 3-4 months.

Count backwards from the trek date, not the trip date: permits are dated products, and the sector you are offered decides where you sleep that night. Mid-week departures and shoulder months (April-May, November) routinely open 3-4 months out while July-August weekends go at the release. Flexible dates convert scarcity into choice.

Can we trek in both countries?

Yes on 8-plus-day itineraries via Kigali-Entebbe links, but two permits doubles cost for diminishing novelty. Spend the second permit on chimps instead.

The value case for the second permit is weak unless you want two very different backdrops: Rwanda's open volcanic slopes versus Bwindi's dense forest. Better uses of that money: a habituation day at US$1,500 for four hours instead of one, Kibale chimps, or Akagera. If you do go both, keep the border crossing to one - Entebbe-Kigali flights save a day versus the road.

Which suits honeymoons?

Rwanda for lodge splendor and ease; Uganda for adventure plus savanna. Both deliver the hour that defines the trip.

What fitness is needed?

Moderate hill fitness both sides; Rwanda's trails run slightly gentler on average, Uganda's sectors vary more. Porters mandatory in our planning.

Minimum age?

Fifteen, strictly enforced in both countries. No exceptions, no upgrades.

What does a 3-day trek cost?

Uganda fly-in from about 2,650 dollars with permit; Rwanda from about 4,500 with permit and transfers. Road versions cost less, time more.

Where the money sits: the permit is US$800 of the Uganda total before a single night is booked, so the cheapest 3-day format is road transfer plus mid-range lodge. Rwanda's US$1,500 permit sets a higher floor. Adding a second trek roughly doubles the permit line - many groups spend that second slot on Kibale chimps instead.

Is one trek enough?

For most, yes - the hour saturates. Second treks suit photographers and primate devotees; habituations suit researchers at heart.

What decides a second trek is photography: portraits need side-light and patience the single hour rarely gives, and different families behave differently (bold habituated groups versus shy ones). Booking a second permit in the same sector on consecutive days cuts transfer waste; a habituation add-on changes the day format entirely.

How do we choose a sector?

Uganda: Rushaga/Nkuringo for family numbers, Buhoma for lodges. Rwanda: all sectors similar. We match sector to dates, fitness and lodge tier.
