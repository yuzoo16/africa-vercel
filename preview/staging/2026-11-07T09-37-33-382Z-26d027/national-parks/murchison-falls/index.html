Murchison Falls National Park is Uganda's largest national park and one of its most important safari destinations, covering approximately 3,840 km². It forms part of the wider Murchison Falls Conservation Area together with Bugungu and Karuma Wildlife Reserves. The park was first gazetted as a game reserve in 1926 and became one of Uganda's first national parks in 1952.

The park's defining feature is the Victoria Nile, which crosses the protected area before being forced through a remarkably narrow gorge at Murchison Falls. The river is squeezed through a gorge only around 8 metres wide before dropping approximately 45 metres into the Devil's Cauldron.

But the waterfall is only one part of the experience.

Murchison combines extensive savanna, Borassus palm-dotted plains, woodland, riverine habitats, Lake Albert scenery and one of Uganda's strongest combinations of game drives and Nile-based wildlife viewing.

It is therefore a destination where the river is not simply scenery. The Nile is central to the ecology, geography and safari experience.

What makes Murchison Falls different?

Many African parks are primarily experienced from a vehicle.

Murchison can be experienced from both land and water.

A morning game drive can take you across open savanna looking for elephants, giraffes, buffalo, lions and antelope. Later, a boat cruise can take you along the Victoria Nile toward the base of Murchison Falls, with hippos and crocodiles concentrated along the water and wildlife coming down to drink.

The waterfall itself then provides another completely different experience.

This combination makes Murchison particularly attractive to travelers who want a varied safari without needing several different national parks.

The geography of Murchison Falls

Murchison Falls lies at the northern end of the Albertine Rift Valley, where the Bunyoro escarpment descends toward the Nile and Lake Albert.

The northern portion of the park is characterized by broad savanna landscapes with Borassus palms, acacia and riverine woodland, while the southern part contains more bush and forest habitats.

The wider protected area is considerably larger than the national park itself. Murchison Falls National Park is approximately 3,840 km², while the broader Murchison Falls Conservation Area, including the adjoining Bugungu and Karuma reserves, covers more than 5,000 km².

This larger ecosystem matters because wildlife does not recognize the administrative boundaries drawn on a map.

The Victoria Nile

The Nile is the central ecological feature of Murchison.

The river divides the park into northern and southern sectors and provides a permanent water source within a landscape where seasonal conditions strongly influence wildlife distribution.

During a safari, this creates an interesting contrast. Away from the river, animals move across open grasslands and woodland. Near the river, wildlife becomes concentrated around water, particularly during drier conditions.

This is one reason the river cruise works so well as a complement to a game drive rather than as a standalone activity.

Murchison Falls

Murchison Falls is where the Victoria Nile becomes dramatically compressed before plunging into the gorge.

UWA describes the falls as one of the park's defining attractions, with the Nile forced through the narrow gorge before dropping 45 metres into the Devil's Cauldron.

From the Nile

A boat cruise approaches the bottom of the falls, allowing visitors to see the increasing force of the river as it moves toward the gorge.

From above

A hike or drive to the top provides a completely different perspective. Rather than looking at the falls from the river, you stand near the point where the Nile is compressed into the gorge.

The sound, spray and power of the water make this one of Uganda's strongest landscape experiences.

Wildlife in Murchison Falls National Park

Murchison supports a large variety of savanna and riverine wildlife.

UWA's park material lists approximately 75–76 mammal species, depending on the source/version, including elephants, lions, leopards, giraffes, buffaloes, hippos, hartebeest, waterbuck, Uganda kob, warthogs and numerous smaller mammals.

The park is particularly strong for travelers who want a classic northern Uganda savanna experience.

Giraffes of Murchison Falls

Murchison is one of Uganda's major strongholds for giraffes.

UWA's giraffe information reports more than 1,500 giraffes in the park in its cited estimate and describes Murchison as an important source population for giraffe translocations elsewhere in Uganda.

This makes giraffe one of the species travelers should actively look for during game drives. Their tall silhouettes against the Borassus palms are also one of the most recognizable visual scenes in the park.

Lions

Murchison supports lions, particularly in the northern savanna. The open grasslands and abundance of herbivores create suitable predator habitat.

Lion sightings are never guaranteed, but travelers spending multiple game drives in the northern sector have meaningful opportunities to search for them. Early morning and late afternoon are particularly valuable periods because predators are often more active when temperatures are lower.

Leopards

Leopards occur in Murchison Falls, but they are considerably more elusive than many of the park's open-country species. They can use woodland, thickets and areas around watercourses where vegetation provides cover.

A leopard sighting therefore requires a different kind of game-drive attention from watching giraffes or buffalo in open grassland.

Elephants

Elephants are an important component of the Murchison ecosystem. They can be encountered in woodland, grassland and around water.

The Nile is especially valuable during dry conditions, when wildlife concentrates around remaining reliable water sources. Seeing an elephant herd approaching or crossing the river can be one of the more memorable moments of a Murchison safari.

Hippos and Nile crocodiles

The river creates a second wildlife world alongside the terrestrial safari.

Hippos spend much of their time in the water during daylight hours, while crocodiles often lie along sandbanks and river margins. The boat cruise gives travelers an unusually good perspective on both.

Instead of viewing a hippo from several metres away across grassland, you may be looking directly along the waterline where dozens of animals are concentrated.

The Murchison boat safari

The Nile boat cruise toward the bottom of Murchison Falls is one of the park's signature activities.

The boat follows the river upstream toward the falls. Along the route, passengers can search the banks and shallows for hippos, crocodiles, elephants, buffalo, waterbuck, antelope, waterbirds and other river-dependent wildlife.

The cruise eventually reaches the area beneath the falls. The experience is particularly valuable because wildlife photography from the water produces perspectives that are impossible from a conventional safari vehicle.

Game drives

Game drives are concentrated primarily in the northern sector, where the open savanna provides better visibility.

The Buligi area is one of the classic game-viewing landscapes. The combination of grassland, woodland and water creates habitat for a wide variety of species.

A typical safari should ideally include at least one early-morning game drive and one later-afternoon drive rather than trying to see everything in a single outing.

Budongo Forest and chimpanzees

Murchison's southern landscape connects with the wider Budongo Forest ecosystem.

Chimpanzee tracking and habituation experiences are available in the Budongo area associated with the Murchison Falls Conservation Area. UWA states that chimpanzee tracking and habituation at Murchison take place throughout the year. Its guidance notes that chimpanzee tracking can actually be productive during rainy periods because dry-season conditions may encourage chimpanzees to move deeper into the forest in search of food.

This creates an important opportunity for travelers who want to combine a conventional savanna safari with a forest primate experience.

Birdwatching in Murchison

Murchison is also a major birding destination. UWA's park material lists more than 450 bird species.

The combination of river, wetlands, savanna, woodland, forest and Lake Albert habitats creates multiple ecological niches.

Birding therefore works particularly well as part of a longer safari rather than being treated as a separate activity.

When is the best time to visit Murchison Falls?

Murchison is a year-round safari destination. However, seasonal conditions change the experience.

Dry periods

During drier conditions, vegetation can become less dense and animals may concentrate around permanent water sources. This can make wildlife easier to locate.

The Nile remains a major attraction because it provides reliable water even when other seasonal water sources diminish.

Wet periods

The landscape becomes greener and bird activity can be excellent. The trade-off is that some tracks can become muddy and driving conditions can be slower.

For travelers who prioritize dramatic green scenery and birdlife, wetter periods can therefore be rewarding.

How to get to Murchison Falls

Murchison is approximately 305 km north of Kampala according to the current UWA park page. UWA estimates roughly four to five hours to the southern entrance gate, with additional time required to reach many lodges inside or around the park.

The park can also be reached by air. UWA lists Pakuba Airfield as a convenient arrival point for the main lodge cluster around Paraa, with Chobe and Bugungu providing other options. For a longer Uganda safari, flying can save substantial road-transfer time.

Which side of Murchison should you stay on?

This is an important itinerary decision. The park is divided by the Nile, and accommodation locations can affect the amount of time spent reaching game-viewing areas.

Paraa area

Useful for travelers wanting convenient access to the main game-viewing areas and river activities.

Northern sector

Particularly attractive to travelers prioritizing game drives because the northern plains contain much of the classic savanna wildlife-viewing landscape.

Southern approach

Useful when combining Murchison with Budongo Forest and chimpanzee experiences.

The best lodge is therefore not necessarily the most luxurious one. It is the one that fits the safari route.

How many days do you need?

2 nights

A short visit can include a game drive, the Nile boat cruise and Murchison Falls itself. This works for travelers with limited time.

3 nights

A much stronger option. It allows multiple game drives, the boat cruise, the falls experience and more relaxed pacing.

4 nights

Best for travelers wanting to add chimpanzee tracking, birding, additional game drives and more time around the Nile.

Murchison Falls FAQs

Is Murchison Falls worth visiting?

Yes, particularly if you want a Uganda safari combining wildlife with a major natural landmark.

The park's biggest advantage is the combination of extensive savanna, the Nile, Murchison Falls and boat-based wildlife viewing.

It does not require choosing between a wildlife safari and a scenic destination. You get both in one protected area.

How many days do you need in Murchison Falls?

Two nights can work for a fast itinerary, but three nights is more comfortable.

Three nights gives you enough time to experience game drives, the Nile cruise and the waterfall without turning the park into a rushed stop between road transfers.

Travelers interested in chimpanzees should consider an additional night.

What animals can you see in Murchison Falls?

Common safari targets include elephants, giraffes, buffalo, Uganda kob, hartebeest, waterbuck, hippos and warthogs.

Lions and leopards are also present.

Giraffe are particularly important to the park's wildlife identity, with UWA reporting a large population in Murchison.

Can you see the Big Five in Murchison Falls?

Murchison has elephants, lions, leopards and buffalo.

It does not currently have a resident wild rhino population comparable to dedicated rhino destinations.

Therefore, it is better described as a major Big Game safari destination rather than promising a conventional Big Five checklist.

Is the Murchison Falls boat safari worth it?

Absolutely.

The boat cruise is one of the experiences that makes Murchison different from many conventional savanna parks.

You experience wildlife from the river and finish at the base of one of Uganda's most spectacular waterfalls.

For a first visit, it should normally be considered a core activity.

Can you see Murchison Falls from a boat?

Yes.

The Nile boat cruise approaches the bottom of the falls.

A separate visit to the top gives another perspective and can be combined with the boat experience.

Can you hike to the top of Murchison Falls?

Yes.

The top-of-the-falls experience provides a close view of the narrow gorge where the Nile is compressed before the dramatic drop.

The precise availability and current activity arrangements should be confirmed with UWA when booking.

Is Murchison Falls good for families?

Yes.

The variety is useful for families because the trip does not depend entirely on long game drives.

The boat cruise and waterfall experience break up the driving and provide different ways for children and adults to experience the landscape.

Can Murchison Falls be combined with Bwindi?

Yes, but the journey should be planned carefully.

Murchison is in northern Uganda while Bwindi is in the southwest, so a road itinerary between them involves a substantial transfer.

A domestic flight or a route incorporating another western Uganda destination can make the overall journey more comfortable.

Can you see chimpanzees in Murchison Falls?

Yes, through chimpanzee tracking and habituation experiences associated with the Budongo Forest area.

UWA confirms that these experiences operate year-round.

Is Murchison Falls better than Queen Elizabeth?

They offer different safari experiences.

Murchison is particularly strong for the Nile, Murchison Falls, large savanna landscapes, giraffes, the boat safari and Budongo chimpanzee experiences.

Queen Elizabeth is particularly strong for the Kazinga Channel, hippos, Ishasha tree-climbing lion opportunities, Kyambura chimpanzees and crater landscapes.

For a longer Uganda safari, there is a strong argument for visiting both rather than choosing one.
