Serengeti National Park

14,750 km² of Migration, 4,000 lions and the greatest wildlife show on earth. Location-by-season is everything — here's how to get it right.

3–4 days minimumBig cats · MigrationYear-round

Serengeti National Park is one of Africa's defining wilderness destinations and the centerpiece of the wider Serengeti-Mara ecosystem. Covering approximately 1.5 million hectares of savanna, the park is internationally recognized for its vast plains, extraordinary wildlife populations and the annual movement of enormous herds of wildebeest, zebra and gazelles. UNESCO describes the annual migration as one of the world's most impressive natural spectacles.

But Serengeti is much more than the Great Migration.

Its permanent wildlife populations, predator-prey relationships, kopjes, rivers, woodlands, wetlands and different regional landscapes make it a year-round safari destination.

The migration is one part of the Serengeti story.

The ecosystem itself is the bigger story.

What makes Serengeti National Park special?

Serengeti is famous for three things above all:

  • The Great Migration
  • Exceptional predator-prey interactions
  • Vast open wilderness

UNESCO identifies Serengeti as the location of the world's largest remaining large-animal migration, involving more than one million wildebeest and hundreds of thousands of other ungulates moving through a circular route across Tanzania and Kenya.

The park is also home to major populations of:

  • Lions

  • Elephants

  • Hippos
  • Hyenas
  • Wildebeest

  • Warthogs
  • Numerous smaller mammals

This combination makes Serengeti one of the world's strongest destinations for both general wildlife viewing and specialist wildlife experiences.

Where is Serengeti National Park?

Serengeti lies in northern Tanzania and forms the core protected area of the larger Serengeti-Mara ecosystem.

The park borders the Ngorongoro Conservation Area to the southeast and connects ecologically with other protected and conservation areas extending toward Kenya.

UNESCO identifies the wider ecosystem as including Ngorongoro, Maswa, Grumeti and Ikorongo, Maasai Mara and other surrounding areas.

This connectivity is essential.

The Great Migration does not occur entirely inside Serengeti National Park.

The animals move across a much larger ecosystem.

The Serengeti landscape

The word "Serengeti" is strongly associated with endless grass plains, and those plains are certainly one of its defining landscapes.

But the park contains far more than open grassland.

TANAPA identifies:

  • Open grasslands
  • Riverine forests
  • Swamps
  • Woodlands
  • Kopjes

as important habitats within the park.

Each habitat supports different wildlife.

The plains provide grazing for enormous herds.

Woodlands provide shade, browse and cover.

Rivers provide water and support riverine ecosystems.

Kopjes provide elevated viewpoints and shelter for both predators and prey.

The endless plains

The southern Serengeti is particularly famous for its open short-grass plains. These landscapes are strongly associated with the wildebeest calving season.

The volcanic history of the region contributes to highly productive soils, while grazing animals continually shape the grassland ecosystem.

The result is one of the most productive large-mammal ecosystems on Earth.

UNESCO describes the combination of volcanic soils, rainfall patterns and the ecological influence of migration as producing one of the world's most productive ecosystems.

Kopjes: Serengeti's rocky islands

Kopjes are rocky outcrops rising above the surrounding plains. They are among the most recognizable features of Serengeti scenery.

These rocks provide:

  • Elevated viewpoints
  • Shade
  • Shelter
  • Resting sites
  • Hiding places
  • Denning opportunities

They can also be useful for wildlife viewing.

A lion resting on a kopje while herds move across the plains below is one of the classic Serengeti scenes.

The Great Migration

What actually is the Great Migration?

The Great Migration is not a single event occurring on one day.

It is a continuous annual movement of enormous herbivore herds through the Serengeti-Mara ecosystem in response to changing rainfall, grass quality, breeding cycles and water availability.

The principal participants are:

  • Wildebeest
  • Zebra
  • Thomson's gazelles
  • Other herbivores

They move through a circular route spanning Tanzania and Kenya.

UNESCO describes the annual journey as approximately 1,000 km across the wider ecosystem.

TANAPA currently describes the migration as involving more than 1.5 million wildebeest, zebras and predators.

Why do the animals migrate?

The migration is fundamentally connected to food and water.

The herds follow seasonal patterns of rainfall and grass production.

Fresh grass provides the energy needed to support millions of herbivores.

Predators follow the herds because large concentrations of prey create extraordinary hunting opportunities.

The migration is therefore not simply a spectacular movement of animals.

It is an ecological mechanism linking:

Rain → grass → herbivores → predators → nutrients → ecosystem productivity.

Serengeti migration calendar

The migration does not obey an exact timetable every year.

Rainfall varies.

Therefore, a responsible safari itinerary should use the calendar as a framework rather than a guarantee. Month by month in the Serengeti →

January to March — calving season

The southern Serengeti and Ndutu region are particularly associated with calving.

TANAPA material describes hundreds of thousands of calves being born during the concentrated calving period, with the short-grass plains providing highly nutritious grazing.

This period is particularly attractive for:

  • Newborn wildlife
  • Predator activity
  • Photography
  • Behavioral observation
  • Large concentrations of wildebeest

The abundance of vulnerable young animals also creates intense predator-prey interactions.

April to May — movement toward the western Serengeti

As conditions change, the herds begin moving away from the southern plains.

The migration's exact path depends on rainfall.

The western Serengeti and Grumeti region can become important during this period.

The landscape also becomes greener during the rains.

This can be a rewarding time for travelers who prioritize scenery, photography and lower visitor density.

June to July — western corridor and northern movement

As the herds move north, the western Serengeti becomes increasingly important.

This period is also associated with the beginning of the dramatic river-crossing season, although crossings cannot be guaranteed on a particular day or at a particular location.

The herds may spend days or longer around a crossing area before moving.

July to October — northern Serengeti and Mara River

The northern Serengeti becomes particularly important during the later migration months.

The Mara River crossings are among the most famous wildlife events in Africa.

But they are also unpredictable.

Animals may gather at the river and delay crossing.

They may approach and retreat.

A crossing may happen suddenly.

Or there may be no crossing during a particular viewing period.

TANAPA has specific rules governing Mara River crossing viewing because inappropriate tourist behavior can damage the experience and the ecosystem. Vehicles must remain in designated areas and must not approach crossing animals.

This is important for responsible safari planning.

November to December — return south

The herds gradually begin moving south again as rainfall returns to the southern plains.

The cycle begins again.

This continuous movement is why Serengeti should be understood as a dynamic ecosystem rather than a destination where wildlife follows a fixed annual schedule.

The Great Migration is not the only reason to visit

One of the biggest mistakes travelers make is believing Serengeti is only worthwhile when the migration is nearby.

It is not.

The park supports enormous populations of resident wildlife.

Lions, leopards, cheetahs, elephants, giraffes, buffalo, hyenas and many other species live in the ecosystem throughout the year.

TANAPA describes Serengeti as a year-round destination with abundant wildlife beyond the migratory herds.

A safari outside the migration season can therefore still be exceptional.

Serengeti predators

The park is famous for predator-prey interactions.

Major predators include:

  • Lion
  • Leopard
  • Cheetah
  • Spotted hyena
  • African wild dog

UNESCO describes Serengeti as supporting one of the world's largest and most diverse large predator-prey systems.

The reason is straightforward.

Millions of herbivores create an enormous and continuously changing food base.

Lions in Serengeti

Lions are among Serengeti's most iconic animals.

They can be found in different habitats, from open plains to woodland and rocky kopjes.

The abundance of wildebeest, zebra, buffalo and other prey allows lions to thrive.

Lion sightings are therefore one of the strongest features of a Serengeti safari.

Leopards in Serengeti

Leopards are more elusive.

They are often associated with trees and areas of cover and can be particularly difficult to find in the vastness of the Serengeti.

A leopard sighting requires patience.

The enormous scale of the ecosystem means a guide can spend significant time searching a relatively large area.

Cheetahs in Serengeti

Serengeti's open plains provide excellent habitat for cheetahs.

The visibility of the grasslands allows them to scan for prey and use speed during hunts.

Cheetahs are particularly interesting during the migration because young and inexperienced herbivores can create opportunities for predators.

Hyenas

Spotted hyenas are among the Serengeti's most important predators and scavengers.

They interact with lions and other carnivores while also hunting their own prey.

They are often encountered around concentrations of herbivores and predator activity.

African wild dogs

Wild dogs occur in the wider Serengeti ecosystem but are much less predictable than lions or hyenas.

They should be regarded as a specialist sighting.

A traveler whose priority is wild dogs should consider destinations where sightings are more consistently associated with the safari route.

The Grumeti River

The Grumeti River is an important feature of the western Serengeti.

It supports riverine habitat and becomes associated with the migration as herds move through the western corridor.

The river is also home to crocodiles and other aquatic wildlife.

However, the famous migration river crossings should not be reduced to a predictable annual "show."

They are natural events whose timing depends on animal behavior and environmental conditions.

The Mara River

The Mara River is one of Serengeti's most famous features.

It forms part of the northern migration route and is associated with the dramatic crossings of wildebeest and other herbivores.

But the river is important even when the migration is absent.

It provides a permanent water source within the wider Serengeti ecosystem.

UNESCO notes that surface water can become a limiting factor during dry years and identifies the Mara as the only perennial river flowing through the park.

Serengeti's dry season

June to October

The dry season is one of the most popular times to visit.

Advantages include:

  • Easier wildlife visibility
  • Less dense vegetation
  • Strong predator sightings
  • Concentration around water
  • Comfortable safari conditions
  • Northern migration opportunities during the appropriate months

This is also peak tourism season in many areas.

Serengeti's green season

November to May

The wetter months bring fresh vegetation and dramatic landscapes.

The southern plains become particularly important around the calving period.

The landscape can be extremely photogenic.

Birdlife is also attractive during this period, especially with migratory species present.

Travelers willing to visit outside the classic peak season can sometimes experience a quieter Serengeti while still enjoying excellent wildlife.

Best time to visit Serengeti

There is no single "best" month for every traveler.

Best for northern migration

July–October. Focus on northern Serengeti, while understanding that river crossings are unpredictable.

Is Serengeti safe?

Serengeti is a managed wildlife environment.

Visitors should remain inside vehicles except at authorized locations or during properly guided activities.

Visitors should follow guide instructions, maintain appropriate distances from animals and never feed wildlife.

TANAPA specifically advises travelers to follow park rules, avoid disturbing wildlife and travel with appropriate guidance.

By air

Flying is an excellent option for travelers who want to maximize time in the park.

Serengeti conservation

The Serengeti's conservation significance extends far beyond the national park boundary.

UNESCO emphasizes that Serengeti National Park forms the core of a much larger ecosystem including Ngorongoro, Maasai Mara and other surrounding conservation areas.

The migration depends on this connectivity.

If animals could not move between seasonal habitats, the ecological system would fundamentally change.

This makes habitat fragmentation one of the most important long-term conservation concerns.

The importance of migration corridors

A wildebeest does not understand where Serengeti National Park ends.

The herd follows rainfall and grass.

This means conservation must protect the wider ecosystem rather than only the boundaries of individual protected areas.

UNESCO specifically emphasizes connectivity between Serengeti, Ngorongoro and surrounding landscapes as essential for maintaining migration.

Responsible tourism and Mara River crossings

The Mara River is an excellent example of why responsible tourism matters.

When large numbers of vehicles gather around crossing points, they can interfere with natural animal behavior and degrade the experience.

TANAPA has therefore introduced specific guidelines requiring vehicles to remain in designated locations and prohibiting vehicles from approaching animals during crossings.

A good safari operator should treat the crossing as a natural event to observe—not a performance to force.

Serengeti FAQs

How many days?

Three nights. The park is huge; two locations in three nights beats one base.

Balloon worth it?

Can I visit year-round?

Yes — resident cats never leave; only the herds move. Green season (Nov–May) is superb value.

Is Serengeti National Park worth visiting?

Yes. Serengeti combines enormous wildlife populations, diverse habitats, major predators and the annual Great Migration. UNESCO recognizes it as one of the world's most important natural heritage landscapes.

What is Serengeti famous for?

It is most famous for the Great Migration, vast grassland landscapes, lions and other predators, and its exceptional large-mammal ecosystem.

When is the Great Migration in Serengeti?

The migration is present in different parts of Serengeti throughout the year. Calving is associated with the southern plains around January–March, while northern migration and Mara River activity are generally associated with later months. Exact timing changes with rainfall.

When is the best time to visit Serengeti?

It depends on your objective. June–October is excellent for general dry-season wildlife viewing, January–March for calving, and July–October for the northern migration phase.

Can I see the Big Five in Serengeti?

Serengeti supports lion, leopard, elephant, buffalo and black rhino, although no safari can guarantee every Big Five sighting.

Is Serengeti good outside migration season?

Yes. Resident wildlife and predators make Serengeti a strong year-round safari destination.

How long should I stay in Serengeti?

Three to four days is a strong general starting point. Longer stays are preferable for migration, photography or travelers wanting to explore multiple regions.

Can I see a Mara River crossing?

It is possible during the northern migration period, but crossings are unpredictable and cannot be guaranteed. Visitors must follow TANAPA's designated viewing rules.

Can I visit Serengeti by road?

Yes. Road access is possible from Arusha and other northern Tanzania destinations, although journeys can be long.

Can I fly to Serengeti?

Yes. Flights serve several airstrips, including Seronera, making fly-in safaris practical for travelers with limited time.

Is Serengeti suitable for families?

Yes, provided the safari is paced appropriately for the children's ages and the driving distances are carefully planned.

Is Serengeti good for photography?

It is one of Africa's strongest photographic destinations, particularly for predators, migration, landscapes, kopjes, calving and wildlife behavior.

What vaccinations and malaria prevention do we need?

Yellow fever certificates are required when arriving from endemic countries; malaria prophylaxis is recommended for most safari regions - discuss options with your doctor 6 to 8 weeks out. We brief every trip on water, sun and insect precautions.

CDC guidance for the region is precise. Yellow fever vaccination is recommended for all safari travelers and becomes a certificate requirement when you arrive via an endemic country such as Rwanda or Uganda. Routine boosters, hepatitis A, typhoid and hepatitis B round out the travel clinic list. Malaria transmission covers every game park below roughly 2,500 meters, the parasite is chloroquine resistant, and the preventatives that work are atovaquone-proguanil, doxycycline, mefloquine or tafenoquine, started before arrival and finished after. Book the clinic six to eight weeks out, and pack repellent with 20 percent DEET plus long sleeves for dusk.
