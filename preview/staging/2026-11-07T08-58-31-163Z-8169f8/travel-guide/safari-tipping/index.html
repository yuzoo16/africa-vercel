Safari Tipping Guide: Who, How Much, by Country

Tipping rewards extraordinary service, never subsidizes wages on our trips - crews are paid properly first. Use these baselines, adjust for exceptional guiding.

Guides

Driver-guides: 15 to 25 dollars per day per group (not per person), handed directly at farewell. Tracker teams in Sabi Sand and walking guides: 10 to 15 dollars per day split by the group.

Camps and lodges

Most camps run a communal tip box: 10 to 15 dollars per person per day covers housekeeping, waiters and back-of-house. Hand anything extra to individuals directly.

Treks and extras

Gorilla and Kilimanjaro porters: 20 to 30 dollars each - they carry your pack and steady you on slopes. Restaurants: 10 percent where service is not included. Balloon crews: 10 to 20 dollars per person.

Currency

US dollars (post-2009 notes, unmarked), euros, or local currency - small bills. ATMs in Arusha, Nairobi and Entebbe dispense local cash; lodges rarely break large notes.

FAQs

Is tipping expected or optional?

Genuinely optional and always appreciated - never a levy. Tip for knowledge, positioning and care, not out of obligation.

Should we tip in dollars or local currency?

Either. Dollars and euros spend easily in safari towns; local currency suits village purchases. Bring small denominations either way.

Either works where dollars are widely used (Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Zambia), but small denominations matter more than currency: crisp US$1 and US$5 notes pay camp tips, while South Africa, Namibia and Botswana run on local currency where cards and ATMs are normal. Keep tips in a separate envelope and exchange before departure - rural borders rarely give fair rates.

How do we tip Kilimanjaro crews fairly?

KPAP-standard operators pool tips transparently: roughly 250 to 350 dollars per climber for a 7-day route, distributed by role. Ask your operator for their schedule in writing.

Is this trip private?

Yes - every itinerary here runs with your own vehicle and guide. No Mixed-group compromises unless you ask for a join-in price.

How fit do we need to be?

Game-drive safaris need no fitness. Trekking trips state their grade honestly - ask for the training notes.

What is NOT included that surprises first-timers?

International flights, visas, travel insurance (required), premium drinks, the balloon flight where offered, and tips. Our quotes line-item park fees and permits so the total you compare is the total you pay.

What happens if it rains?

Game drives run rain or shine - vehicles have roofs and ponchos, animals remain active, and green-season light is superb for photography. Only true downpours pause activity, and camps flex schedules around them.

What insurance do we need?

Comprehensive travel insurance is required, including medical evacuation (AMREF/Flying Doctors cover, about 30 dollars, is recommended for remote circuits). Bring policy details - camps and operators ask for them.

Can we customise the route?

Freely - add nights, upgrade camps, swap parks or append beach and trekking. Request a proposal and we re-quote line by line.
