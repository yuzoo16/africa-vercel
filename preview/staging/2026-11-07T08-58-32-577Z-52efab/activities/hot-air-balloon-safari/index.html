A Different Perspective of the Wilderness

A hot-air balloon safari gives you a completely different perspective of the African wilderness.

Instead of following wildlife along a road, you rise above the landscape at sunrise, watching plains, rivers, woodland and herds unfold beneath you. The experience is particularly spectacular in the Serengeti, where balloon flights can reveal the scale of the ecosystem in a way that is difficult to appreciate from a safari vehicle.

A balloon safari is also one of the more expensive optional activities on an African safari, so the important questions are not simply what a balloon safari is, but how much it costs, where you can do it, what the price includes, and whether it is actually worth adding to your itinerary.

For travelers who value landscapes, sunrise photography, a quieter perspective and memorable experiences beyond conventional game drives, the answer can be yes. For travelers on a tight budget or on a very short safari, the same money may be better spent on an additional safari day.

Where Can You Take a Hot-Air Balloon Safari in Africa?

Hot-air ballooning is available in selected wildlife destinations rather than in every African national park.

The most established safari balloon destinations are:

  • Serengeti, Tanzania
  • Tarangire, Tanzania
  • Maasai Mara, Kenya
  • Okavango Delta, Botswana
  • Pilanesberg, South Africa
  • Selected private reserves and safari areas elsewhere in southern Africa

The experience differs considerably between destinations. In the Serengeti and the Maasai Mara the focus is on vast savannah landscapes and wildlife below, while in the Okavango Delta the aerial perspective adds another dimension because of the maze of waterways, islands, floodplains and woodland.

Balloons in the northwestern Okavango Delta launch around sunrise over the flooded landscape. The activity operates from selected camps inside the designated flying area and is seasonal, generally from April through mid-October.

Serengeti Hot-Air Balloon Safari

The Serengeti is arguably the most iconic place to experience a hot-air balloon safari in Africa.

The Serengeti is home to more than 1.5 million wildebeest, zebras and their predators, and sunrise balloon flights are offered among the park's visitor experiences.

A typical Serengeti balloon morning begins before sunrise. You are collected from your camp or lodge and transferred to the launch site. After take-off the balloon drifts with the wind over the plains while the landscape gradually becomes visible in the morning light.

The flight is generally around one hour. After landing, the traditional experience includes a champagne toast and a bush breakfast before you return to your safari vehicle and continue the day's game viewing.

How much does a Serengeti balloon safari cost?

For 2026, a shared Serengeti balloon safari is approximately US$599 per person with major operators. Published rates describe this as covering the balloon flight, early collection, launch transfer, champagne toast and bush breakfast.

A separate balloon operation fee applies in Serengeti under the park tariff, although older tariff figures are still quoted online and operators may incorporate applicable charges into the advertised rate. Always confirm the final price and inclusions at the time of booking.

What is included?

A standard shared Serengeti balloon experience commonly includes:

  • Early-morning collection from your camp or lodge
  • Transfer to the balloon launch area
  • Approximately one-hour balloon flight
  • Experienced commercial balloon pilot
  • Ground crew and recovery vehicles
  • Champagne or sparkling-wine toast after landing
  • Bush breakfast
  • Return transfer to the safari route or agreed meeting point

Park entry and other safari costs should be checked separately, because they may already form part of your main safari package.

Where does the Serengeti balloon fly?

Balloon operations can be positioned in different parts of the Serengeti depending on the season and the operating area. Published operator information lists:

  • Central Serengeti - throughout the year
  • Western Corridor - approximately June to October
  • Northern Serengeti - approximately June to October
  • Ndutu - approximately late December to March

Availability is subject to weather, operating conditions and the location of your accommodation. This matters when planning a migration safari, because the best balloon location is not the same throughout the year.

Tarangire Hot-Air Balloon Safari

Tarangire offers another ballooning option in northern Tanzania, and a different landscape underneath.

Instead of an almost uninterrupted sea of grass, you may see giant baobabs, woodland, seasonal rivers and large elephant concentrations. That mix makes the flight more varied than a purely open-plains morning, and it suits shorter Tanzania safaris where Tarangire is already on the route.

Hot-air balloon safaris are offered among the park's visitor experiences, and the Tarangire page covers what else the park is known for.

Published 2026 operator rates are around US$550 per person for a shared Tarangire balloon safari, although rates can change and should be confirmed when booking. Ballooning is particularly attractive when the park already forms part of a northern Tanzania itinerary.

Maasai Mara Hot-Air Balloon Safari

The Maasai Mara is another major ballooning destination, with the same format and a different horizon.

The experience follows a similar sequence: an early-morning transfer, sunrise flight, aerial wildlife viewing and a bush breakfast after landing. The Kenya safari pages cover how the Mara fits into a wider route.

Published 2026 market prices generally place a shared Maasai Mara balloon safari in the region of US$450-600 per person, depending on the operator, season and exactly what is included. Because fees and inclusions vary, travelers should compare the final price rather than relying only on the headline flight rate.

The experience can be particularly appealing during the Great Migration season, but there is an important distinction: a balloon does not guarantee a view of a river crossing or of a particular animal, because it follows wind conditions rather than driving toward a specific wildlife sighting.

For that reason, the best way to use a balloon safari is as an additional perspective on the Mara rather than as a replacement for conventional game drives. Migration itineraries are set out on the Great Migration page.

Hot-Air Balloon Safari in the Okavango Delta

A balloon safari in Botswana offers something different again.

The Okavango Delta is defined by water, channels, islands, floodplains and woodland. From a balloon, the landscape becomes a large aerial map beneath you, and balloons fly from treetop level to higher elevations with the winding channels and islands visible from above.

Because balloons launch around sunrise, guests normally need to stay in one of the camps located inside the designated flying area rather than attempting to arrive from Maun on the same morning. Balloon operations currently run from April 1 to October 15.

This makes an Okavango balloon safari particularly suitable for a fly-in luxury Botswana safari rather than for a short stopover.

Hot-Air Balloon Safari in South Africa

South Africa offers ballooning in selected safari destinations, including Pilanesberg.

The Pilanesberg experience combines aerial views of the park's volcanic landscape with the possibility of seeing elephants, antelope, buffalo and other wildlife below.

Published 2026 rates for a one-hour Pilanesberg balloon experience are approximately R6,592 per international guest, plus the applicable park fee, with the whole experience taking roughly five hours including transfers and the ground programme.

This can work well for travelers combining a safari with Johannesburg or Sun City, and it suits a first balloon flight before a longer South Africa safari.

Why Are Balloon Safaris So Expensive?

The price is not simply paying for one hour in the air.

A balloon operation is a small aviation business running in a remote area, and it carries costs that a game drive does not:

  • commercially licensed pilots
  • large specialist balloons
  • fuel
  • ground crews
  • launch and recovery vehicles
  • remote-area logistics
  • safety equipment
  • balloon maintenance
  • landing arrangements
  • transfers
  • breakfast logistics
  • aviation and conservation-related operating costs
  • balloon operation fees in applicable parks

The result is an activity with considerably higher operating costs than a conventional game drive, and the price reflects that structure rather than the flight alone.

Is a Hot-Air Balloon Safari Worth the Cost?

For the right traveler, yes. But it should not automatically be added to every safari.

A balloon safari is worth considering if you:

  • want a once-in-a-lifetime safari experience
  • enjoy landscapes as much as individual animal sightings
  • are interested in sunrise photography
  • are celebrating a honeymoon, anniversary or birthday
  • want to see the Serengeti or the Mara from a completely different perspective
  • have enough time for an early-morning activity
  • have already allocated a comfortable budget for the main safari
  • prefer experiences over adding more accommodation luxury

It may not be worth it if you:

  • are traveling on a strict budget
  • have only two or three safari days
  • are primarily interested in close-up wildlife photography
  • would rather spend the money on another full safari day
  • have mobility limitations that make basket boarding difficult
  • would be disappointed if the balloon does not pass directly over a particular animal sighting

The key point is that a balloon safari does not replace a game drive. A game drive allows your guide to follow tracks, stop at sightings, position the vehicle and spend time watching individual animals. A balloon does the opposite: you surrender control of the route to the wind and experience the landscape on a much larger scale. That difference is exactly what makes it special.

What Is a Balloon Safari Morning Like?

A typical morning follows a sequence similar to this.

  • 1. Pre-dawn collection: you leave your camp or lodge while it is still dark.
  • 2. Drive to the launch site: the ground team prepares the balloon while the pilot checks weather and flying conditions.
  • 3. Sunrise take-off: the balloon rises as the sun begins to illuminate the landscape.
  • 4. One-hour flight: the balloon drifts with the wind over the ecosystem, and depending on the destination you may see wildlife, rivers, wetlands, woodland, plains and other safari vehicles far below.
  • 5. Landing: the ground crew follows the balloon and assists with the landing.
  • 6. Bush breakfast: a traditional breakfast is served in the wilderness, commonly accompanied by a champagne or sparkling-wine toast.
  • 7. Return to safari: you reconnect with your safari vehicle and continue your game drive.

In the Serengeti, published operator information indicates that guests can return to their safari guide around mid-morning after the balloon experience.

How Long Does a Balloon Safari Take?

The flight itself is normally around 45-60 minutes, depending on destination and conditions.

The total experience takes considerably longer because of:

  • pre-dawn collection
  • transfer to the launch site
  • balloon preparation
  • flight
  • landing
  • breakfast
  • return transfer

Plan on roughly four to five hours for the complete morning experience, although exact timing depends on the destination and your accommodation.

When Is the Best Time for a Balloon Safari?

Ballooning is generally a sunrise activity, so the question is less about a particular time of day and more about choosing the right season and destination.

Serengeti: for general wildlife and landscape photography, the dry season can provide excellent visibility and wildlife viewing. June to October is the strongest period for migration viewing, and January to February for the calving season. See the Tanzania safari pages for route planning.

Great Migration: a balloon can add a spectacular aerial perspective during migration periods, but migration location changes continuously. If your objective is a Mara River crossing, do not book a balloon on the assumption that a crossing will happen beneath you on a particular morning.

Tarangire: ballooning can be paired with the park's seasonal wildlife concentrations, especially when elephants and other animals gather around available water.

Okavango Delta: the balloon season is tied to the operating area and weather conditions, with operations running from April through October.

Hot-Air Balloon Safari for Photographers

A balloon safari is excellent for landscape and aerial photography, but it is not necessarily the best choice for photographers whose priority is close-up animal portraits.

From the air you can photograph:

  • herd patterns
  • rivers and waterways
  • morning shadows
  • acacia trees
  • savannah textures
  • dust trails
  • migration movement
  • wetland channels
  • sunrise landscapes
  • large-scale wildlife concentrations

The changing altitude can produce compositions that are impossible from a safari vehicle. However, the balloon moves with the wind, so you cannot ask the pilot to stop beside a lion or reposition repeatedly for a specific animal.

For serious wildlife photography, combine ballooning with a private photographic vehicle rather than replacing game drives with the balloon. See the Africa photographic safaris guide and the photographic safaris activity page for how those days are built.

Hot-Air Balloon Safari for Honeymoons

A balloon safari works particularly well as part of a honeymoon.

A typical honeymoon itinerary could combine:

  • private safari vehicle
  • luxury tented camp
  • sunrise balloon flight
  • bush breakfast
  • sundowner
  • private bush dinner
  • plunge pool
  • Zanzibar beach stay

For Tanzania, a balloon flight in the Serengeti can be positioned in the middle of a longer safari before continuing to Ngorongoro, Tarangire or Zanzibar.

The balloon itself is not necessarily the most romantic part of the experience. It is the combination of sunrise, silence, wilderness and a private celebration breakfast that makes it memorable. Honeymoon routes are set out on the honeymoon safaris page.

Hot-Air Balloon Safari with Zanzibar

For travelers visiting Tanzania from Zanzibar, a balloon safari can be incorporated into a mainland safari before returning to the island.

A practical combination might look like:

Zanzibar → Serengeti → Ngorongoro → Zanzibar

or:

Zanzibar → Tarangire → Serengeti → Zanzibar

The balloon should be scheduled on a morning when you are already staying close enough to the launch area. It should not be treated as an activity that can simply be added to a long transfer day, because flights begin before sunrise and depend on weather. Private vs Shared Balloon Safari

Most travelers use a shared balloon.

A private balloon can be arranged in some destinations, but the price is substantially higher. A private experience may make sense for:

  • honeymooners
  • marriage proposals
  • families
  • small groups
  • photographers
  • special celebrations

Published 2026 private balloon rates run into several thousand US dollars per balloon, depending on passenger capacity. For most travelers, the shared balloon provides the best balance between experience and cost.

Who Can Fly on a Safari Balloon?

Age and physical requirements vary by operator, so the rules for one destination may not apply to the next.

Some current Serengeti operators accept children over seven and ask passengers to be reasonably mobile and fit. Requirements can include:

  • minimum age
  • ability to climb into the basket
  • ability to stand during the flight
  • passenger weight information
  • weather-related restrictions
  • medical considerations

Always confirm the requirements with the operator before booking, especially for young children, older travelers or anyone with mobility limitations.

What Happens If the Weather Is Bad?

Hot-air ballooning is weather-dependent, and the pilot makes the final decision on whether conditions are safe for flight.

Possible reasons for cancellation or postponement include:

  • strong winds
  • poor visibility
  • rain
  • storms
  • unsafe launch or landing conditions

This is another reason to schedule the balloon early enough in the itinerary to allow some flexibility. Do not build an entire safari around a balloon flight that has no alternative date.

What Should You Wear?

Dress for the early-morning safari rather than for the warmer conditions you may experience later in the day.

  • warm layers
  • comfortable trousers
  • closed shoes
  • light jacket or fleece
  • hat or cap for later in the morning
  • sunglasses
  • camera
  • binoculars

Temperatures can be surprisingly cool before sunrise, particularly in the Serengeti. Avoid bulky clothing that makes getting into the basket difficult.

What Should You Bring?

Keep your equipment compact, because large luggage normally stays at your accommodation.

  • camera
  • spare battery
  • memory cards
  • binoculars
  • sunglasses
  • light jacket
  • sunscreen
  • small personal bag

For photography, a versatile zoom lens is generally more practical than carrying an entire camera bag into the balloon.

Is a Balloon Safari Safe?

Commercial safari balloon operations use specialized equipment, trained pilots and ground crews, and flights are conducted subject to weather and operational conditions.

Balloon operations in Tanzania are conducted under civil aviation rules, and pilots hold commercial balloon pilot licences.

Travelers should nevertheless follow all operator instructions and disclose relevant mobility or medical concerns before flying. The most important safety decision is whether the pilot considers conditions suitable for flight.

How Far in Advance Should You Book?

Balloon flights have limited daily capacity, so they should be booked before the safari.

Book well ahead especially during:

  • the July to October migration season
  • Christmas and New Year
  • peak honeymoon periods
  • popular luxury camp stays
  • high-demand northern Serengeti dates

Some operators recommend booking many months ahead because seats sell out during busy periods. Waiting until you arrive at camp can work when space remains, but it should not be the strategy if the balloon experience is important to your trip.

How to Decide if You Should Add a Balloon Safari

Three questions settle it.

  • 1. Is the main safari already long enough? If you have only two nights in the Serengeti, an additional game-drive day may provide more wildlife value than a balloon.
  • 2. Is your budget comfortable? At around US$599 per person in the Serengeti in 2026, ballooning is a significant optional expense.
  • 3. Do you value the experience itself? If sunrise landscapes, aerial photography, unique experiences and memorable moments matter to you, the cost may be easier to justify.

If your priority is simply seeing as many animals as possible, put more of your budget into additional safari days, better positioning or a private vehicle.

How to Add a Balloon Safari to an African Safari

A balloon safari works best when it is treated as part of the itinerary rather than as an isolated excursion.

The planning should consider:

  • which park you are staying in
  • exact camp location
  • balloon launch area
  • season
  • wildlife movements
  • number of nights
  • morning game-drive plans
  • internal flights
  • road transfers
  • weather flexibility
  • budget
  • age and mobility requirements

Hot-Air Balloon Safari FAQs

How much does a hot-air balloon safari cost?

Prices vary by destination. In 2026, a shared Serengeti balloon safari is around US$599 per person, while Tarangire is around US$550. Maasai Mara prices commonly fall around US$450-600 per person depending on operator and inclusions.

Balloon safaris price per seat, not per camp: a shared basket over the Serengeti runs around US$599 in 2026 with champagne landing included, Tarangire near US$550, and Mara baskets US$450-600 depending on operator. Private basket hire exists but multiplies the seat price several times over.

How long is a balloon safari?

The flight itself is normally about 45-60 minutes, but the complete morning experience can take around four to five hours including collection, transfer, preparation, breakfast and the return.

Is a Serengeti balloon safari worth it?

For travelers who value aerial landscapes, sunrise and a unique safari experience, it can be one of the most memorable additions to a Serengeti itinerary. It is expensive, however, and should not replace essential game-drive time on a short safari.

Can you see the Great Migration from a balloon?

Yes, you may see migration herds from the air when they are in the area of your flight, but their location changes continuously. A balloon cannot guarantee a particular herd or river crossing.

Can children go on a balloon safari?

Age restrictions vary by operator. Some Serengeti operators accept children over seven, while other operators or destinations may have different rules.

Is the balloon flight guaranteed?

No. Flights depend on weather and operational conditions, and the pilot makes the final decision on whether conditions are suitable.

Is the balloon private?

Usually not. Most standard balloon safaris use shared baskets. Private balloons are available in selected destinations but cost substantially more.

What happens after landing?

Most classic safari balloon experiences finish with a bush breakfast, often accompanied by a champagne or sparkling-wine toast, before guests return to their safari vehicle.

Can I do a balloon safari from Arusha?

The balloon itself operates near the safari destination rather than from Arusha city. You normally need to be staying near the launch area the night before, because flights take place around sunrise.

Can I combine a balloon safari with Zanzibar?

Yes. A balloon safari can be incorporated into a Tanzania safari before flying to or from Zanzibar. It is best to schedule the balloon while you are already staying in the relevant safari area.

Plan Your Hot-Air Balloon Safari

For Tanzania, the Serengeti is the strongest choice for travelers who want the classic balloon morning: sunrise over vast plains, wildlife below, a bush breakfast after landing, and the chance to continue the day's game drive afterwards. For the Okavango Delta, ballooning offers a completely different perspective of waterways and islands, while in Kenya the Maasai Mara pairs the same sunrise experience with one of East Africa's most famous wildlife landscapes.

A balloon safari should be chosen according to where you are staying, the season, your safari priorities and your budget. The important thing is not simply to add a balloon because it sounds impressive: place it in the right part of the itinerary, allow enough time for the experience, and make sure the rest of the safari already gives you the wildlife time you want.
