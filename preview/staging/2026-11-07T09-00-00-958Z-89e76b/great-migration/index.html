What is the Great Migration?

The Great Migration is the annual movement of huge herds of wildebeest, zebra and gazelles through the connected Serengeti-Mara ecosystem of Tanzania and Kenya.

It is not one event, on one date, in one place. The migration is a continuous annual cycle of roughly 1,000 kilometres, involving more than a million wildebeest and hundreds of thousands of other grazing animals. The ecosystem runs beyond the boundaries of Serengeti National Park, into the surrounding protected areas and across the Kenya-Tanzania border, which is why the southern plains of Tanzania and the Maasai Mara in Kenya are two ends of the same system rather than two separate wildlife destinations.

The herds move because of a short list of needs:

  • Rainfall
  • Fresh grass
  • Water availability
  • Nutritional requirements
  • Reproductive cycles

They are followed the whole way by predators:

  • Lions
  • Leopards
  • Cheetahs
  • Spotted hyenas
  • Crocodiles

The result is not one spectacle but an entire year of changing wildlife concentrations. That distinction matters when planning a safari: calving, the northward push and the river crossings each require a different region and a different month. The year-round cycle → Where to go → Migration FAQs →

The Great Migration is a year-round cycle

The animals move throughout the year. The only real question is where they are in a given month.

One of the biggest misconceptions about a migration safari is that the migration is only the Mara River crossings. Crossings are one part of a cycle that never stops running, so a trip built only around a crossing date is a trip built on the smallest part of the story.

A simplified annual cycle looks like this:

  • January-March → Calving
  • April-May → Movement north and west
  • June-July → Western Serengeti and the northern movement
  • July-October → Northern Serengeti and Maasai Mara
  • November-December → Return toward the southern Serengeti

Exact timing changes from year to year, because rainfall decides where fresh grazing becomes available. January and February are the core calving months, and June to October is the dry-season window when the herds are in the north of the ecosystem.

January to March: calving season

For travelers interested in newborn wildebeest and predator activity, the southern Serengeti and the adjoining Ngorongoro plains are the areas that matter.

The herds gather on the short-grass plains whenever conditions give them enough to eat, and this period is known for:

  • Wildebeest births
  • Newborn calves
  • Zebra in large numbers
  • Large grazing herds
  • Predator activity
  • Green landscapes
  • Photography opportunities

Calving season is not simply about seeing babies. Concentrating vulnerable young animals creates intense predator-prey interactions involving lions, cheetahs, hyenas and other predators, which is why the same small area can produce a different sighting on every drive.

Best areas for calving

Consider:

  • Southern Serengeti
  • Ndutu area
  • Ngorongoro Conservation Area plains
  • Central Serengeti

The precise location of the herds changes with rainfall and grazing conditions, so the camps that work in a strong season can be in the wrong place in a weak one. Calving itinerary ideas →

April to May: the herds begin moving north

As the short-grass areas become less productive, the migration begins moving north and west.

This period can be overlooked, because it does not have the same attention as calving or river crossings. It can still provide a different safari, and often a quieter one:

  • Large herds
  • Zebra
  • Predators
  • Green landscapes
  • Fewer visitors in some areas
  • Excellent general wildlife viewing

The migration does not suddenly leave one region and appear in another. It is a gradual movement across the ecosystem, and the route between two zones is often where the animals are on a given morning.

June: Western Serengeti and the Grumeti

Around June, migration activity can shift toward the western Serengeti.

The Western Corridor becomes particularly interesting as the herds move toward the areas associated with the Grumeti River before continuing north. A June itinerary therefore often combines migration viewing with the wider wildlife of the Serengeti, and may move through the central Serengeti, then the western Serengeti and then the northern Serengeti rather than sitting in one area for the whole trip.

July to October: Northern Serengeti and the Mara River

This is the period most commonly associated with the famous Mara River crossings.

As the herds move north, they reach the northern Serengeti and can eventually cross between Tanzania and Kenya. The spectacle can involve:

  • Thousands of wildebeest
  • Zebra
  • Crocodiles
  • Lions and other predators
  • Steep riverbanks
  • Large concentrations of safari vehicles

There is no guaranteed Mara River crossing date. The animals may gather at a riverbank for hours or days without crossing, may cross at one location and later cross again elsewhere, and may also turn back.

Serengeti National Park has specific rules for crossing viewing, including designated vehicle positions and a ban on approaching animals during a crossing. This is why a migration safari should be planned around time spent in the migration area, rather than around a promise of a particular crossing on a particular day. How crossings actually work →

November to December: return south

The herds begin moving south again as seasonal rains return to the southern Serengeti.

This creates another opportunity to experience the migration without focusing entirely on the Mara River:

  • Moving herds
  • Zebra
  • Predator sightings
  • Green landscapes
  • Fewer vehicles in some areas
  • A different perspective on the migration cycle

By December the cycle is progressing toward the southern plains again, which is where the next calving season will begin. The month-by-month table →

Where should you go for the Great Migration?

There is no single best migration destination. The right location depends on which part of the migration you want to experience.

Southern Serengeti

The southern section is the one to use for calving and for the short-grass plains:

  • Calving and newborn wildebeest
  • Predator activity
  • Large grazing herds
  • Short-grass plains

Central Serengeti

Central Serengeti works in almost any month:

  • General year-round wildlife
  • Predator viewing
  • Migration movement
  • Kopjes
  • A balanced safari itinerary

Western Serengeti

The western section matters when the herds are on that side of the ecosystem:

  • June migration movement
  • Grumeti region
  • Large herds
  • Less predictable but potentially rewarding migration viewing

Northern Serengeti

The northern section is where the migration season peaks:

  • Northern movement
  • Mara River
  • River-crossing season
  • The Kenya-Tanzania migration ecosystem

Maasai Mara

The Maasai Mara forms the northern part of the wider Serengeti-Mara ecosystem.

It is the section that matters when the migrating herds are in Kenya, although the Mara is also a strong wildlife destination outside the migration period. The Mara is contiguous with the Serengeti and part of the same ecosystem that supports the annual movement of wildebeest and zebra, so a border crossing is a change of park rules rather than a change of wildlife. Tanzania vs Kenya for the Great Migration

The migration belongs to the Serengeti-Mara ecosystem, not to one country. The herds naturally cross the Tanzania-Kenya border.

Tanzania gives you access to several different phases of the migration inside one country:

  • Southern Serengeti
  • Central Serengeti
  • Western Serengeti
  • Northern Serengeti
  • Ngorongoro Conservation Area

Kenya is associated with the northern section of the migration and with the Mara River crossings, and the main migration destination there is the Maasai Mara. Tanzania migration safaris →

Which side should you choose?

The answer depends on:

  • Travel month
  • Exact migration objective
  • Number of safari days
  • Budget
  • Accommodation preferences
  • Desired level of flexibility
  • Whether you want to combine Tanzania and Kenya

For a trip focused on calving, southern Tanzania is more relevant. For the northern migration and the Mara River, northern Tanzania and Kenya become more relevant. Safari by month →

When is the best time for a Great Migration safari?

There is no single best month for everyone. Choose the month based on the experience you want.

  • For calving - January to March, focused on the southern Serengeti and Ndutu.
  • For general migration movement - April to June, with the central and western Serengeti.
  • For Mara River crossings - July to October, focused on the northern Serengeti and Maasai Mara.
  • For fewer crowds - consider the shoulder periods rather than concentrating only on the peak river-crossing months.
  • For a full migration journey - a longer itinerary can follow several sections of the ecosystem instead of attempting to predict one specific wildlife event.

Whichever month you choose, the itinerary should place you in the right region for that phase and leave enough nights to absorb a change in the weather. Improving your odds →

How long should a Great Migration safari be?

Length decides how much of the ecosystem you can cover and how tolerant the plan is of animals moving somewhere else.

3-4 Days

Possible for travelers with limited time, but the shorter the trip, the more dependent you are on the location of the herds when you arrive. It works best as a focused single-region safari.

5-6 Days

A practical duration for concentrating on one migration area while allowing several game drives in it, which is usually enough time for the plan to survive one change of weather.

7-8 Days

Provides greater flexibility and can combine different Serengeti regions, so the route can follow the phase rather than committing to one zone from the start.

9-12 Days

Suitable for travelers wanting a more comprehensive migration experience, potentially combining:

  • Southern Serengeti
  • Central Serengeti
  • Northern Serengeti
  • Ngorongoro
  • Zanzibar

12-14 Days

Useful for combining Tanzania and Kenya, particularly when the itinerary includes several ecosystems rather than concentrating exclusively on the migration. Itinerary ideas →

What makes a migration safari different?

A normal Serengeti safari can focus on the park as a whole. A migration safari adds an objective: following the seasonal movement of the herds.

That distinction matters, because it means the route is decided by where the animals are likely to be rather than by which lodges are available. Before the itinerary exists, these questions have to be answered:

  • Timing - when are you traveling?
  • Location - where are the herds likely to be?
  • Duration - how many days can you spend following them?
  • Flexibility - can you change the route if conditions change?
  • Accommodation - is the camp positioned where you need it?
  • Transport - should you drive or fly?
  • Wildlife objective - calving, large herds, predator interactions, river crossings or photography?

The answer to those questions determines the itinerary. Back to the guide →

Mara River crossings: what travelers need to know

The Mara River crossing is one of the most famous scenes associated with the Great Migration, and also one of the easiest experiences to misunderstand.

A river crossing is not guaranteed simply because you visit the northern Serengeti in August or September. The animals can:

  • Gather at the river
  • Move along the bank
  • Cross quickly
  • Stop and retreat
  • Choose another crossing point
  • Remain away from the river

Even when a crossing occurs, it may happen suddenly, and the vehicles nearest the bank will not always be the ones that saw it first.

Serengeti National Park requires vehicles to remain in designated viewing areas and prohibits approaching animals during crossings. The rules are intended to reduce pressure on the animals and protect the ecosystem, so a responsible migration safari puts wildlife welfare and park regulations ahead of getting closer to the action. Why the migration is unpredictable

Rainfall drives the availability of fresh grazing, which means the migration does not operate like a train timetable.

A safari company can use historical patterns and current information to plan the route, but nobody can honestly guarantee:

  • A river crossing on a specific day
  • A particular herd location
  • A predator kill
  • A specific animal sighting
  • A precise migration date

What can be planned is time in the right ecosystem during the appropriate seasonal window. That is the realistic way to approach a migration safari, and it is also the reason the itinerary should be built around nights in the region rather than around a single promised sighting. How to increase your chances of seeing the migration

There are several practical ways to improve your opportunity.

Spend more nights in the migration area - three nights in the right area gives you more opportunities than arriving for one night and leaving immediately.

Choose accommodation carefully - a camp's location can make a major difference, and two properties twenty minutes apart can have completely different seasons.

Avoid overloading the itinerary - constantly moving between destinations reduces the amount of time available for wildlife viewing.

Travel at the right time - match the destination to the migration phase rather than to a fixed set of attractions.

Use experienced guides - a knowledgeable guide can interpret tracks, animal behavior, recent sightings and local conditions.

Consider a private vehicle - more flexibility can be particularly useful for photographers and travelers with a specific migration objective.

None of these guarantees a sighting. Together they change the odds in your favor. How long to stay →

A Serengeti safari can be excellent at any time of year, because the park has resident wildlife and predators.

A migration safari adds a specific objective: following the seasonal movement of the herds. If you travel outside the major migration periods, you can still experience:

  • Lions

  • Elephants
  • Buffalo
  • Giraffes
  • Hippos
  • Hyenas

  • Gazelles

Responsible Great Migration safaris

The scale of the migration means responsible tourism matters.

A responsible safari should:

  • Follow park regulations
  • Keep appropriate distances from wildlife
  • Stay on permitted roads
  • Avoid chasing animals
  • Respect designated river-crossing viewing areas
  • Avoid excessive vehicle congestion
  • Follow guide instructions
  • Minimize waste
  • Support conservation
  • Respect local communities

At Mara River crossings, designated viewing sites and vehicle limits exist to reduce harmful tourism practices. The goal is not simply to see the migration. It is to experience it without compromising the ecosystem that makes it possible. Crossing rules →

Great Migration safari FAQs

When is the Great Migration?

The migration takes place throughout the year. Different parts of the Serengeti-Mara ecosystem become important at different times as the herds respond to rainfall, fresh grazing and water.

What month is best for the Great Migration?

There is no single best month. January to March is associated with calving in the southern Serengeti, while July to October is commonly associated with the northern migration and potential Mara River crossings.

When is the Great Migration river crossing?

River crossings generally occur during the northern migration period, particularly around July to October, but the exact timing is unpredictable.

Can you guarantee a river crossing?

No. A responsible safari operator should not guarantee a specific crossing on a particular day.

Where is the Great Migration?

The migration moves through the Serengeti-Mara ecosystem of Tanzania and Kenya.

Is the Great Migration only in Tanzania?

No. The ecosystem crosses the Tanzania-Kenya border, with the Serengeti forming the southern portion and the Maasai Mara the northern portion.

Is January a good time for the Great Migration?

January can be excellent for travelers interested in the calving season in the southern Serengeti.

Is February good for the Great Migration?

February is strongly associated with calving in the southern Serengeti and can offer opportunities to see newborn wildebeest and increased predator activity.

Is June good for the Great Migration?

June can be a good period for following the herds as they move through the western and central Serengeti, although the precise location depends on conditions.

Is August good for the Great Migration?

August falls within the northern migration period and can provide opportunities for Mara River crossings, but crossings are never guaranteed.

Is September good for the Great Migration?

September can still be a strong period for northern Serengeti and Maasai Mara migration viewing, with the exact location of the herds varying from year to year.

Can I combine the Great Migration with Zanzibar?

Yes. A Tanzania migration safari can be followed by a Zanzibar beach stay.

How many days do I need for a Great Migration safari?

Five to seven days gives more flexibility than a very short safari, while eight to twelve days allows a broader migration itinerary and a possible Zanzibar or Ngorongoro extension.

Is the Great Migration suitable for children?

Yes, with appropriate planning. A private vehicle, suitable accommodation and a realistic daily driving schedule can make a migration safari more manageable for families.

Is a private safari better for the Great Migration?

The two formats serve different travelers. A private vehicle provides more control over timing and route, while a group safari can reduce the cost per traveler and provide a social experience.
