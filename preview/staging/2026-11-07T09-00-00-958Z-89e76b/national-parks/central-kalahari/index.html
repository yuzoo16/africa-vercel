Central Kalahari Game Reserve

The Central Kalahari Game Reserve (CKGR) is one of Botswana's most remote and distinctive safari destinations, covering approximately 52,800 km² according to the Botswana Tourism Organisation. Rather than rivers, permanent wetlands and dense concentrations of wildlife, the reserve is defined by immense open spaces, Kalahari grasslands, fossil river valleys, pans, scrub savanna and semi-arid woodland. Its wildlife has evolved around an environment where surface water can be scarce, making the Central Kalahari particularly rewarding for travelers interested in desert-adapted species, predators, wilderness and landscapes far removed from conventional safari routes.

The reserve is especially associated with black-maned Kalahari lions, cheetahs, brown hyenas, gemsbok, springbok, wildebeest, hartebeest, eland and giraffe. After good summer rains, its northern grasslands can become dramatically productive as fresh grasses attract large concentrations of herbivores and, consequently, predators.

A safari here is not simply a drier version of an Okavango or Chobe safari. The appeal lies in the scale of the landscape, the adaptations of its wildlife and the feeling of being in a genuinely remote Kalahari wilderness.

Best for: wilderness seekers, photographers, experienced safari travelers, predator enthusiasts, self-drive adventurers and travelers who want a very different side of Botswana.

Why visit the Central Kalahari Game Reserve?

The Central Kalahari offers a type of safari that is difficult to reproduce elsewhere in Botswana.

There are no great permanent rivers running through the reserve and no equivalent of the Okavango's extensive floodplains. Instead, wildlife survives across an enormous semi-arid landscape where animals respond to rainfall, grazing conditions, pans and seasonal water availability.

This creates several distinctive reasons to visit.

Vast Kalahari wilderness

The first impression is scale.

Golden grasslands, low scrub, sandveld, fossil river valleys and distant horizons can continue for enormous distances with remarkably little development. Botswana Tourism describes the reserve as the largest and most remotely situated reserve in Southern Africa and the second-largest wildlife reserve in the world.

The sense of space is itself part of the safari experience.

Instead of moving between a series of obvious water sources, you can spend long periods crossing apparently empty country before suddenly encountering a pride of lions, a herd of gemsbok or a group of springbok.

That contrast between apparent emptiness and sudden wildlife encounters is one of CKGR's defining characteristics.

Black-maned Kalahari lions

The Central Kalahari is particularly famous for its lions, including males with exceptionally dark manes.

UNESCO's tentative-list documentation specifically identifies the black-maned lion as one of the reserve's notable natural features.

These lions are not a separate species or subspecies. They are African lions whose appearance is strongly associated with the Kalahari environment and populations.

For photographers and predator enthusiasts, encountering a dark-maned male against the open Kalahari landscape can be one of the reserve's most memorable experiences.

Wildlife adapted to dry conditions

CKGR is an excellent destination for understanding how wildlife survives in semi-arid environments.

Gemsbok, for example, are exceptionally well adapted to dry conditions. Springbok, wildebeest, hartebeest and other herbivores move through the landscape according to food and rainfall rather than simply remaining around permanent water.

The reserve's wildlife community includes major predators such as lion, cheetah, leopard, African wild dog, brown hyena, spotted hyena and jackal.

Research by Cheetah Conservation Botswana has documented all six large carnivores - lion, leopard, cheetah, African wild dog, spotted hyena and brown hyena - in wildlife-management areas bordering the CKGR.

A different Botswana safari

Travelers who have already visited the Okavango Delta, Moremi or Chobe often find CKGR particularly interesting because the ecological experience is so different.

  • Okavango: water, floodplains, mokoro and aquatic wildlife
  • Chobe: riverfront wildlife and exceptional elephant concentrations
  • Moremi: wetlands, woodland, floodplains and diverse habitats
  • Central Kalahari: open grasslands, dry valleys, pans, desert-adapted wildlife and enormous wilderness

This makes the Central Kalahari an excellent addition rather than simply another safari stop.

The landscape of the Central Kalahari

The Central Kalahari sits within the enormous Kalahari Basin.

Its landscape is considerably more varied than the phrase Kalahari Desert might suggest.

UNESCO describes the CKGR landscape as a combination of large clay pans, fossil river valleys, vegetated sand dunes, semi-arid shrub savanna, scrub, Terminalia sandveld, acacia woodland, grasslands and seasonal shallow depressions.

The fossil valleys are particularly important because they reveal the history of an ancient drainage system. Deception and Okwa valleys contain evidence of former rivers that once drained toward the ancient Lake Makgadikgadi system.

This geological history helps explain why apparently dry valleys can become productive wildlife areas after rainfall.

Deception Valley

Deception Valley is one of the best-known areas of the Central Kalahari.

Located in the northern part of the reserve, it contains broad open grasslands associated with an ancient fossil river system.

Following good rains, fresh grasses develop across the valley and attract herbivores including springbok, gemsbok, wildebeest and other grazing species. Predators follow the concentrations of prey. Botswana Tourism specifically highlights Deception Valley for its post-rain concentrations of herbivores.

Its proximity to Matswere Gate also makes Deception Valley one of the more accessible and frequently visited parts of the reserve.

For travelers visiting CKGR for the first time, this northern sector is often one of the most practical places to focus a safari.

Sunday Pan, Leopard Pan and Passarge Valley

Deception Valley is not the only area worth exploring.

Botswana Tourism identifies Sunday Pan, Leopard Pans, Passarge Valley and Piper's Pan among the other worthwhile areas to drive within the reserve.

These areas become particularly interesting when planning a longer safari because wildlife distribution in the Kalahari is strongly influenced by rainfall, grazing conditions and available water.

Rather than treating the reserve as a single wildlife-viewing zone, a longer trip can explore several different landscapes.

Piper's Pan

Piper's Pan lies farther south within the reserve and forms part of the broader network of pans and valleys that give CKGR its distinctive character.

For travelers staying several nights, reaching more remote areas such as Piper's Pan can add a stronger sense of expedition and isolation.

The experience here is less about ticking off a checklist of animals and more about reading tracks, understanding animal movement and experiencing the Kalahari environment.

Wildlife in Central Kalahari Game Reserve

The wildlife of CKGR is shaped by its environment.

This is not the destination to choose if your only objective is to see the highest possible concentration of Big Five animals.

Instead, it is particularly valuable for travelers interested in Kalahari specialists, predators and species adapted to seasonal water scarcity.

Lions

Lion is one of the reserve's signature species.

The famous dark-maned males provide one of the strongest visual associations with the Central Kalahari.

Lion sightings can be excellent when prey concentrations increase following rainfall, but this remains a huge wilderness rather than a small enclosed viewing area. Sightings should therefore be treated as wild encounters rather than guaranteed attractions.

Cheetahs

The open Kalahari landscape can provide suitable hunting habitat for cheetahs.

Their ability to use open terrain makes them an especially interesting species to look for around broad grasslands and valleys.

Cheetah Conservation Botswana's research confirms the importance of the broader CKGR landscape for large carnivores.

Brown hyena

Brown hyena is one of the species that makes the Kalahari particularly special.

Unlike the more familiar spotted hyena, brown hyenas are closely associated with arid and semi-arid environments.

They are predominantly nocturnal and elusive, so seeing one requires patience and good guiding.

UNESCO specifically identifies brown hyena among the threatened species for which the CKGR provides important habitat.

African wild dogs

Wild dogs occur in the wider Central Kalahari landscape, although sightings are never guaranteed.

Their movements across large territories make them particularly dependent on connected habitats extending beyond individual protected-area boundaries.

Gemsbok

The gemsbok, or oryx, is one of the quintessential animals of the Kalahari.

Its ability to survive in dry environments makes it perfectly suited to the reserve's conditions.

Seeing a herd of gemsbok moving across golden grassland is one of the most characteristic Central Kalahari wildlife scenes.

Springbok

Springbok can occur in large numbers, particularly when rainfall creates good grazing conditions.

Botswana Tourism specifically highlights springbok and gemsbok among the herbivores that can gather in the northern reaches following good summer rains.

Wildebeest and hartebeest

Blue wildebeest and red hartebeest are among the important grazing species of the Kalahari ecosystem.

Their distribution changes according to seasonal food availability.

Eland

The eland is another large antelope found in the reserve.

Because eland can make use of a variety of vegetation and travel long distances, they are well suited to the large-scale environment.

Giraffe and kudu

Giraffe and greater kudu occur in suitable woodland and scrub habitats.

They are generally less associated with the most open grassland than species such as springbok and gemsbok.

Smaller Kalahari species

One of the pleasures of a Central Kalahari safari is looking beyond the headline species.

Depending on location and conditions, wildlife encounters can include:

  • Bat-eared fox
  • Honey badger
  • Aardwolf
  • Caracal
  • Jackal
  • Meerkat
  • Warthog
  • Steenbok
  • Ostrich
  • Numerous smaller mammals and birds

Some of these species are elusive, making tracking and experienced guiding particularly valuable.

Birds of the Central Kalahari

The CKGR is also important for birds, particularly species adapted to open and semi-arid landscapes.

Birding highlights can include ostrich, kori bustard, secretarybird, red-crested korhaan, various raptors, Kalahari-adapted grassland birds and ground-dwelling species associated with open plains.

The reserve's large landscapes and relatively low development also create excellent opportunities for observing birds of prey.

UNESCO's assessment specifically notes the importance of the area for diverse birds of prey.

When is the best time to visit the Central Kalahari?

The answer depends on what you want from the safari.

December to March: green Kalahari and wildlife concentration

The summer rains transform the landscape.

Dry grasslands can become green, pans may temporarily hold water and grazing conditions improve dramatically.

This is when the northern sections, particularly Deception Valley, can experience strong concentrations of herbivores.

Botswana Tourism notes that during and shortly after good summer rains, the northern grasslands can teem with springbok, gemsbok, wildebeest, hartebeest, eland and giraffe.

The disadvantages are equally important: rain can make some roads difficult, some tracks become muddy, thunderstorms can interrupt game drives, and wildlife may disperse when food and water become widely available.

So the green season is not automatically the easiest season for every traveler.

April and May: a useful shoulder period

The landscape can remain productive after the rains while conditions gradually become easier for travel.

For travelers who want a balance between greener scenery and improving road conditions, this period can be attractive.

June to October: dry-season wilderness

The landscape becomes progressively drier.

Grass loses its green color and visibility can improve as vegetation thins.

Wildlife movements become increasingly influenced by remaining water sources.

This can create excellent predator opportunities, but distances between sightings can also be substantial.

Temperatures vary significantly. Winter nights can become very cold even though daytime conditions can be pleasant.

November: building toward the rains

November is a transitional period.

Temperatures rise, landscapes remain dry in many areas and the first storms may begin appearing.

It can be an interesting time for travelers who prefer fewer visitors and dramatic weather, although rainfall timing is unpredictable.

Safari activities in Central Kalahari Game Reserve

Game drives

Game drives are the primary way to explore CKGR.

Because of the reserve's scale, drives can cover significant distances while following tracks, vegetation and wildlife activity.

The experience is particularly rewarding with guides who understand animal spoor and seasonal movements.

Wilderness drives

The reserve's remote character makes longer wilderness drives particularly appealing.

These are not simply transfers between attractions. The landscape itself becomes part of the safari.

Nature walks

Botswana Tourism lists nature walks with Basarwa/San guides among the activities available in the reserve.

A walking experience should focus on genuine interpretation of the environment - animal tracks, plants, traditional ecological knowledge and survival in a dry landscape - rather than reducing San culture to a staged performance.

Cultural experiences

Cultural experiences connected with San communities can provide important context about the relationship between people and the Kalahari.

The history requires sensitivity.

The CKGR was originally established as a sanctuary for San people, according to Botswana Tourism, while UNESCO's tentative-list documentation recognizes the long relationship between Basarwa/San communities and this landscape.

A responsible cultural experience should therefore emphasize living culture, knowledge and community benefit rather than presenting San people as a frozen image of the past.

Stargazing

Few safari environments are better suited to looking at the night sky than a remote Kalahari landscape.

With little artificial light and vast open horizons, nighttime can become one of the highlights of a stay.

Botswana Tourism specifically highlights the extraordinary visibility of the stars over CKGR.

Central Kalahari for photography

CKGR is exceptionally photogenic, but for different reasons from Botswana's wetlands.

The strongest subjects include black-maned lions, gemsbok, springbok, cheetah, brown hyena, golden grasslands, Kalahari sunsets, ancient pans, dust and silhouettes, storm clouds during the green season and night skies.

The open landscape provides opportunities for environmental wildlife photography, where the animal is photographed within its broader habitat rather than filling the entire frame.

Sunrise and sunset can be particularly dramatic because the low-angle light emphasizes grass, sand and distant vegetation.

Accommodation in the Central Kalahari

Accommodation ranges from basic public campsites to more comfortable safari camps and lodges associated with concessions.

The difference between these experiences is significant.

Public camping

Public campsites provide the most basic and independent way to experience CKGR.

They suit experienced self-drive travelers who are comfortable with remote camping and carrying their own supplies.

Safari camps

Private safari camps provide a more comfortable experience, generally combining accommodation, meals, guided drives and logistical support.

Tau Pan, for example, is a semi-permanent camp inside the reserve, located on an elevated ridge overlooking Tau Pan. Botswana Tourism lists game drives, excursions toward Deception Valley, Sunday, Piper and Passarge Pans, stargazing and nature walks with Bushman trackers among its experiences.

Fly-in safari

Fly-in access makes the Central Kalahari much easier to combine with other Botswana destinations.

Botswana Tourism notes that lodge concessions inside and around the reserve allow fly-in tourism.

For travelers with limited time, flying can transform CKGR from a difficult overland destination into a practical two- or three-night extension.

Getting to Central Kalahari Game Reserve

The route depends on where you are staying.

Botswana Tourism identifies Matswere, Xade and Tsau among the reserve's gates.

Matswere Gate

Matswere is particularly relevant for travelers entering toward Deception Valley.

It is one of the practical access points for the northern part of the reserve.

Xade Gate

Xade provides access from the western side of the reserve and is important for travelers approaching from the Ghanzi/Kang direction.

Tsau

Tsau provides another access point into the reserve and can be relevant depending on the route and safari itinerary.

Self-drive access

Self-driving in CKGR is possible, but it is not equivalent to driving through a well-maintained national-park road network.

The combination of deep sand, long distances, remote campsites, limited services, changing road conditions, extreme temperatures and limited recovery options means that travelers should have appropriate 4x4 experience and preparation.

For visitors unfamiliar with remote Kalahari driving, a guided safari is generally the more comfortable option.

How many nights do you need?

Two nights

Possible, but short.

A two-night stay works best as an extension to another Botswana safari, particularly when flying in to a lodge.

Three nights

Recommended minimum for a proper first safari.

Three nights allow time to settle into the environment, undertake several drives and experience both morning and afternoon or evening wildlife activity.

Four to five nights

Ideal for travelers specifically interested in CKGR.

More nights allow exploration beyond one immediate area and improve the opportunity to experience changing wildlife conditions.

Six to seven nights

Best suited to photographers, wildlife enthusiasts, serious Kalahari travelers, self-drive expeditions and travelers combining several remote areas.

The longer you stay, the more the destination becomes about understanding the ecosystem rather than simply collecting sightings.

Central Kalahari vs Okavango Delta

The Central Kalahari is a semi-arid Kalahari landscape defined by fossil valleys and pans, desert-adapted wildlife, black-maned Kalahari lions, gemsbok and springbok, very remote conditions, an excellent wilderness atmosphere and water-based activities being absent - it is best for a different Botswana experience.

The Okavango Delta is an inland wetland defined by floodplains, lagoons and channels, aquatic and wetland wildlife, exceptional diversity of habitats, red lechwe, hippo and aquatic species, a wider range of safari styles and water-based activities - it is best for a classic Delta safari.

For a first Botswana safari, the Okavango may provide a broader range of experiences.

For a traveler who has already experienced the Delta, the Central Kalahari can be an outstanding contrast.

Central Kalahari vs Moremi

Moremi is considerably more varied in terms of wetlands, floodplains and water-associated wildlife.

CKGR offers something fundamentally different.

Moremi is particularly strong for travelers who want water, diverse habitats, boating in appropriate areas, mokoro experiences in connected Delta zones and dense wildlife in productive areas.

CKGR is stronger for wilderness, Kalahari landscapes, desert-adapted animals, open horizons, black-maned lions, solitude and remote safari experiences.

Central Kalahari vs Chobe

Chobe is famous for huge elephant and buffalo concentrations, particularly along the Chobe Riverfront.

CKGR does not compete with Chobe on elephant viewing.

Instead, it provides a much drier ecosystem, fewer water-dependent species, stronger emphasis on Kalahari-adapted wildlife, greater sense of isolation and different predator-prey dynamics.

A Chobe and Central Kalahari combination can therefore be more interesting than choosing between them.

Central Kalahari vs Makgadikgadi

Both destinations demonstrate the influence of the wider Kalahari environment, but their landscapes and wildlife experiences are different.

Makgadikgadi is particularly associated with enormous salt pans and seasonal wildlife movements.

CKGR is characterized more by grasslands, fossil river valleys, scrub, sandveld and wildlife adapted to semi-arid conditions.

They work well together for a broader Kalahari itinerary.

Central Kalahari and Okavango safari

One of the strongest ways to experience Botswana is to deliberately combine opposite environments.

Example: 7-day Botswana safari

  • Days 1 to 3: Okavango Delta - waterways, floodplains, game drives and water-based activities where conditions allow
  • Days 4 to 6: Central Kalahari - Kalahari landscapes, predators, gemsbok, springbok, lions and wilderness drives
  • Day 7: Departure

This itinerary creates a genuine ecological contrast rather than repeating the same type of safari.

Central Kalahari for honeymoons

The Central Kalahari is not the conventional honeymoon choice - and that is exactly why it can appeal to certain couples.

It suits couples who value privacy, remote camps, dramatic landscapes, uninterrupted sunsets, stargazing, intimate wilderness experiences, photography and a sense of adventure.

A honeymoon combining the Okavango Delta and the Central Kalahari can be particularly memorable.

The Delta provides water, luxury and classic Botswana safari scenery, while the Kalahari adds a completely different atmosphere.

Central Kalahari for families

CKGR can work for families, but the safari style matters.

A private safari vehicle gives families greater flexibility over driving times, meal schedules, rest periods, wildlife stops and the pace of the day.

However, the reserve's remoteness and long drives can be tiring for very young children.

Families should also select accommodation carefully because camp policies regarding children's ages and activities vary.

For older children interested in wildlife, tracking and adventure, the Kalahari can be an excellent educational safari.

Conservation and the human landscape

The conservation story of CKGR is inseparable from its human history.

Botswana Tourism states that the reserve was initially established in 1961 as a sanctuary for San people. UNESCO's tentative-list documentation also emphasizes the long relationship between Basarwa/San communities and the Kalahari environment.

The reserve is currently managed by Botswana's Department of Wildlife and National Parks under the country's wildlife legislation, according to UNESCO's documentation.

CKGR is also part of a much larger ecological landscape.

Wildlife does not recognize reserve boundaries. Carnivores and herbivores can move through protected areas, wildlife-management areas and communal landscapes.

Cheetah Conservation Botswana's research in wildlife-management areas around CKGR has demonstrated the importance of these surrounding landscapes for large carnivores and for reducing human-wildlife conflict.

This makes responsible tourism particularly important.

A safari operator should support conservation, respect local communities and avoid presenting wildlife or indigenous cultures as commodities detached from their wider context.

Is the Central Kalahari worth visiting?

Yes - particularly if you understand what you are going for.

  • Big Five density: choose destinations such as Moremi, Okavango or Chobe alongside CKGR
  • Elephants: Chobe and the Okavango region are stronger choices
  • Water-based safari: Okavango
  • Black-maned lions: CKGR is exceptional
  • Kalahari landscapes: CKGR
  • Gemsbok and desert-adapted wildlife: CKGR
  • Remote wilderness: CKGR
  • A quieter, less conventional Botswana experience: CKGR

The Central Kalahari should therefore not be sold simply as another wildlife reserve. Its real value is the combination of landscape, wildlife adaptation, remoteness and cultural history.

Who should visit the Central Kalahari?

Wildlife enthusiasts find excellent predator and Kalahari-adapted species. Photographers get open landscapes and dramatic light. Honeymooners get privacy and remote wilderness. Adventure travelers get a remote 4x4 and wilderness experience. Families get a strong educational wildlife experience with suitable private arrangements. Birders get diverse dry-country birdlife and raptors. Repeat safari travelers get a very different Botswana ecosystem. Luxury travelers have exclusive fly-in camps available. Self-drive travelers find one of Botswana's more demanding safari environments.

Central Kalahari safari planning tips

Choose the right season

Do not automatically assume Botswana's dry season is the best for every CKGR safari. The Kalahari responds strongly to rainfall, making the green season particularly interesting.

Allow enough nights

The reserve is enormous. A rushed one-night visit does not allow the destination to reveal itself.

Consider a private vehicle

For families, photographers and travelers with specific wildlife interests, a private vehicle provides considerably more flexibility.

Do not compare it only by animal count

CKGR's value lies partly in what happens between sightings: the landscape, silence, tracking, light and sense of distance.

Use an experienced guide

In a vast semi-arid environment, knowledge of animal tracks, seasonal movements and habitat can dramatically improve the quality of the safari.

Combine ecosystems

A Central Kalahari and Okavango itinerary is stronger than treating CKGR as a substitute for the Delta.

Central Kalahari Game Reserve FAQs

Is Central Kalahari Game Reserve a national park?

No. It is a Game Reserve, not a national park. It is managed within Botswana's protected-area system and by the Department of Wildlife and National Parks. UNESCO's documentation identifies it as the Central Kalahari Game Reserve and notes its management under Botswana's wildlife legislation.

The distinction matters because visitors sometimes group every Botswana protected area under the term national park, even though Botswana uses different protected-area classifications.

What is Central Kalahari Game Reserve famous for?

It is best known for its enormous Kalahari wilderness, black-maned lions, desert-adapted wildlife, open grasslands, fossil river valleys, pans and exceptional sense of remoteness.

It is also significant culturally because of the historical and continuing relationship between San communities and the Kalahari landscape.

Can you see the Big Five in Central Kalahari?

CKGR should not be selected as a traditional Big Five destination.

Its major attractions are lions, cheetahs, brown hyenas, gemsbok, springbok, wildebeest and other Kalahari-adapted wildlife. The reserve's ecosystem is fundamentally different from Botswana's wetter northern safari areas.

If seeing elephant and rhino is a major priority, combine CKGR with another Botswana destination rather than relying on the Central Kalahari alone.

Are there black-maned lions in Central Kalahari?

Yes. Dark-maned Kalahari lions are one of the reserve's most famous wildlife features, and UNESCO's tentative-list documentation specifically identifies the black-maned lion among the area's notable wildlife.

As with all wild animals, however, a particular lion sighting can never be guaranteed.

What is the best month to visit Central Kalahari?

There is no single best month for every traveler.

For green landscapes and concentrations of herbivores following rainfall, the December to March period can be excellent.

For easier travel conditions and the classic dry Kalahari landscape, the cooler dry-season months can be preferable.

The choice should therefore depend on whether wildlife concentration, green scenery, road conditions or cooler weather is the priority.

Is Central Kalahari good for wildlife viewing?

Yes, but it requires the right expectations.

The reserve is enormous, and animals are not concentrated around permanent rivers in the way they are in some northern Botswana destinations.

Wildlife viewing can become excellent when rainfall concentrates grazing animals in productive areas, particularly in the northern parts of the reserve.

A skilled guide can make a significant difference because reading tracks, vegetation and seasonal movements is important in such a large landscape.

How many days should I spend in Central Kalahari?

For a meaningful first visit, three nights is a good starting point.

Four or five nights are better for travelers specifically interested in the Kalahari because they allow more time to explore different areas and wait for wildlife encounters.

If CKGR is part of a broader Botswana safari, two or three nights can work well as a contrasting component.

Can I self-drive in Central Kalahari Game Reserve?

Yes, but this is an adventure for experienced 4x4 travelers rather than a casual self-drive park visit.

The reserve is extremely large and remote, and some tracks can involve deep sand and difficult driving conditions.

Self-drivers need to plan fuel, water, navigation, recovery equipment, communications and camping supplies carefully. For travelers without remote-sand-driving experience, a guided safari is the safer and more comfortable choice.

Is Central Kalahari suitable for first-time safari travelers?

Yes, provided they choose the right style of trip.

A fly-in lodge safari with professional guides can make CKGR accessible to first-time safari travelers.

A fully independent self-drive camping expedition is a very different proposition and is better suited to travelers with previous remote 4x4 experience.

Can you combine Central Kalahari with the Okavango Delta?

Absolutely.

In fact, the two destinations complement each other exceptionally well.

The Okavango provides wetlands, waterways and floodplain wildlife, while CKGR provides semi-arid grasslands, fossil valleys, pans and Kalahari-adapted species.

Combining them creates a much more complete picture of Botswana's ecological diversity.

Is Central Kalahari good for photographers?

Yes, especially for photographers interested in environmental wildlife photography.

The open landscape allows animals to be photographed against broad horizons, golden grasslands and dramatic skies.

Black-maned lions, gemsbok, springbok and cheetahs can produce particularly strong images, while sunrise, sunset and night-sky photography add another dimension to the trip.

Is there Wi-Fi and mobile phone coverage?

Connectivity should not be treated as a defining feature of a CKGR safari.

Remote camps and wilderness areas can have limited or inconsistent connectivity. Travelers should prepare to spend periods disconnected from normal communications.

That is part of the appeal for many visitors, but it also means travelers should make important communications before entering remote areas.

What should I pack for Central Kalahari?

Pack for substantial temperature changes.

  • Light, breathable clothing
  • Warm layers for winter mornings and evenings
  • Closed walking shoes
  • Sun protection
  • Hat
  • Sunglasses
  • Camera equipment
  • Binoculars
  • Personal medication
  • Reusable water bottle
  • Headlamp
  • Insect repellent
  • Neutral-colored safari clothing

Self-drive campers require considerably more equipment because they need to carry essential water, food, fuel and recovery supplies.

Is Central Kalahari good for a honeymoon?

Yes, particularly for adventurous couples who prefer remote wilderness to conventional beach-style luxury.

A private camp, isolated landscape, sunset drive and clear Kalahari night sky can create an exceptionally intimate experience.

For a luxury honeymoon, pairing CKGR with the Okavango Delta or another Botswana destination can provide both wilderness and a more indulgent lodge experience.
