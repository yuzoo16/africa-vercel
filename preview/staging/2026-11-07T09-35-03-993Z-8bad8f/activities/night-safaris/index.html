Night Safaris in Africa: Bushbabies, Aardvarks & Hunting Lions

After dark the bush changes its rhythm - bushbabies, aardvarks and hunting lions, and exactly where a night drive is legally allowed.

Add to my safari WhatsApp

An African night safari reveals a side of the wilderness that many travelers never experience. When the sun disappears, the bush changes its rhythm. Bushbabies leap between branches, aardvarks emerge to search for termites, hyenas begin patrolling, and lions may set out to hunt under the cover of darkness.

Unlike a daytime game drive, a night safari focuses on wildlife that becomes active after sunset, alongside predators whose behavior can change dramatically after dark. Guided by an experienced ranger using a spotlight or suitable filtered lighting, travelers scan the surrounding vegetation, woodland, and open ground for movement that would be easy to miss during the day.

However, not every African national park permits night driving, and some of the most elusive nocturnal animals are difficult to find even in the right location. Choosing a destination that allows after-dark wildlife viewing is therefore just as important as choosing the right season or safari lodge.

This guide explains what you might see on an African night safari, where to experience one, how night drives work, and how to combine them with a broader wildlife holiday.

What Is an African Night Safari?

An African night safari is a guided wildlife experience that takes place after sunset, usually in a 4x4 safari vehicle. A guide and, where applicable, a tracker search for nocturnal mammals, birds, reptiles, and predators using their knowledge of animal behavior and the limited visibility available after dark.

Some night drives begin as afternoon safaris and continue after sunset. Others depart specifically for nighttime wildlife viewing, depending on the destination and operator.

The experience is fundamentally different from a daytime safari for three reasons.

Wildlife activity changes. Animals that rest during the hottest hours may become active after dark. Other species emerge from burrows or sheltered roosts to feed.

Visibility becomes more selective. Instead of scanning a distant landscape, guides concentrate on the area illuminated by their lights, animal movement, sounds, tracks, and other signs.

Encounters are less predictable. A night drive may reveal a bushbaby feeding in a tree or a hyena moving along the road. On another evening, the most memorable experience may be hearing lions calling in the distance or watching the stars above the wilderness.

A night safari is not simply a daytime game drive with less light. It is an opportunity to understand a different part of the ecosystem.

1. Bushbabies: The Small Primates That Come Alive After Dark

Bushbabies, also known as galagos, are small nocturnal primates found in various parts of sub-Saharan Africa. Their large eyes, strong hind limbs, and remarkable jumping ability help them navigate branches in search of food.

They can be among the most entertaining animals encountered on a night drive, although their small size and rapid movements make them challenging to observe.

What makes bushbabies special?

Bushbabies are adapted to life in trees. Depending on the species, their diet may include insects, fruit, tree gums, and other food resources. Their powerful hind legs allow them to leap between branches, while their hands and feet help them grip the surfaces on which they move.

Their large eyes are particularly useful in low-light conditions. When a guide's light catches a bushbaby's eyes, the reflection may be the first indication that one is sitting among the branches.

You might then see it leap to another tree, investigate a branch, or pause briefly before disappearing into the darkness.

Where can you see bushbabies on safari?

Bushbabies occur across a broad range of African habitats, including woodland and forest environments. In southern Africa, parts of the Greater Kruger region provide opportunities to encounter galagos around suitable habitats and lodge grounds. In East Africa, nocturnal wildlife can also be encountered on permitted night drives in appropriate locations.

The chance of seeing one depends on the local species, habitat, guide's knowledge, and conditions during the outing.

Some safari lodges have bushbabies moving around trees near camp, but a lodge setting should not be mistaken for a guaranteed sighting. These remain wild animals that move freely.

Why bushbabies are worth looking for

Large mammals often dominate safari photographs, but bushbabies offer a completely different wildlife encounter. Observing a small primate leap between branches can reveal how much activity takes place in the canopy after dark.

For families and travelers interested in smaller wildlife, they can be a memorable highlight of an evening safari.

2. Aardvarks: The Rare Night Safari Encounter

The aardvark is one of Africa's most unusual mammals. Its name comes from Afrikaans and means “earth pig,” a reference to its appearance and burrowing habits.

Although its body shape may remind some visitors of a pig or anteater, the aardvark belongs to its own mammalian order, Tubulidentata. It is specialized for feeding primarily on ants and termites.

Seeing one in the wild can be a remarkable achievement because aardvarks are predominantly nocturnal, often solitary, and difficult to locate.

How aardvarks survive after dark

Aardvarks use powerful claws to break into termite mounds and ant nests. Their elongated snouts and long, sticky tongues help them collect insects from openings in the soil.

They are also accomplished diggers. Their burrows provide shelter, and abandoned burrows can later become valuable refuges or den sites for other animals.

Because aardvarks generally spend the day in burrows, a conventional daytime game drive is unlikely to provide the same opportunity to see them active as a properly conducted night safari.

Where are aardvarks most likely to be seen?

Aardvarks occur in suitable habitats across parts of sub-Saharan Africa, including savannah, woodland, and other areas where their food sources are available. However, their presence in a region does not mean they are frequently seen by visitors.

Botswana's private concessions and community safari areas can offer opportunities to search for aardvarks on night drives. The wider savannah and woodland ecosystems of southern Africa also support the species.

Even in productive areas, sightings remain unpredictable. Aardvark encounters should be treated as a special possibility rather than something an operator can promise.

Why aardvarks matter to the ecosystem

Aardvarks are not just unusual animals to photograph. Their feeding behavior influences insect populations, and their digging creates burrows that may subsequently be used by other species.

These burrows can provide shelter for animals that cannot excavate their own refuges as effectively. In this way, aardvarks help shape their environment beyond their own feeding activities.

For travelers interested in rare mammals and animal adaptations, an aardvark sighting can be one of the most distinctive moments of a night safari.

3. Hunting Lions: The Drama of Predators After Dark

Lions are often photographed sleeping during the day, but their activity patterns are more complex than those images suggest. Lions may hunt, travel, socialize, patrol territories, or rest at different times depending on temperature, prey availability, disturbance, and other conditions.

A night drive creates the possibility of observing some of this behavior when light levels are low.

What might a hunting lion encounter look like?

A guide may find lions moving through the grass, following a herd, gathering near prey, or traveling along a familiar route. In some circumstances, visitors may witness hunting behavior.

However, seeing a lion at night is not the same as witnessing a successful hunt. Hunts are relatively brief compared with the time lions spend resting or moving, and many attempts do not result in a kill.

A lion may be following prey without making an immediate attack. It may abandon a hunt, change direction, or simply move out of view.

For this reason, the most responsible safari descriptions distinguish between the possibility of seeing hunting behavior and a guarantee of witnessing a kill.

Why darkness can favor predators

Many of Africa's large predators take advantage of reduced light and cooler temperatures when hunting or traveling. Darkness can change the way prey animals detect approaching predators, although hunting success depends on many other factors.

Lions do not hunt exclusively at night. They can be active during daylight, particularly when circumstances favor it.

The appeal of a night drive is the opportunity to observe behavior that may be less apparent during a typical daytime outing.

Can you see a lion hunt on every night safari?

No. A lion hunt is an unpredictable wildlife event, and most night drives should not be planned around the expectation of witnessing one.

You may instead see lions resting, calling, interacting, or moving through the landscape. You might also encounter hyenas, leopards, civets, genets, or smaller nocturnal animals.

A successful night safari is not defined by a dramatic kill. It is defined by observing wild animals responsibly and learning how their behavior changes after sunset.

4. Other Nocturnal Animals to Look For

Bushbabies, aardvarks, and lions are excellent reasons to consider a night safari, but they are only part of the after-dark wildlife community.

Leopards

Leopards may travel or hunt at night, although they are also active during daylight. Their ability to move quietly through vegetation makes them challenging to spot. A guide may locate one through fresh tracks, movement, or a brief glimpse of its eyes.

Spotted hyenas

Spotted hyenas are intelligent, adaptable carnivores that may forage, hunt, patrol, or interact with other members of their clan after dark. Their calls can carry a long distance across the bush.

Civets and genets

These small, often elusive mammals are frequently associated with nighttime activity. Civets and genets may forage along the ground or move through vegetation searching for food.

Their patterned coats can be difficult to see in low light, so a short, well-positioned glimpse can be particularly rewarding.

Porcupines

Porcupines are primarily nocturnal and may emerge to forage after dark. Their quills are a distinctive defense, but they generally avoid unnecessary confrontation.

Serval

Serval are slender wild cats with long legs and large ears. They are adapted to hunting small animals, particularly in habitats where vegetation and prey support their hunting behavior. A serval sighting can be an exciting result of a patient night drive, but it is not guaranteed.

Aardwolves and other specialized mammals

Aardwolves feed primarily on termites and are distinct from the larger, more carnivorous hyenas. Depending on the destination, guides may also search for springhares, African wildcats, honey badgers, and other nocturnal species.

The species available to see differ between ecosystems. A desert-edge night safari will not necessarily produce the same sightings as a woodland drive beside a major river system.

5. Where to Experience Night Safaris in Africa

The most important decision is choosing a location where night driving is actually permitted. Regulations differ between countries, parks, private concessions, and community-managed areas.

Tanzania: Tarangire and Lake Manyara

Tanzania is a particularly useful option for travelers who want to add a night drive to a northern safari circuit.

The Tanzania National Parks Authority lists night game drives as an activity in Tarangire National Park and Lake Manyara National Park, subject to the applicable rules and arrangements.

Tarangire's woodland, savannah, and river habitats support a variety of mammals and predators. Lake Manyara offers a different mixture of groundwater forest, woodland, and lake-associated environments.

A night drive can complement daytime game viewing in these parks by focusing on nocturnal mammals and after-dark behavior.

Night drives in Tanzania are regulated activities. Visitors should book through an authorized operator and confirm the current requirements, fees, departure arrangements, and ranger provisions.

Do not assume that a night drive is allowed throughout Tanzania's northern circuit. An itinerary focused on Serengeti National Park or the Ngorongoro Crater should not automatically be advertised as including a night drive inside those areas. Availability depends on the specific protected area and authorized operating arrangements.

Best for: Travelers combining a traditional northern Tanzania safari with a dedicated after-dark wildlife experience.

Botswana: Khwai and the private concessions of the Okavango region

Botswana is a compelling option for travelers interested in rare nocturnal mammals and specialist wildlife viewing.

The Botswana Tourism Organisation explains that night driving is prohibited in official national parks and reserves but can be available in private concession areas outside them. Community safari areas may also offer night drives under their operating arrangements.

Khwai, on the edge of the Moremi region, is one area travelers can investigate when night drives are a priority. Selected private concessions in the Okavango Delta and Linyanti region are other possibilities.

The precise activity offering depends on the camp and its concession or community-area arrangements. Confirm that the specific lodge offers guided night drives before booking.

Best for: Travelers hoping to search for aardvarks, civets, genets, and other elusive mammals, alongside Botswana's well-known daytime wildlife.

South Africa: Guided night drives in selected reserves and parks

South Africa offers night wildlife viewing in certain locations through authorized guided activities. Availability varies by park and accommodation.

Kruger National Park, for example, offers guided night drives through selected rest camps. Visitors should book through the relevant park or camp rather than assume they can drive independently after closing time.

Private reserves in the Greater Kruger region may offer their own guided night-drive experiences, subject to reserve rules and lodge arrangements.

South Africa's variety of habitats means a night safari can focus on large predators, smaller carnivores, bushbabies, nocturnal birds, or a combination of species.

Best for: Travelers who want to add an evening wildlife experience to a South African safari, particularly when staying in an area with established guided night drives.

6. What Happens During a Night Game Drive?

A night drive generally begins around dusk or after dark, depending on the destination and the operator's schedule.

The guide explains the activity, the rules for remaining in the vehicle, and how wildlife will be approached. A spotlight or approved filtered light is then used to scan the surrounding habitat.

Searching for eyeshine

Many mammals have eyes that reflect light back toward its source. This reflection can help a guide detect an animal in darkness before its shape is clearly visible.

Eyeshine is a clue, not a definitive species identification. The guide considers the animal's size, position, movement, habitat, and other visible features before deciding what it might be.

Small mammals can be especially difficult to identify because the light may reveal only a brief glimpse of the eyes or a small section of the animal's body.

Following sounds and movement

A guide may also listen for calls, rustling vegetation, alarm calls, or movement along the road. These signs can help narrow the search area, although sounds do not always indicate the presence of the species visitors hope to find.

The guide's knowledge of the landscape and local animal behavior is essential. A spotlight alone does not make an effective night safari.

How long does a night safari last?

The duration depends on local rules and the operator's schedule. Some drives run for a few hours, while others form the after-dark portion of a longer afternoon excursion.

In Tanzania, the national parks' published night-game-drive guidelines specify an operating window of 19:00 to 23:00, with an armed ranger required to accompany visitors. Specific arrangements and applicable rules should be confirmed for the park and activity booked.

Is a night safari safe?

Night safaris are conducted under controlled conditions, but they take place in environments containing potentially dangerous wildlife. Visitors should remain in the vehicle unless the guide authorizes leaving it at an approved location.

Do not attempt to approach animals, shine your own light into the bush without permission, or pressure the guide to get closer to a predator.

Following the guide's instructions protects visitors, guides, and wildlife. See our safari safety guide for the wider picture.

7. Responsible Lighting and Wildlife Photography at Night

Lighting is an important part of night wildlife viewing. Guides need enough illumination to locate animals and identify their behavior, but strong or prolonged light can disturb wildlife.

A responsible operator should follow local regulations and use appropriate lighting techniques, avoiding unnecessary disturbance or extended exposure.

What should travelers know about spotlights?

A spotlight can make it easier to locate an animal, but the guide should control its use. Visitors should avoid shining torches directly into animals' eyes or attempting to keep a beam fixed on an animal that is moving away.

Some operators use filtered lights, including red-filtered spotlights, where suitable and permitted. Lighting practices vary, so ask the operator about its approach if wildlife welfare is a particular concern.

Photographing wildlife after dark

Night photography is challenging because available light is limited and animals may move quickly. A camera that performs well in low light, a suitable lens, and familiarity with your settings can improve the results.

Practical advice:

  • Increase your camera's ISO when necessary rather than relying only on flash.
  • Use a shutter speed suitable for moving animals.
  • Keep your equipment secure while the vehicle is moving.
  • Ask the guide before changing position or moving equipment.
  • Follow all rules concerning flash photography and spotlights.
  • Accept that some encounters are better experienced than photographed.

Do not sacrifice a safe viewing position or disturb an animal to obtain a photograph. The opportunity to observe its behavior is often more valuable than a technically perfect image.

8. Best Time for an African Night Safari

Night drives can operate throughout much of the year where they are permitted, but the experience changes with the season and habitat.

Dry season

In some savannah environments, dry-season conditions can make animals easier to detect because vegetation is less dense. Wildlife may also concentrate around dependable water sources.

However, nocturnal animals do not all respond to the dry season in the same way. Aardvark activity, for example, is influenced by food availability, temperature, and local conditions.

Green season

The rainy season can bring lush vegetation, insects, and changes in food availability. It may also make roads muddy and reduce visibility in some areas.

Green-season night drives can still be rewarding, particularly for visitors interested in the behavior of smaller mammals and the sounds of the nocturnal environment.

Temperature and moonlight

Cooler evenings may make some night drives more comfortable, while hot conditions can influence the timing of animal activity. Moonlight can affect visibility, but it does not reliably predict which species will appear.

The most useful approach is to choose the season based on your broader safari goals and ask the operator what conditions are typical at the specific location. Our best time to go on safari guide covers the wider calendar.

9. How to Combine a Night Safari With a Longer African Safari

A night drive is most effective when it fits naturally into the itinerary. Instead of crossing a large distance simply to spend a single evening searching for nocturnal wildlife, choose a destination where night driving complements the rest of the safari.

Option 1: Tanzania Northern Circuit

Suggested duration: 5–7 days

Combine Tarangire and Lake Manyara with other northern Tanzania safari destinations. Plan a night drive in a park where it is officially permitted, then use the remaining days for conventional game drives and landscape exploration.

A possible structure is:

  • Days 1–2: Tarangire National Park, with a night drive if arranged and available.
  • Days 3–4: Ngorongoro Conservation Area for crater wildlife viewing.
  • Days 5–6: Serengeti National Park for expansive savannah and predator viewing.
  • Day 7: Departure or onward travel.

This itinerary does not imply that night drives are available in every park on the route. Arrange the night activity specifically in Tarangire or another approved location.

Option 2: Botswana Wildlife and Nocturnal Safari

Suggested duration: 6–8 days

Combine daytime wildlife viewing in the Okavango region with a stay in a camp that specifically offers night drives.

A possible structure is:

  • Days 1–3: Okavango Delta, with game drives and other activities available through your camp.
  • Days 4–5: A suitable private concession or community area where night drives are permitted.
  • Days 6–7: Continue wildlife viewing in a second area, subject to transfer logistics.
  • Day 8: Departure.

If you want to see nocturnal wildlife, confirm the precise concession or community area rather than relying on the broad destination name. A camp in a national park may operate under different rules from a camp just outside its boundary.

Option 3: South Africa Safari

Suggested duration: 4–6 days

Choose a park or reserve with an authorized guided night-drive activity, then combine the experience with daytime game viewing.

A possible structure is:

  • Days 1–3: Stay in a safari area with guided game drives and a night-drive option.
  • Day 4: Enjoy another wildlife activity, depending on the location and lodge schedule.
  • Days 5–6: Add another South African destination or return to your departure airport.

The exact itinerary depends on the chosen reserve, transfer times, and availability of guided activities.

10. How Much Does a Night Safari Cost?

There is no single price for an African night safari. The cost depends on the country, protected area, lodge category, transport arrangements, and whether the activity is included in the accommodation rate.

Some lodges include scheduled night drives as part of their safari package. Elsewhere, visitors may pay a separate activity fee, park fee, or concession charge.

Before comparing quotations, establish what is included:

  • Accommodation and meals.
  • Daytime game drives.
  • Night-drive activity fees.
  • Park, concession, or conservation fees.
  • Transport between the airport and lodge.
  • Private vehicle arrangements, if required.
  • Drinks, equipment, and other optional services.

If night wildlife is a major reason for your trip, it may be worth choosing a lodge based on its activity offering rather than selecting the cheapest accommodation and hoping to arrange a drive later. Our safari cost breakdown explains how quotes are usually structured.

11. Who Should Choose a Night Safari?

Night safaris appeal to more than experienced wildlife photographers.

  • Wildlife enthusiasts may enjoy learning how animal activity changes after dark and observing species that are rarely encountered on ordinary daytime drives.
  • Photographers may value the challenge of capturing small mammals, predators, and nocturnal behavior in difficult light.
  • Families may find a night drive memorable, but should confirm age limits, the duration of the activity, and whether it is appropriate for their children.
  • Repeat safari travelers may appreciate the opportunity to focus on species beyond the familiar daytime checklist of elephants, giraffes, zebras, and lions.
  • First-time visitors can also enjoy night drives, especially when they are combined with daytime game viewing. A night safari should complement, rather than replace, the broader wildlife experience.

Frequently Asked Questions About Night Safaris in Africa

What animals can you see on an African night safari?

Depending on the destination, you may encounter bushbabies, aardvarks, civets, genets, porcupines, servals, hyenas, leopards, and lions. Nocturnal birds and other small animals may also be seen. The species available depend on the habitat and local wildlife populations, and sightings are never guaranteed.

Can you see lions hunting on a night safari?

It is possible to observe lions moving, stalking, or hunting at night, but witnessing a complete hunt is uncommon and cannot be promised. Lions spend much of their time resting, and a hunting attempt may occur outside the area illuminated by the vehicle. A night safari offers an opportunity to observe after-dark behavior, not a scheduled wildlife performance.

Where is the best place in Africa for a night safari?

The best destination depends on your priorities. Tanzania's Tarangire and Lake Manyara national parks offer regulated night-drive opportunities. In Botswana, selected private concessions and community areas can be particularly interesting for nocturnal mammals. South Africa also offers guided night drives in selected parks and reserves. Always verify that the specific lodge or park permits the activity.

Are night drives allowed in the Serengeti?

Do not assume that standard night drives are available inside Serengeti National Park. The park operates under its own visitor rules, and a night drive should only be advertised where the relevant authority expressly permits it. Some camps in adjoining private areas may offer after-dark activities under different arrangements, so check the exact location and operating rules before booking.

Are night safaris available in Botswana's national parks?

Botswana Tourism Organisation states that night driving is prohibited in official national parks and reserves, while private concessions outside those areas can offer night drives. Community areas may also allow them. If a night safari is important, confirm whether your camp is in a permitted concession or community area instead of assuming that any lodge near the Okavango Delta or Moremi can offer one.

Is an aardvark easy to see on a night drive?

No. Aardvarks are elusive, predominantly nocturnal mammals that spend the day in burrows and search for food after dark. Their presence in a region does not guarantee a sighting on a particular drive. Aardvark encounters are best treated as a special bonus of a well-planned nocturnal wildlife experience.

Are bushbabies common on night safaris?

Bushbabies may be encountered in suitable woodland and forest habitats, particularly where guides know their usual activity areas. Their small size and fast movements can make them difficult to observe, even when they are present. A guide may first detect their eye reflection or hear activity in the canopy before identifying an individual.

What time do night safaris start?

The schedule depends on the destination and operator. Some drives continue from the late afternoon into darkness, while others begin after sunset. Tanzania's published national-park guidelines specify an operating window of 19:00 to 23:00 for regulated night game drives. Confirm the actual departure time and duration with the operator you book.

Are night safaris safe for tourists?

Guided night drives are conducted under established operating rules, but they take place in wildlife habitats containing potentially dangerous animals. Visitors should remain in the vehicle unless the guide authorizes leaving it at a designated location. Follow all instructions, avoid shining personal lights at animals without permission, and never approach wildlife independently.

Is a night safari suitable for children?

It can be suitable, depending on the child's age, the operator's rules, and the duration of the drive. Some properties have minimum age requirements or recommend night drives only for children who can remain quiet and seated. Families should check these conditions before booking and consider whether a shorter activity would be more comfortable.

Can I photograph wildlife during a night safari?

Yes, but night photography can be difficult because of low light and moving subjects. Use a camera and lens suitable for low-light conditions, follow the guide's instructions, and ask before using flash or any independent lighting. Some operators use spotlights or filtered lighting, but visitors should not assume every lighting technique is permitted or appropriate for every species.

What should I wear on a night game drive?

Wear comfortable clothing with a warm outer layer, since temperatures may fall after sunset even when the afternoon is hot. Closed shoes are useful, and a small bag can hold personal essentials. Avoid carrying unnecessary equipment that may become difficult to manage in a moving vehicle. Ask the lodge whether blankets are provided.

Does a night safari replace a daytime game drive?

No. The two experiences complement each other. Daytime game drives offer broad landscape views and opportunities to observe grazing animals, birds, and predators in daylight. Night drives focus on nocturnal species and after-dark behavior. Combining both gives travelers a more varied view of the ecosystem.

How many nights should I spend in an area offering night safaris?

One night may be enough to experience a guided night drive, but staying two or more nights gives you greater flexibility and time for daytime activities. If you are hoping to see rare animals such as aardvarks, a longer stay provides more opportunities without guaranteeing a sighting. Choose the duration based on the destination, your budget, and the rest of your itinerary.

Plan an African Night Safari

A night safari opens a different window into Africa's wildlife. Bushbabies reveal the activity of the treetops, aardvarks demonstrate the remarkable adaptations of nocturnal mammals, and lions offer the possibility of observing predator behavior under cover of darkness.

The key is to choose a destination where night driving is permitted and to book with an operator that follows the relevant rules. Tanzania, Botswana, and South Africa each offer different opportunities, but the wildlife and activities available depend on the exact park, concession, and lodge.

For a broader wildlife holiday, combine an authorized night drive with daytime game viewing, a walking safari where available, or a multi-park itinerary. Allow time for the unexpected, because the most memorable moment of a night safari is often the animal you never planned to find.
