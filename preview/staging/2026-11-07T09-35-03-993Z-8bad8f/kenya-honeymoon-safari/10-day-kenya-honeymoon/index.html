10 days on the Kenya circuit: Amboseli, Masai Mara, Diani Beach. Every day below is a real day on the ground, not a transfer with a game drive attached.

Diani Beach gets 6 nights

Diani Beach holds 6 of the 10 days, and that is deliberate: beach days are the reason the itinerary exists, so the split has to favour them.

What 10 days buys

10 days moves past the point of rushing: whole days rather than stops, and enough slack that weather or wildlife can bend the plan without breaking it.

Getting around Kenya

Kenya is a road circuit from Nairobi: the Rift Valley lakes sit on the way south, and the Mara is the end of the line. Samburu runs north instead, so it belongs to a loop that comes back. Internal flights between the parks are the alternative when the driving days need to go.

Best time to book

Kenya runs on two seasons. The one you pick changes what you see, what the camps cost and how far ahead you need to commit.

Dry season

June to October is the classic window: dry, comfortable and productive, and July to October is when the migration reaches the Mara. Wildlife concentrates around the remaining water, vegetation thins, and the parks are at their busiest in August.

Green season

The short rains arrive in November and the long rains in March to May. The landscape greens, rates drop and the crowds thin, and January and February are strong months for birding as the northern migrants arrive.

What actually drives the timing

The Mara has no single best month: migration from July to October, predator viewing year-round, and quieter, greener, cheaper parks from November to May. Lake Nakuru's flamingo numbers shift with the lake level rather than the calendar.

How far ahead to book

The migration months and July to August want six to twelve months of notice for the better camps. Green-season departures usually need two to four months, and the Christmas window compresses early.

FAQs

How far ahead should we book?

Peak Migration and gorilla-permit months need 6 to 12 months. Green season often works with 2 to 4 months notice.

Is this trip suitable for children?

With a private vehicle and pools at stops, yes for most routes. Gorilla trekking has a strict minimum age of 15.

How much should we tip?

As a guideline: 15 to 25 dollars per day for your driver-guide (per group, not per person), 10 to 15 dollars per day into camp communal tip boxes, and 20 to 30 dollars for a gorilla porter. Restaurants 10 percent where service is not included.

Can dietary needs be handled in bush camps?

Yes - vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free and most allergies are routinely catered with advance notice. Remote camps need 30 days notice for specialist supplies; tell us at proposal stage.

Camps cook from scratch, so most requests are a kitchen question rather than a supply one. Vegetarian, vegan and gluten-free are routine; halal and kosher products have to be imported in the remotest valleys, which is where the 30-day notice applies - Skeleton Coast, northern Uganda, the deeper Okavango camps. Menus are planned per group before the first grocery run, so tell us at proposal stage rather than on arrival. Guides carry your dietary notes on every drive, and camps refill filtered water wherever possible instead of trucking in bottles.

Can older travelers or limited mobility join?

Often yes: game drives are vehicle-based, many lodges have ground-floor rooms with walk-in showers, and we grade walking and trekking honestly. Tell us constraints and we design around them - except gorilla trekking, which demands real hill fitness.

Can we add a beach extension?

Yes - Zanzibar, Diani and Mozambique links are routine. Say the word and the flights slot into your proposal.

A deposit of 20 to 30 percent confirms camps, permits and flights; the balance is due 60 days before travel. We accept bank transfer and major cards (card fees disclosed upfront). Full terms arrive in writing before you pay anything.
