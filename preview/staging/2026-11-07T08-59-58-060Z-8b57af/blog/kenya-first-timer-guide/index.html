Before you fly

Apply for the eTA online (typically 72 hours); yellow fever certificate if transiting endemic zones; prophylaxis per doctor. Pack for 15kg bush-flight limits even on road loops - internal hops creep in.

The classic first week

Day 1 Nairobi arrival plus Sheldrick elephants. Days 2-3 Amboseli tuskers and Kili dawns. Day 4 Naivasha boats and Crescent Island walks. Days 5-7 Masai Mara - three nights minimum for cats and crossings. Fly the long legs, drive the scenic ones.

Mistakes to skip

Two Mara nights (too few), reserve-gate day trips in August (crowds), skipping travel insurance, and overpacking hard cases. Private vehicle removes the minibus lottery.

FAQs

Is Kenya safe for first-timers?

Yes on standard circuits with pre-booked transfers and vetted camps. Nairobi needs normal big-city sense - we brief every arrival.

Fly or drive to the Mara?

Fly under five days; road for week loops where Naivasha and Nakuru are part of the experience. Hybrids split the difference.

What will we actually see?

Guaranteed nothing, likely everything: Mara cats daily in season, Amboseli herds reliably, rhinos at Nakuru, flamingo flocks shifting by lake levels.

How much spending money?

300-600 dollars per week covers drinks, souvenirs and tips. Cards work in cities; cash for tips and markets.

Plan US$40-80 per person per day outside the safari: drinks at camp (US$5-15 each), curios (US$20-100 bargaining range), visa-on-arrival cash where applicable and tips. Cities take cards; park gates, village stalls and guide tips want cash in small notes.

Can we add beach?

Direct Mara-to-Diani flights make 3 beach nights seamless. Safari first, sand after.

What about kids?

Superb first family safari: short hops, boats and walks between drives, strong kids programs. Under-fives need private vehicles and pool stops.

How do deposits and payments work?

A deposit of 20 to 30 percent confirms camps, permits and flights; the balance is due 60 days before travel. We accept bank transfer and major cards (card fees disclosed upfront). Full terms arrive in writing before you pay anything.

We accept bank transfer and Visa or Mastercard; card payments add the processor's 2-4 percent, disclosed line by line. Splitting the balance across two cards is possible at proposal stage, and paying early in green season occasionally unlocks camp flexibility worth a few percent - always against a written schedule, never against an informal promise.

Are your guides qualified?

Our driver-guides average a decade in their parks, hold national guiding licenses plus first-aid certification, and are assigned by specialty - Migration positioning, photography, birding or family pacing.

How do we get the best price without hurting the trip?

Travel green season, bring four or more travelers to split the vehicle, choose tented camps over lodges, and drive where flying saves little. Never cut guide quality, Migration-zone positioning or insurance.

The four levers that actually work: green season (published rates 20-40 percent below high season with fewer vehicles at sightings), four or more travelers splitting the vehicle, tented camps over permanent lodges of the same operator, and road transfers under two hours instead of flight sectors. The three that do not: cutting guide quality, cutting Migration-zone positioning, and dropping insurance - the last one being a false economy at US$100-300 for a multi-thousand-dollar trip.
