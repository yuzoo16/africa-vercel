What a private safari is

One vehicle, one guide, one group. The day is shaped around you rather than around a timetable shared with strangers.

A private safari in Africa means the safari is built around your group instead of around a schedule shared with other travellers. Your own safari vehicle, your own guide and the freedom to shape each day change the experience considerably - from deciding when to leave camp in the morning to spending longer at a sighting that matters to you.

But "private safari" can mean different things. A private vehicle is not necessarily a private concession. A private guide is not necessarily a specialist guide travelling with you for the entire journey. And a private safari does not automatically mean an ultra-luxury safari. These distinctions matter when comparing safari packages.

The basic principle is simple: your vehicle, your guide, your interests, your pace.

A private safari normally means that the safari vehicle and guide are reserved exclusively for your travelling party. Instead of joining other guests for game drives, the vehicle is used only by you and your companions. That gives you considerably more control over the day:

  • When to leave camp
  • How long to stay on a game drive
  • Which animals you want to look for
  • How much time to spend photographing wildlife
  • Whether to have a picnic breakfast
  • When to return to camp
  • Whether to stay longer at an interesting sighting
  • Which activities matter most to your group

In Tanzania, for example, a private driver-guided safari means the exclusive use of a 4x4 or safari minivan with a professional driver-guide for the duration of the trip. That is fundamentally different from a shared safari in which several unrelated travellers occupy the same vehicle. Tanzania private safaris → Kenya private safaris →

Your vehicle: why it matters

The vehicle is your base for wildlife viewing, for travelling between parks and, in some destinations, for moving between camps.

In Tanzania, private safaris commonly use dedicated 4x4 safari vehicles with a driver-guide. The exact vehicle varies considerably between destinations: a photographic safari may use a purpose-built 4x4 with a pop-up roof, while a fly-in safari in Botswana generally uses an open-sided vehicle supplied by the lodge.

A good private safari vehicle should provide:

  • Large windows or open sides where appropriate
  • Good wildlife visibility from every seat
  • Comfortable seating
  • Space for camera equipment
  • Charging facilities where available
  • Drinking water
  • Storage for personal luggage
  • A roof or pop-up viewing configuration where applicable
  • Good ground clearance
  • Reliable communication equipment where appropriate

Ask what vehicle you will actually get before you pay a deposit, because the difference between a closed 4x4 with a pop-up roof and an ageing minibus is felt all day. Game drives → Photographic safaris → Tanzania photographic safaris →

Your guide: the person who shapes the safari

A private vehicle is only part of the experience. The guide can make an enormous difference.

A good private guide should understand animal behaviour, tracks and signs, bird identification, park geography, seasonal wildlife movements, photography opportunities, local communities, conservation, park regulations and road conditions. Knowing where wildlife has recently been sighted is what turns a long empty drive into a good morning.

The best arrangement is not to treat the guide as a driver who simply follows instructions. Instead, the guide contributes local knowledge while you explain what you want from the safari. That conversation can shape the entire day:

  • "We would rather spend two hours with one leopard than drive around looking for five different species."
  • "We are photographers and want to prioritise good morning light."
  • "We have young children, so we would prefer shorter drives today."

On a privately guided trip the same guide can stay with you across camps and borders, which is where continuity starts to pay: the guide already knows what you have seen, what you still want and how you like the day structured. Game drives → Bird watching → Safari photography guide →

Your pace: the biggest advantage of going private

This is where private safaris become particularly valuable.

Imagine finding a leopard at 7:30 in the morning. On a shared vehicle, other guests may be ready to continue because they have already taken their photographs. On a private safari, you can potentially stay longer if the guide believes the situation is appropriate and park regulations allow it.

The same applies to a pride of lions, a cheetah stalking prey, wild dogs preparing for a hunt, elephants crossing a road, a rare bird, a spectacular landscape or a photographic opportunity.

The principle is not "stay everywhere forever". It is having the freedom to decide where your time is most valuable.

That freedom works both ways. If a sighting does nothing for you, you can leave it. If the children are tired, you can return to camp early. If the light is improving, you can stay out. None of that requires a negotiation with six strangers. How many days you need →

Private safari destinations in Africa

Nine countries, nine different reasons to go private - and one shared advantage: the vehicle stays yours across all of them.

Private safari travel works almost everywhere in Africa, but the value of going private changes with the destination. On a busy northern circuit, a private vehicle buys you distance from other vehicles and control over departure times. In the Okavango or the Luangwa valley, it buys continuity of guiding across remote camps. In a self-drive country like Namibia, private guiding is what adds wildlife knowledge to an otherwise independent trip.

  • Tanzania - a private driver-guide can stay with the group throughout a road-based itinerary
  • Kenya - major wildlife destinations combined into private itineraries with a single vehicle
  • Botswana - private guiding and low-density wilderness, mostly by light aircraft
  • South Africa - several private-safari models, from private reserves to exclusive-use villas
  • Namibia - private guided road safaris, fly-in routes or self-drive with selected guided activities
  • Zambia - private game drives combined with walking and river experiences
  • Zimbabwe - Victoria Falls paired with private wildlife time in Hwange and Mana Pools
  • Rwanda - one vehicle connecting Akagera, the Volcanoes and Lake Kivu
  • Uganda - primates and classic safari joined by a single private guide

The country sections below set out what each one does best and the routes that suit private travel. African safaris → East Africa safaris → Southern Africa safaris →

Tanzania private safari

Particularly well suited to private safaris, because a private driver-guide can remain with the group throughout a road-based itinerary.

Why Tanzania works privately

The northern circuit is long enough that changing vehicles at every park boundary would undo the point of going private. With one driver-guide, the route can be adjusted day by day: an early start because the migration has moved, a late start because the light was poor, an extra night in the Serengeti because the sighting list is not finished. Southern Tanzania - Nyerere, Ruaha and the areas around Dar es Salaam - is the alternative for travellers looking for a less conventional route and fewer vehicles.

Private routes in Tanzania

  • Northern Circuit - Arusha → Tarangire → Ngorongoro → Serengeti. Best for first safaris, the Big Five, the Great Migration, photography, honeymoons and families.
  • Northern Tanzania and Zanzibar - Arusha → Tarangire → Ngorongoro → Serengeti → Zanzibar. A private safari followed by an Indian Ocean beach stay.
  • Southern Tanzania - Dar es Salaam → Nyerere → Ruaha. Useful for travellers after a quieter route.
  • Luxury private Tanzania - Arusha → Tarangire → Ngorongoro → Serengeti → Zanzibar, with fly-in transfers and a private beach stay.

Kenya private safari

Kenya's major wildlife destinations combine easily into private itineraries with one vehicle and one guide.

Why Kenya works privately

Kenya's classic circuit covers a lot of ground, and the difference between a fixed departure at 6:30 and a departure at 7:30 with a private vehicle is the difference between a rushed morning and a relaxed one. Private conservancies around the Maasai Mara add a second advantage: lower vehicle density and activities that are not available inside the national parks.

Private routes in Kenya

  • Classic Kenya - Nairobi → Amboseli → Maasai Mara.
  • Kenya wildlife circuit - Nairobi → Samburu → Lake Nakuru → Maasai Mara.
  • Kenya safari and beach - Nairobi → Maasai Mara → Diani Beach.
  • Luxury private Kenya - Nairobi → Laikipia → Maasai Mara → Diani.

Kenya's tourism authorities emphasise the country's diverse safari landscapes, including the Maasai Mara and Amboseli, and park regulations apply regardless of whether the vehicle is privately booked. Kenya private safaris → Kenya safaris →

Botswana private safari

Particularly suited to travellers who want private guiding and low-density wilderness.

Why Botswana works privately

Distances are large, camps are small and most transfers are by light aircraft, which means the itinerary is already built around a small group. Adding a private guide gives continuity across camps that would otherwise each supply their own driver. Game drives operate across parks, reserves and private concessions, and private concessions allow different operating possibilities from the national parks.

Private routes in Botswana

  • Classic Botswana - Maun → Okavango Delta → Moremi → Chobe.
  • Botswana and Victoria Falls - Okavango → Moremi → Chobe → Victoria Falls.
  • Botswana honeymoon - Okavango → Moremi or Khwai, with a private camp and private vehicle.

Botswana's private concessions are where off-road driving and night driving are sometimes permitted under the operating rules of the concession itself, which is a different thing from the rules of a national park. Botswana safaris → Southern Africa safaris →

South Africa private safari

Several different private-safari models in one country, which makes it the easiest place to dial privacy up or down.

Why South Africa works privately

Couples and families can choose between private game reserves, private lodges, national parks with private guides, exclusive-use villas and private safari vehicles. Kruger and the Greater Kruger region offer access to some of the country's most established safari areas, and guided game drives are conducted in safari vehicles with qualified guides across parks including Kruger and Addo.

Private routes in South Africa

  • Cape Town and private Kruger - Cape Town → Winelands → Greater Kruger.
  • Private South Africa - Johannesburg → Madikwe → Greater Kruger → Cape Town.
  • Family private safari - Cape Town → Garden Route → Addo → Greater Kruger.

South Africa also has the shortest internal transfers of any major safari country, which is why private itineraries here rarely need a charter. South Africa safaris → South Africa family safaris →

Namibia private safari

Namibia's private safaris take several forms, and the choice usually starts with whether you want to drive yourself.

Why Namibia works privately

Couples may choose a private guided road safari, a private 4x4, a fly-in safari, a luxury lodge circuit, or self-drive with selected guided activities. Self-drive gives independence and access to public safari areas, while private guided travel adds professional wildlife knowledge and access to areas that are not available to self-drivers.

Private routes in Namibia

  • Classic Namibia - Windhoek → Sossusvlei → Swakopmund → Damaraland → Etosha.
  • Luxury Namibia - Windhoek → Sossusvlei → Damaraland → Etosha.
  • Namibia and Botswana - Sossusvlei → Damaraland → Etosha → Zambezi Region → Botswana.

Namibia is also the one country on this list where the self-drive option is genuinely strong, so the decision is worth making deliberately rather than by default. Namibia safaris → Namibia self-drive →

Zambia private safari

Particularly interesting for travellers who want a combination of private game drives and walking or river experiences.

Zambia's guiding tradition is built around small vehicles and few people on foot, which makes the transition to a private format unusually natural. South Luangwa is the classic base for walking and night drives, while the Lower Zambezi adds canoeing and tiger fishing. Neither destination is about ticking off a list, so a private vehicle matters more here for time than for status.

  • South Luangwa private safari - Lusaka → South Luangwa.
  • Zambia wildlife safari - South Luangwa → Lower Zambezi.
  • Zambia and Victoria Falls - South Luangwa → Lower Zambezi → Livingstone.

Walking safaris and boat trips are usually small-group activities even on a private itinerary, so it is worth asking which parts of the day are genuinely exclusive to you. Walking safaris → Boat safaris →

Zimbabwe private safari

Zimbabwe can combine Victoria Falls with private wildlife experiences in well-guided parks.

Zimbabwe's national parks run excellent guided drives, and Hwange and Mana Pools both reward staying longer in one area rather than moving constantly. Victoria Falls sits close enough to either park to be added without a domestic flight, which keeps the itinerary simple.

  • Victoria Falls and Hwange - Victoria Falls → Hwange.
  • Zimbabwe wildlife safari - Victoria Falls → Hwange → Mana Pools.
  • Zimbabwe and Zambia - Victoria Falls → Hwange → Livingstone → Lower Zambezi.

Rwanda private safari

A private vehicle is particularly useful when combining different parts of Rwanda.

Rwanda is compact, the roads are good and the two main wildlife areas - Akagera in the east and the Volcanoes in the north - sit on opposite sides of the country. Having one vehicle and one guide turns what could be a series of separate transfers into a single continuous trip, and it makes a same-day change of plan possible when permit dates shift.

  • Gorilla and wildlife - Kigali → Akagera → Volcanoes.
  • Gorilla and Lake Kivu - Kigali → Volcanoes → Lake Kivu.
  • Complete Rwanda - Kigali → Akagera → Volcanoes → Lake Kivu.

Gorilla permits are limited and should be secured before the rest of the itinerary is fixed. Gorilla safaris → Gorilla trekking →

Uganda private safari

Uganda can be explored through private guided itineraries combining primates and classic safari.

Uganda's itinerary is long by road, so a private guide who stays from Entebbe to Bwindi avoids repeating briefings at every lodge. Kibale's chimpanzees, Queen Elizabeth's game drives and Bwindi's gorillas are three very different days, and the order of them can be adjusted around permit dates and energy levels.

  • Gorilla safari - Entebbe → Bwindi → Lake Bunyonyi.
  • Gorilla and wildlife - Entebbe → Kibale → Queen Elizabeth → Bwindi.
  • Complete Uganda safari - Entebbe → Murchison Falls → Kibale → Queen Elizabeth → Bwindi.

How many days do you need for a private safari?

Long enough that you are not moving every night - and short enough that the long drives do not eat the trip.

3 to 4 days

Suitable for a short safari, a first safari, a weekend safari, a safari from a major city, Nyerere from Dar es Salaam or a short Maasai Mara safari. Private guiding still helps, because the little time you have is not spent waiting for other people.

5 to 7 days

A strong length for the Tanzania northern circuit, Maasai Mara plus Amboseli, Kruger, Botswana, Zambia or Zimbabwe. This is where a private vehicle starts to change the shape of the day rather than just the departure time.

8 to 10 days

Useful for Tanzania plus Zanzibar, Kenya plus Diani, Botswana plus Victoria Falls, South Africa plus Cape Town or a full Namibia route.

11 to 14 days

Suitable for a multi-region safari, a multi-country safari, safari plus beach, gorilla plus wildlife, Tanzania plus Zanzibar or Kenya plus beach.

The longer the itinerary, the more valuable continuity of guiding and careful pacing can become. How many days for a safari → All safari packages →

How to make a private safari more personal

Having a private vehicle is only the starting point. Tell your safari operator what actually matters to you.

Wildlife priorities

Big Five, predators, elephants, wild dogs, the migration, primates, birds or photography - name the one that matters most, because it decides the route, the season and the length of stay.

Photography priorities

Sunrise photography, predator photography, birds, landscapes or night photography. If photography is the point, ask about seat configuration, window access, camera support and charging before you ask about the lodge.

Pace

Would you rather leave before sunrise, have a relaxed breakfast, stay out for a full day, return to camp for lunch, or have fewer but longer drives? There is no default answer, and the guide can only work to the one you give.

Accommodation

Tented camps, luxury lodges, private villas, family accommodation, plunge pools or private concessions. A private safari does not require the most expensive lodge - couples can prioritise a private vehicle while choosing a comfortable mid-range property, and that trade tends to improve the trip far more than moving up a room category does.

Activities

Walking safari, boat safari, balloon flight, cultural experiences, fishing, canoeing, or beach time. Ask which of them are private on your itinerary and which are shared by default - a private vehicle does not automatically make every lodge activity private. Private vehicle vs private guide

These are related but different concepts, and they are booked separately more often than you would expect.

Private vehicle

You have exclusive use of the safari vehicle. The guide assigned to you may change when you change camps or safari operators, which is normal on a road safari and less useful on a multi-region trip where you would rather not repeat the same briefing three times.

Private guide

A dedicated guide travels with your group, potentially throughout the safari, and that provides continuity. The guide learns what wildlife you have already seen, what you still want to see, your photography preferences, your preferred pace, your interests and how you like your safari days structured.

For a multi-region safari this can be a significant advantage. For a short single-country trip it rarely matters enough to pay for separately. Private safaris overview →

What is a privately guided safari?

A privately guided safari goes one step further than a private vehicle.

Instead of using the guide assigned by each individual camp, a dedicated safari guide accompanies your group through multiple destinations where the logistics allow it. This can be particularly valuable on longer journeys - for example Nairobi → Samburu → Maasai Mara → Diani, designed around continuity of guiding rather than treating each destination as a completely separate experience.

The availability and practicality of this arrangement varies by country and itinerary. Cross-border guiding depends on permit rules, vehicle registration and where the operator is licensed, so it should be confirmed during the design stage rather than assumed. Tailor-made safaris →

Private safari vs private concession

Four different things, often sold under one label.

  • Private safari - your vehicle and guide are reserved for your group.
  • Private concession - the safari takes place on privately managed land or a concession with its own operating rules.
  • Exclusive-use camp - your group books an entire camp, lodge or villa.
  • Private guide - a dedicated guide accompanies your group.

These can be combined. A high-end Botswana itinerary, for example, could involve a private vehicle, a private guide, a private concession and exclusive-use accommodation at once. But a private vehicle inside a national park does not automatically give permission for activities that park regulations prohibit.

Botswana prohibits off-road driving and night driving in its official national parks and reserves, while some private concession areas permit those activities under their own operating rules. Tanzania's park rules similarly prohibit off-road driving and generally prohibit night driving except for permitted organised activities. Botswana safaris → Tanzania safaris →

Private safari does not mean unlimited freedom

Your vehicle may be private, but the wilderness still has rules.

Depending on the destination, regulations may control:

  • Speed
  • Off-road driving
  • Wildlife distances
  • Night driving
  • Where visitors can leave vehicles
  • Park opening and closing times
  • Walking activities
  • Boat activities

TANAPA requires visitors in Tanzania to follow authorised roads and tracks and prohibits off-road driving in its parks. Kenya Wildlife Service requires visitors in its parks to remain in designated vehicles, stay on established roads and observe speed limits and wildlife-viewing rules. SANParks conducts guided game drives in safari vehicles with qualified guides across parks including Kruger and Addo.

So the value of a private safari is flexibility within the rules, not freedom to ignore them. A guide who knows exactly where the line is drawn is worth more than one who promises everything. Safari safety → Safari visas →

Private safari cost

Private safari pricing is influenced by much more than the vehicle.

The main factors are the number of travellers, accommodation level, destination, season, number of safari days, vehicle type, guide, park fees, domestic and charter flights, activities, beach extensions, private concessions and exclusive-use accommodation.

What drives the price

The vehicle cost is often shared across the group, so a private safari becomes considerably more economical per person as group size increases. A private vehicle for six people does not necessarily cost three times as much as one for two people. This is why private safaris make particular sense for families, groups of friends, couples travelling with another couple and multigenerational families.

Pricing should therefore be calculated for the actual group rather than judged against a shared per-person rate alone. Expect a private arrangement to cost roughly 20 to 40 percent more than joining a shared departure for two travellers, with that gap shrinking quickly at four or more - while an already-private camp game drive may cost nothing extra at all. Private safari vs luxury safari

These are not the same thing, and confusing them is the most expensive mistake on this page.

Private describes the way you travel. Luxury describes the level of accommodation and service. A private safari can use mid-range lodges, luxury camps, family lodges or budget-friendly safari accommodation, and a luxury safari can technically involve shared activities.

The two combine freely: private plus luxury, or private plus mid-range. This distinction gives travellers more control over their budget, because the private vehicle is usually the better place to spend and the room category is the easier place to save. Where the budget is fixed, that trade tends to improve the trip far more than moving up a room category does.

Private safari vs self-drive

Self-drive offers independence, but you are responsible for navigating, driving and finding wildlife yourself.

A private guided safari gives you professional wildlife knowledge, a local driver-guide, a safari vehicle, route planning, wildlife tracking, park logistics, local interpretation and someone else handling the driving.

Botswana illustrates the difference particularly well: self-drive can provide independence and access to public safari areas, while private guided travel adds professional wildlife knowledge and access to areas that are not available to self-drivers. For first-time safari travellers, the guided private format can therefore provide independence without requiring you to manage the entire safari yourself. Namibia self-drive → First safari guide →

Private safari for photographers

Wildlife photographers are among the travellers who benefit most from a private safari, because photography mostly requires patience.

You may need to wait for better light, hold position while an animal changes direction, watch behaviour develop, reposition the vehicle, return to a sighting later, spend more time with one species, or stop for birds and smaller wildlife that other guests might overlook. A shared vehicle can make all of those priorities difficult. A private vehicle gives the photographer much greater control.

What to ask about

For serious photographers, the conversation should go beyond simply requesting a private vehicle:

  • Seat configuration
  • Vehicle photography design
  • Window access
  • Camera support
  • Charging
  • Dust protection
  • Guide photography experience
  • Number of guests
  • Ability to spend extended periods at sightings

Where photography is the main reason for the trip, the vehicle specification is worth more than the room. Photographic safaris → Photographic safari collection → Tanzania photographic safaris →

Private safaris for couples and honeymoons

A private safari is particularly suited to couples, because the alternative is sharing the vehicle with strangers.

With your own guide and vehicle, it is easier to combine wildlife with romantic experiences such as sunrise game drives, private bush breakfasts, sundowners, bush dinners, private picnic lunches, special celebrations, flexible game-drive times, luxury lodge stays and private plunge-pool accommodation.

What to combine it with

A private safari can also be combined with a beach destination such as Zanzibar, Diani, Mozambique or Mauritius. A route such as Serengeti → Ngorongoro → Zanzibar creates a natural private bush-and-beach honeymoon without a long transfer between the two halves. Honeymoon safaris → Tanzania beach and safari →

Private safaris for families and groups of friends

The two groups who get the most out of a private vehicle are the ones who already know each other well.

Families

Families benefit from private vehicles because children do not necessarily follow the same schedule as adults. A private vehicle allows parents to discuss shorter game drives, later departures, longer lunch breaks, returning to camp early, child-friendly activities, wildlife interests and flexible meal stops. This is particularly useful when travelling with younger children.

However, age restrictions for specific activities still apply - a private vehicle does not override park or activity regulations. For example, chimpanzee trekking is restricted by age in Uganda, so the activity list should be checked against the specific country before the itinerary is fixed.

Groups of friends

Private safaris become particularly practical when several friends travel together. Instead of paying individually for seats on multiple shared vehicles, the group can occupy one vehicle and divide the cost. The group can then agree on its own priorities: wildlife, photography, birding, adventure, cultural experiences, walking or relaxation.

This is especially useful for groups that already know each other and want the freedom to travel together. Family African safaris → Family safari guide → Group African safaris →

Private safari for solo travellers

Solo travellers can also book private safaris, and the main consideration is cost.

When a vehicle and guide are reserved for one person, the fixed cost of operating that vehicle is carried by one traveller rather than being divided among several passengers. For solo travellers, a shared safari can therefore be considerably cheaper.

A private safari becomes more attractive when the traveller has specific interests such as photography, birding or a very flexible schedule, because those are exactly the things a shared departure cannot provide. Solo safari guide → Budget African safaris →

Questions to ask before booking a private safari

Before paying a deposit, ask these nine - and write down the answers.

About the vehicle and the guide

  • Is the vehicle genuinely private? Confirm that no other travellers will join your game drives.
  • Is the guide private? Ask whether the same guide stays with you throughout the safari.
  • What type of vehicle will we use? Ask for the vehicle type and seating arrangement.
  • Can we change the daily schedule? Confirm what flexibility the operator provides.
  • Are activities private? A private safari vehicle does not necessarily mean every lodge activity is private.
  • Are walking and boating activities shared? Check each activity individually.

About money

  • Are park fees included? Check whether conservation and park fees are part of the quoted price.
  • Are domestic flights included? This matters particularly for Botswana, Zambia and remote safari areas.

About the rules

  • What are the park restrictions? Private bookings do not override national park regulations.

Private safari FAQs

What is a private safari?

A private safari is a safari where the vehicle and guide are reserved exclusively for your travelling party, allowing greater control over the itinerary and the daily schedule.

Is a private safari worth it?

It depends on your priorities and group size. It can be particularly useful for families, honeymooners, photographers, birders and groups who want control over the pace.

Is a private safari the same as a luxury safari?

No. Private refers to exclusivity of the safari arrangement, while luxury generally refers to accommodation and service. The two can be combined, or kept apart.

Is a private safari the same as a private concession?

No. A private concession refers to the land or operating area. A private safari refers to your vehicle and guide.

What is the difference between a private vehicle and a private guide?

A private vehicle gives you exclusive use of the safari vehicle. A private guide is one dedicated guide who travels with your group across camps and, where logistics allow, across borders.

What is a privately guided safari?

One dedicated guide accompanies your group through multiple destinations instead of each camp supplying its own guide. It works best on longer, multi-stop itineraries.

Can we have our own safari guide?

Yes. Depending on the destination and operator, you can book a dedicated private guide for your safari.

Can we choose when to leave for game drives?

Usually, within park regulations and the operating rules of the camp or reserve.

Can we stay longer at an animal sighting?

A private vehicle gives you considerably more flexibility to do so, subject to guide judgement, park rules and the behaviour of the animals.

Can we change the daily schedule?

In principle yes - that is the point of the format. Confirm what flexibility your operator provides before you pay a deposit.

What are the park restrictions?

Speed, off-road driving, wildlife distances, night driving, leaving vehicles, park opening and closing times, walking and boat activities are all regulated. Private bookings do not override national park rules.

Can families book private safaris?

Yes. Private safaris are particularly useful for families because the itinerary can be adapted to the children's needs, although age restrictions for specific activities still apply.

Do groups of friends benefit from a private safari?

Yes, and usually at a lower cost per person. One vehicle is paid for once and divided among the group instead of buying individual seats on a shared departure.

Is a private safari good for photographers?

Decisively so. Positioning for light without a group vote, staying at a sighting and stopping for smaller subjects are all far easier with a vehicle reserved for you.

Does a private safari suit solo travellers?

It can, especially with specific interests such as photography or birding. The cost of the vehicle is carried by one traveller rather than shared, so a shared departure is usually cheaper.

Is a private safari more expensive?

Generally, yes, because the vehicle and guiding resources are dedicated to your group. However, the cost per person becomes more reasonable when several people share the vehicle.

Generally yes, because the vehicle and guiding resources are dedicated to your group - but the premium scales with group size. Split two ways a private vehicle adds 30-60 percent per person over join-in; split four or five ways it often lands within 10-20 percent of a shared safari while keeping exclusive use of the vehicle and guide.

Can we have a private safari on a budget?

Yes. Private does not automatically mean luxury. A private vehicle can be combined with mid-range accommodation to keep the overall price lower.

Yes. Private does not automatically mean luxury - the budget-private formula is mid-range tented camps (US$150-250 per person per day), a private 4x4 split by your group only, and green-season dates. The total lands 20-30 percent under a join-in luxury quote while keeping exclusive guiding; what you give up is lodge pools and spa menus, not wildlife access.

Are park fees included?

Check whether conservation and park fees are part of the quoted price. On some proposals they are included, on others they are itemised separately.

Fee schedules change annually and differ by citizenship - Serengeti around US$70 per adult per day, Maasai Mara conservancies around US$100, Kruger US$10-40 - so the right test is whether your quote lists each park, each day and each tariff. Ours does, and the same question exposes operators who discount the headline and re-add fees later.

Are domestic flights included?

This matters particularly for Botswana, Zambia and remote safari areas, where light-aircraft charters can be a significant share of the total.

Are activities private?

A private safari vehicle does not necessarily mean every lodge activity is private. Walking, boating and balloon flights are often small-group activities - check each one.

Can a private safari include Zanzibar?

Yes. A private Tanzania safari can be combined with Zanzibar, creating a private bush-and-beach itinerary.

Can a private safari include gorilla or chimpanzee trekking?

Yes. Rwanda and Uganda both combine trekking with private vehicle days, but permits are limited and should be secured first.

Private vehicles change the format more than the permits: you can time the trek-day start at will, run a chimp morning and Bwindi afternoon on one day, and route through the park without group-departure constraints. The cost driver is private guiding versus permits - permits are fixed-price either way, so the trek day itself does not get cheaper in a private trip.

Can a private safari end at Victoria Falls?

Yes. Botswana, Zambia and Zimbabwe itineraries can all end at Victoria Falls, which works well where a beach finish does not suit you.

Can we combine different African countries?

Yes. Private safari itineraries can be designed across countries such as Kenya and Tanzania, Botswana and Zimbabwe, or Zambia and Zimbabwe, subject to border, flight and vehicle logistics.
