First Safari? Start Here - Country, Days, Style

First safaris go wrong in predictable ways: too many parks, wrong month, shared vehicles with timetable tyranny. This playbook avoids all three.

Pick one country

Tanzania or Kenya for classic game viewing; South Africa for easy family logistics; Uganda for primates. One country done well beats two rushed every time.

Pick five to seven days

Five days covers a classic circuit; seven adds Migration depth or a second ecosystem. Shorter only works as a fly-in express to one park.

Go private, go dry season

Your own vehicle removes every compromise that ruins first trips. Travel June to October or January to February for the easiest wildlife viewing.

FAQs

Tanzania or Kenya for a first safari?

Tanzania for the complete circuit plus Zanzibar; Kenya for easier logistics and slightly lower cost. Both deliver lions, elephants and the Migration in season.

What is the biggest first-timer mistake?

Overstuffing: four parks in five days means windscreens, not wildlife. Two to three parks with two-night stays wins.

Should first-timers add Zanzibar or trekking?

Beach after safari, always - three-plus nights. Save Kilimanjaro and gorillas for trip two unless fitness and budget are solid.

The reasoning is fatigue sequencing: gorilla trek days and Kilimanjaro sit mid-trip when legs are fresh, and the coast only recovers you if it comes last. Zanzibar needs a real transfer from the bush - fly Kigali or Entebbe direct to Dar or Zanzibar rather than backtracking through Nairobi. Three nights minimum on the island; two turns the beach into an airport hotel.

What photography gear should we bring?

A 100 to 400mm zoom covers 90 percent of sightings; add a wide second body for landscapes. Beanbags beat tripods in vehicles. We provide charging setups and dust management briefings - tell us your kit and targets.

How do internal flights and luggage limits work?

Bush flights allow 15 to 20kg in soft duffels; lodges store hard cases free. We route connections with realistic margins and meet you at every airstrip - missed-connection cover is built into fly-in trips.

Is this trip private?

Yes - every itinerary here runs with your own vehicle and guide. No Mixed-group compromises unless you ask for a join-in price.

How fit do we need to be?

Game-drive safaris need no fitness. Trekking trips state their grade honestly - ask for the training notes.

What is NOT included that surprises first-timers?

International flights, visas, travel insurance (required), premium drinks, the balloon flight where offered, and tips. Our quotes line-item park fees and permits so the total you compare is the total you pay.

What happens if it rains?

Game drives run rain or shine - vehicles have roofs and ponchos, animals remain active, and green-season light is superb for photography. Only true downpours pause activity, and camps flex schedules around them.
