Khwai

Northern Botswana's community concession: night drives, walking safaris and the Khwai River.

Khwai is one of northern Botswana's most distinctive safari destinations, located along the Khwai River between Moremi Game Reserve and Chobe National Park. Unlike Moremi, which is a formal game reserve, the main Khwai Community Concession is managed through a community-based natural resource management model associated with the Khwai Development Trust (KDT).

The area is particularly attractive because it combines the wildlife-rich environment of the Okavango region with safari activities that are restricted or unavailable inside national parks. Depending on the specific concession and operator, travelers can experience night drives, off-road wildlife viewing, walking, mokoro excursions and motorboat trips alongside conventional daytime game drives.

Khwai is therefore not simply another version of Moremi. Its strongest selling point is the combination of exceptional wildlife, the Khwai River, community-based tourism and a broader range of safari activities.

What is Khwai?

Khwai refers to a settlement and surrounding wildlife areas in the north-eastern Okavango region.

The Khwai village lies immediately north of Moremi Game Reserve, while the Khwai River forms an important ecological feature around which wildlife and tourism activities are concentrated. Botswana government reporting describes the village as being on the north bank of the Khwai River, just outside Moremi's North Gate.

The Khwai Development Trust was established in April 2000 to represent the interests of the Khwai community and enable residents to participate in sustainable natural-resource use and tourism.

This gives Khwai a very different identity from a conventional national park.

Khwai is not a national park

This distinction is essential. Khwai is primarily a community-managed wildlife concession rather than a national park.

The area associated with the Khwai Development Trust lies north of Moremi and south of Chobe National Park.

Historically, the wider Khwai area has been associated with controlled hunting areas and community-based natural-resource management. Today, photographic tourism is an important part of the area's tourism economy.

Because it is outside the formal national-park system in the main community concession, certain activities can be conducted there that are more restricted inside Moremi.

Khwai and Moremi: why they are often combined

Khwai and Moremi are neighboring safari destinations, and combining them is one of the most logical ways to experience northern Botswana.

Moremi provides:

  • Extensive game-drive opportunities
  • Floodplains
  • Lagoons
  • Woodland
  • Okavango Delta scenery
  • Access to areas such as Chief's Island

Khwai adds:

  • Night drives
  • Walking opportunities
  • Off-road driving where permitted
  • Community tourism
  • Mokoro excursions
  • Motorboat trips
  • A different perspective on the same broader ecosystem

This means a traveler can spend several nights in Moremi and then move into Khwai without simply repeating the same safari experience.

The Khwai River

The Khwai River is central to the area's ecology. It provides a reliable water source around which wildlife movements become particularly interesting during the dry season.

The river and its surrounding floodplains support:

  • Elephants

  • Crocodiles
  • Antelope
  • Lions
  • Leopards
  • African wild dogs
  • Waterbirds

The river also provides the setting for water-based activities such as mokoro and motorboat excursions organized through the Khwai Development Trust.

Elephants in Khwai

Elephants are one of Khwai's signature animals. During dry periods, elephants are strongly attracted to the Khwai River and surrounding water sources. Wildlife tourism sources describe large elephant and buffalo concentrations around the river during the dry months.

This can produce memorable sightings of elephants:

  • Drinking at the river
  • Crossing channels
  • Feeding in woodland
  • Moving through camp areas
  • Walking along riverbanks
  • Swimming or wading through water

The presence of elephants around the community area also demonstrates why Khwai is not a conventional fenced safari park. The village, camps and wildlife exist within a shared landscape.

Buffalo

Buffalo are another important part of the Khwai ecosystem. Large herds can move toward the river during the dry season, creating opportunities for dramatic predator-prey encounters.

Buffalo are particularly important to lion ecology because large herds can provide substantial prey opportunities.

Lions

Khwai is an excellent destination for travelers interested in lions. The mixture of floodplains, woodland and riverine habitat provides several hunting environments.

The possibility of night drives also changes how lions can be observed. Instead of seeing lions only during their daytime resting periods, a night drive can reveal:

  • Hunting behavior
  • Movement after sunset
  • Vocalizations
  • Interaction with other predators
  • Nocturnal activity

This is one of the reasons Khwai is often combined with Moremi.

Leopards

Leopards are present in the Khwai area and can be particularly rewarding to search for during early morning, late afternoon and night activities. Their behavior makes them naturally difficult to find.

The advantage of Khwai is not that leopard sightings are guaranteed, but that the combination of experienced guides, varied habitat and night driving creates more opportunities to encounter animals that are active outside conventional daytime safari hours.

African wild dogs

African wild dogs are among the most sought-after predators in the Okavango region.

Their highly mobile nature makes sightings unpredictable, but Khwai's position within northern Botswana's connected wildlife landscape makes it a destination worth considering for travelers specifically interested in wild dogs.

A pack may cover large distances during a single day, so finding them depends on recent movements rather than simply being in the right reserve.

When a sighting occurs, the behavior can be spectacular:

  • Pack communication
  • Greeting ceremonies
  • Resting together
  • Coordinated hunting
  • Feeding
  • Raising pups

Cheetahs

Cheetahs occur in the wider Khwai ecosystem, particularly in more open areas suitable for high-speed hunting. They are naturally less common than some of the Delta's larger herbivores and predators, so sightings should be considered a bonus.

Hippos and crocodiles

The Khwai River provides habitat for both hippos and crocodiles. These species are especially interesting during water-based activities.

From a motorboat or mokoro, travelers can observe the river from the animals' own environment rather than viewing the water from a distant vehicle.

Red lechwe

The red lechwe is one of the antelope species particularly adapted to the wetland environment. These animals can move through shallow water and flooded grasslands with remarkable ease.

Their presence is another indication of how different Khwai is from a purely dry savannah reserve.

Birdwatching in Khwai

Khwai's combination of river, floodplain, woodland and open areas creates excellent birding conditions.

Water-based activities are particularly useful for birdwatchers because they allow slow movement through habitats where waterbirds concentrate. Depending on season and habitat, birders may encounter:

  • African fish eagle
  • Kingfishers
  • Herons
  • Egrets
  • Storks
  • Bee-eaters
  • Hornbills
  • Raptors
  • Jacanas
  • Wattled cranes
  • Ground hornbills
  • Various waterbirds

For serious birders, Khwai is best incorporated into a longer northern Botswana itinerary rather than treated as a one-night stop.

Why night drives make Khwai special

One of the strongest reasons to choose Khwai is the possibility of night drives. This changes the safari experience completely.

During daylight, travelers tend to focus on:

  • Elephants
  • Buffalo
  • Giraffe
  • Zebra
  • Lions
  • Antelope

After dark, a different wildlife community becomes more visible. Depending on location and luck, night drives can reveal:

  • Serval
  • Genet
  • Civet
  • Porcupine
  • Hyena
  • Bushbuck
  • Nocturnal birds
  • Predators becoming active

The night also changes familiar animals. A pride of lions seen sleeping under a tree at midday may behave completely differently after sunset.

This is one of Khwai's biggest advantages over a conventional national-park safari.

Off-road game drives

Another attraction of the Khwai concession is that off-road driving can be permitted under the rules governing the relevant concession. This can be particularly valuable when guides are following predators.

For example, if a wild dog pack moves away from the road, a guide operating in an area where off-road driving is allowed may be able to follow the animals more closely than would be possible in a conventional national park.

However, off-road access should never be treated as permission to chase wildlife. Responsible guides balance the opportunity for close observation with animal welfare and conservation requirements.

Walking safaris in Khwai

Walking is another major attraction. A walking safari changes the traveler from an observer inside a vehicle into a participant moving through the environment. The guide can interpret:

  • Tracks
  • Droppings
  • Animal trails
  • Plants
  • Insects
  • Bird calls
  • Predator signs
  • Animal behavior

Walking is particularly valuable for travelers who have already experienced conventional game drives and want to understand the ecosystem at a more detailed level.

Because Khwai contains dangerous wildlife, walking must be conducted under professional guidance and according to the rules of the specific concession.

Mokoro safaris in Khwai

The mokoro is one of the most recognizable ways to experience the Okavango ecosystem. The Khwai Development Trust offers mokoro trips accompanied by KDT escort guides.

The experience is quiet and slow. Rather than driving through the bush, travelers glide through the river and shallow waterways. A mokoro is particularly rewarding for:

  • Photography
  • Birdwatching
  • Water landscapes
  • Close observation of reeds and aquatic plants
  • Quiet exploration

It should not be confused with a conventional game drive. The value of the mokoro is the wetland experience itself, although wildlife sightings can certainly occur.

Motorboat safaris

For travelers who prefer a faster or more comfortable water experience, the Khwai Development Trust also offers motorboat excursions with KDT escort guides. Motorboats are particularly useful where deeper or broader waterways make a conventional mokoro less suitable.

They can provide opportunities to observe:

  • Hippos
  • Crocodiles
  • Elephants near water
  • Waterbirds
  • River landscapes

Cultural experiences in Khwai

Khwai is particularly valuable for travelers who want their safari to include interaction with the people who live alongside the wildlife. The Khwai Development Trust represents the community of Khwai Village and uses tourism as part of its broader community-development model.

Cultural experiences can provide an opportunity to understand:

  • Life in a remote Botswana village
  • The relationship between communities and wildlife
  • Traditional livelihoods
  • The importance of tourism
  • How conservation affects local communities

A cultural visit should be approached as a genuine community experience rather than simply as entertainment.

Community-based conservation in Khwai

This is one of the most important aspects of the destination. The Khwai Development Trust was established in April 2000 and represents the community in the management and sustainable use of local natural resources.

According to the Trust, tourism income contributes to community activities including:

  • Housing support for vulnerable residents
  • Support for elderly residents
  • Community dividends
  • Assistance with medical transportation
  • Support during emergencies and funerals

This gives Khwai a direct connection between safari tourism and community development.

The model is not without challenges. Botswana government reporting has documented disputes over tourism leases, land allocation and community ownership in Khwai, showing that community-based conservation is an ongoing governance process rather than a simple tourism slogan.

Khwai and community tourism: why it matters to travelers

When travelers spend money in Khwai, they are participating in an economy where local institutions have a role in tourism.

The Botswana government reported in 2025 that joint-venture partnerships involving community trusts in Khwai and Mababe represented more than P72 million in investment, more than P3.2 million in annual rentals, and employment for 127 people.

The exact financial arrangements vary by concession and operator, but the broader point is important: Khwai demonstrates how tourism can become part of the economic value of wildlife for communities living beside protected areas.

Khwai's relationship with Moremi and Chobe

Khwai occupies an exceptionally strategic location. The Khwai Development Trust describes its area as being north of Moremi Game Reserve and south of Chobe National Park.

This makes Khwai an excellent connecting point in a northern Botswana safari. A traveler can create an itinerary such as Maun → Moremi → Khwai → Chobe, or Maun → Okavango Delta → Khwai → Savuti → Chobe.

This progression works because the destinations are geographically and ecologically connected.

Best time to visit Khwai

Khwai can be visited throughout the year, but the experience changes considerably with the seasons.

May to August

This is one of the strongest periods for classic Khwai safari conditions. The weather becomes cooler and drier while water remains an important feature of the landscape. Wildlife increasingly concentrates around reliable water sources.

The combination of dry weather, riverine habitat, concentrated wildlife, cooler mornings and excellent photography makes this a popular period.

September to October

The landscape becomes increasingly dry. Temperatures rise substantially, particularly in October. Wildlife can become increasingly concentrated around the Khwai River and other reliable water sources.

This can be excellent for predator and elephant viewing, although the heat should be considered when planning long game drives.

November to March

The summer rains transform the landscape. The bush becomes greener and water becomes more widely available. This can disperse some wildlife because animals are no longer forced to concentrate around permanent water.

However, the season can be excellent for birding, green landscapes, photography, newborn animals and dramatic thunderstorms. Road conditions can become more difficult after heavy rainfall.

April

April is a transition period. The landscape begins moving toward the dry season, although conditions vary considerably between years.

Water levels and road conditions should be checked closer to travel because the Okavango system can experience substantial hydrological variation.

Flooding and access to Khwai

Water is one of Khwai's greatest attractions, but it can also affect access.

In 2026, significant flooding affected parts of the Okavango region, disrupting some roads and game-drive routes while allowing some camps to continue operating through boat transfers. Botswana government reporting noted that some areas remained closed while alternative routes and water-based activities were used.

This is important when planning a self-drive safari. The fact that a route was accessible in one month or one year does not guarantee identical conditions on your travel date.

How to get to Khwai

From Maun by 4x4

Khwai can be reached overland from Maun. The journey involves remote safari roads and can include sandy or waterlogged sections depending on the season. A properly equipped 4x4 is essential for self-driving.

By light aircraft

Fly-in safari guests can reach the Khwai area through local airstrips serving safari camps. This is the easiest option for travelers staying at remote lodges.

It also allows Khwai to be incorporated into a multi-destination Botswana safari without spending long periods driving between camps.

Self-drive safari in Khwai

Khwai is particularly attractive to experienced self-drive travelers. The community-managed area provides opportunities for travelers who want to combine:

  • Independent travel
  • Camping
  • Game drives
  • Mokoro excursions
  • Community experiences

The Khwai Development Trust operates campsites including Magotho, Matswere and Sable Alley.

Self-driving in this environment should not be underestimated. Travelers need:

  • A capable 4x4
  • Recovery equipment
  • Spare tires
  • Adequate fuel
  • Sufficient drinking water
  • Food supplies
  • Navigation equipment
  • Emergency communication capability

Wildlife is present around roads and campsites, so travelers must also understand wilderness safety.

Camping in Khwai

Camping can be one of the most immersive ways to experience the area. The Khwai Development Trust operates community campsites and offers visitors a way to stay within the wildlife landscape while contributing to the community tourism system.

The experience is very different from staying inside a conventional hotel. At night, the surrounding bush remains active. Elephants, hippos and other wildlife can move through the wider area, meaning visitors must treat the campsite as part of the wilderness.

Luxury lodges in Khwai

Travelers who prefer comfort can choose from safari lodges and tented camps around the Khwai area. Botswana Tourism's accommodation directory describes Khwai properties with access to:

  • Game drives
  • Floodplains
  • Riverine vegetation
  • Drier woodland
  • Mokoro activities
  • Luxury tented accommodation

The exact activities depend on the property's location and concession.

This is important because Khwai is sometimes used broadly in accommodation marketing. Travelers should verify exactly which concession the camp occupies before booking.

Why stay in Khwai after Moremi?

This is one of the most useful itinerary questions. If you have already spent three nights in Moremi, you might wonder whether Khwai is necessary.

The answer is yes, if you want different activities. Moremi gives you a strong daytime game-drive experience. Khwai can add:

  • Night drives
  • Walking
  • Off-road tracking
  • Community experiences
  • Mokoro
  • Motorboat excursions

Therefore, the value of Khwai is not simply more wildlife. It is more ways to experience the wildlife.

Khwai vs Moremi

The two destinations sit side by side but are managed very differently. Khwai is a community-managed concession outside formal national-park boundaries, where night drives are available in the relevant concession, walking is available in appropriate areas and off-road driving may be permitted. It has a strong community tourism component and the Khwai River at its center.

Moremi is a formal, protected game reserve where night driving is restricted, walking is more regulated and management follows formal reserve rules. Both offer excellent wildlife, self-drive options and access to the wider Okavango wetland system.

The strongest itinerary is often both, rather than choosing one.

Khwai vs Okavango Delta

The Okavango Delta is the larger ecosystem. Khwai is a specific wildlife and community tourism area on the northern/eastern side of the Delta system.

Choose the broader Okavango experience if your priority is:

  • Mokoro
  • Wetland landscapes
  • Luxury fly-in camps
  • Water-based safari

Choose Khwai if you particularly want:

  • Night drives
  • Walking
  • Community tourism
  • Predator tracking
  • A combination of water and land activities

Khwai vs Chobe

Khwai and Chobe work extremely well together. Khwai is particularly strong for predator viewing, night drives, walking, Okavango habitats and community tourism.

Chobe is particularly famous for large elephant populations, river-based wildlife viewing, Chobe River cruises and northern Botswana's river ecosystem.

A combination gives travelers two very different wildlife environments.

Khwai for honeymoons

Khwai is a strong honeymoon destination for couples who want an adventurous safari rather than a purely luxury-resort experience. A honeymoon can combine Khwai → Chobe → Victoria Falls, or Okavango Delta → Khwai → Chobe.

The romantic appeal comes from the setting:

  • Remote tented camps
  • River landscapes
  • Private dinners
  • Sunset drives
  • Night wildlife
  • Mokoro excursions
  • Wilderness surroundings

For honeymooners who prioritize complete privacy, a private concession may be preferable to the busier community concession.

Khwai for families

Khwai can work well for families, but accommodation and activity rules should be checked carefully. Night drives and walking activities are not automatically appropriate for young children.

Families should select accommodation based on:

  • Minimum age
  • Family room configuration
  • Child-friendly activities
  • Game-drive duration
  • Meal flexibility
  • Transfer arrangements

Older children can find Khwai particularly exciting because the safari extends beyond the conventional daytime game drive.

Khwai for photographers

Khwai is particularly attractive for wildlife photographers because it offers several different shooting conditions. Photographers can work with:

  • Sunrise
  • Sunset

  • River landscapes
  • Elephants

Night drives also create opportunities for a completely different photographic style, although photographers should check camp and guide policies regarding flash and specialist equipment.

Conservation challenges in Khwai

The success of Khwai's tourism model also brings challenges. The area is ecologically sensitive and has experienced increasing tourism pressure.

Botswana government reporting has highlighted concerns around illegal tourism operations and land allocation in the wider Khwai area, with environmental assessment and planning issues remaining important.

There are also human-wildlife challenges because communities and wildlife occupy neighboring landscapes. Khwai is therefore a useful example of a broader conservation question: how can communities benefit economically from wildlife while still maintaining a functioning ecosystem? The answer is ongoing rather than complete.

Responsible tourism in Khwai

A responsible Khwai safari should support both conservation and the local community. Travelers can contribute by:

  • Booking legitimate tourism operators
  • Using registered community services
  • Following guide instructions
  • Respecting wildlife distances
  • Avoiding feeding animals
  • Keeping campsites clean
  • Reducing plastic waste
  • Supporting local crafts where appropriate
  • Participating respectfully in community experiences

The community-based model works best when tourism creates real local value.

Khwai Safari FAQs

Is Khwai a national park?

No. Khwai is primarily associated with a community-managed wildlife concession rather than a national park.

The Khwai Development Trust represents the community of Khwai Village and manages tourism-related activities and facilities as part of its community-based natural-resource management model.

Where is Khwai located?

Khwai lies in north-western Botswana along the Khwai River, immediately north of Moremi Game Reserve and south of the wider Chobe region.

This location puts it within one of Botswana's most productive wildlife landscapes.

Why is Khwai famous?

Khwai is famous for combining excellent wildlife with a broader range of safari activities than travelers may find inside a conventional national park. Its main attractions include:

  • Wildlife
  • Khwai River
  • Night drives
  • Walking
  • Off-road game viewing where permitted
  • Mokoro
  • Boat excursions
  • Community tourism

That combination is what makes Khwai particularly distinctive.

Can you see the Big Five in Khwai?

The wider Khwai/Okavango ecosystem supports the major large African mammals, including elephant, buffalo, lion, leopard and rhinoceros. However, a Big Five sighting is never guaranteed.

Elephants, buffalo and lions are generally easier possibilities than leopard or rhino.

The quality of the safari should be judged by the overall wildlife experience rather than by whether a checklist of five species was completed.

Can you do night drives in Khwai?

Yes, night drives are one of Khwai's major attractions in areas where the concession rules permit them.

This is an important difference from Moremi Game Reserve. Night drives allow travelers to search for nocturnal wildlife and observe species that behave differently after sunset.

Can you walk in Khwai?

Walking safaris are possible in appropriate areas under professional guidance.

Walking should never be treated as an independent activity because the area contains elephants, lions, hippos and other potentially dangerous wildlife. The guide determines where and when walking is appropriate.

Can you do a mokoro trip in Khwai?

Yes. The Khwai Development Trust specifically offers mokoro trips accompanied by KDT escort guides.

Water levels and exact routes can vary, so travelers should confirm the activity with their camp or community operator.

Can you visit Khwai on a self-drive safari?

Yes, Khwai is one of the northern Botswana destinations that can be incorporated into a self-drive itinerary. However, travelers need a capable 4x4 and substantial preparation.

The area is remote, roads can become difficult after rain, and wildlife is present around camps and roads. Self-drive is best suited to travelers who already have genuine off-road experience.

How do I get to Khwai from Maun?

There are two main approaches:

  • Overland: by properly equipped 4x4
  • Air: by light aircraft to an airstrip serving the relevant safari camp

The better option depends on whether the trip is designed as a self-drive/mobile safari or a lodge-based fly-in safari.

How many nights should I stay in Khwai?

Three nights is a strong minimum for most travelers. It allows time for daytime game drives, a night drive and at least one additional activity such as mokoro, boating or walking where available.

Two nights works for a shorter itinerary, while four or five nights are appropriate for travelers particularly interested in wildlife photography or predators.

Is Khwai better than Moremi?

Not necessarily. They are different experiences within the same broader ecosystem.

Moremi is a formal game reserve with outstanding wildlife and diverse Delta habitats. Khwai adds activities such as night drives, walking and community tourism.

For a first Botswana safari, combining both is often more valuable than choosing only one.

Is Khwai good for a honeymoon?

Yes. Khwai is particularly suitable for adventurous honeymooners who want intimate wilderness experiences rather than a traditional resort atmosphere.

Couples can combine luxury accommodation with private game drives, night drives, mokoro trips, bush dinners, sunset experiences and wildlife viewing.

For maximum privacy, a private concession may be more suitable than the busier community concession.

Is Khwai expensive?

Prices vary substantially depending on whether you choose:

  • Community camping
  • Mobile safari
  • Mid-range lodge
  • Luxury tented camp
  • Private concession

This makes Khwai more flexible than the idea of a Botswana luxury safari might suggest. The community camping model can provide a very different price point from an exclusive fly-in lodge.

Is Khwai crowded?

It depends heavily on the exact area. The community concession has more tourism activity than the most exclusive private concessions. During busy seasons, areas around major river crossings can see multiple safari vehicles.

Travelers whose highest priority is solitude should consider a private concession, while travelers who value activity variety and community access may prefer Khwai.

What wildlife is best in Khwai?

There is no single answer, but the area is particularly attractive for:

  • Elephant
  • Lion
  • Leopard
  • Buffalo
  • African wild dog
  • Hippo
  • Red lechwe

The combination of predators, large herbivores and water makes Khwai particularly rewarding for wildlife enthusiasts.

Is Khwai safe?

Khwai is a genuine wilderness destination. It is safe when travelers follow professional guidance, but wildlife is not separated from visitors by the same barriers found in urban environments. Guests should:

  • Never walk outside camp alone at night
  • Follow guide instructions
  • Never approach wildlife
  • Keep food secure
  • Use camp escorts where required
  • Respect all wildlife warnings

Botswana government reporting has itself highlighted the need for strong safety measures around tourism camps because dangerous wildlife moves through the Khwai area.
