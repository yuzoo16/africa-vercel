Best time to book

The northern circuit runs on two seasons. The one you pick changes what you see, what the camps cost and how far ahead you need to commit.

Dry season: June to October

Tanzania's cooler, drier high season is the easiest window for wildlife. Water shrinks to the Tarangire River and the remaining pools, vegetation thins and animals gather where they can still drink, so sightings come faster and the crater floor is at its most open. It is also peak price and peak crowd: July and August are the busiest weeks in the parks, and the better camps go first.

Green season: November to May

Short rains begin in November and the landscape greens within weeks, with rates down and the parks nearly empty. January to March is calving season on the southern Serengeti, where predators concentrate around the newborn herds and the photography is at its sharpest. March and April bring the long rains, when lodges cut hardest, visitor numbers fall to their lowest and the light is superb for photography. Outside the Christmas and New Year window, this is where the value sits.

What actually drives the timing

The migration moves all year and it does not follow a fixed map: the southern Ndutu plains for calving from January, the central Seronera through the green months, and the northern Mara from July to October. Camp zone follows the herds, which is why your month matters more than your itinerary length.

How far ahead to book

June to August and the peak migration months want six to twelve months of notice for the camps worth choosing. Green-season departures usually go through on two to four months. Christmas, New Year and Easter compress early whatever the season.

How do we get the best price without hurting the trip?

The four levers that actually work: green season (published rates 20-40 percent below high season with fewer vehicles at sightings), four or more travelers splitting the vehicle, tented camps over permanent lodges of the same operator, and road transfers under two hours instead of flight sectors. What is the best month for this trip?

What vaccinations and malaria prevention do we need?

Yellow fever certificates are required when arriving from endemic countries; malaria prophylaxis is recommended for most safari regions - discuss options with your doctor 6 to 8 weeks out. We brief every trip on water, sun and insect precautions.

CDC guidance for the region is precise. Yellow fever vaccination is recommended for all safari travelers and becomes a certificate requirement when you arrive via an endemic country such as Rwanda or Uganda. Routine boosters, hepatitis A, typhoid and hepatitis B round out the travel clinic list. Malaria transmission covers every game park below roughly 2,500 meters, the parasite is chloroquine resistant, and the preventatives that work are atovaquone-proguanil, doxycycline, mefloquine or tafenoquine, started before arrival and finished after. Book the clinic six to eight weeks out, and pack repellent with 20 percent DEET plus long sleeves for dusk.

What photography gear should we bring?

A 100 to 400mm zoom covers 90 percent of sightings; add a wide second body for landscapes. Beanbags beat tripods in vehicles. We provide charging setups and dust management briefings - tell us your kit and targets.

How do internal flights and luggage limits work?

Bush flights allow 15 to 20kg in soft duffels; lodges store hard cases free. We route connections with realistic margins and meet you at every airstrip - missed-connection cover is built into fly-in trips.
