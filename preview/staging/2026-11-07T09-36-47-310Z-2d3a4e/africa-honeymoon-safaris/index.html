What a honeymoon safari is

Wildlife, privacy and a beach finish in one journey - built around the two of you rather than around a fixed departure.

A honeymoon safari in Africa combines wildlife, privacy, adventure and relaxation in a single trip. Instead of choosing between a safari and a beach holiday, couples can bring together private game drives, secluded safari suites, bush dinners, private plunge pools, sundowners, star beds and Indian Ocean beaches in one carefully paced journey.

For many couples the right shape is simple: start in the bush, finish beside the ocean. That might mean several days watching lions in the Serengeti, a private dinner under the stars in the Maasai Mara or a boat through the waterways of Botswana, followed by a flight to Zanzibar, Diani Beach, Mozambique, Mauritius or the Seychelles for a few days where nothing has to be done.

A well-designed honeymoon safari is not about seeing as many places as possible. It is about choosing the right combination of wildlife, accommodation, privacy and experiences, then leaving enough time to enjoy each of them. A route with four excellent bases will usually beat a route with seven rushed ones, and on a honeymoon the difference is felt every single day. First safari guide → All safari packages →

What makes a honeymoon safari different

A standard safari is built around wildlife viewing. A honeymoon has to balance wildlife with privacy, romance, relaxation and flexibility.

That balance changes the way an itinerary is constructed. Rather than changing camps every night, couples can stay two or three nights in one exceptional property. Rather than sharing every game drive, a private vehicle gives the couple control over the pace. Rather than eating in the restaurant every evening, selected nights can be private bush dinners, sundowners on a ridge or an intimate table set somewhere in the bush.

The upgrades that actually change the trip

The most valuable honeymoon upgrades are not always the most expensive room. A private vehicle, a secluded camp, one extra night in the right wildlife area and a direct flight to the beach can make more difference to how the week feels than moving every room category up by one grade.

That is worth knowing before the quotes arrive, because it lets a couple spend deliberately rather than spending uniformly. The list below is roughly the order in which these choices tend to earn their keep.

  • A private vehicle - the single biggest change to the daily rhythm, and the one most couples notice first
  • A secluded property - privacy at camp matters more than square footage in the room
  • Two or three nights in one place - fewer unpackings, slower mornings, better light for photography
  • A beach block that is long enough - three nights is a minimum; four or five lets the safari actually settle
  • One or two signature experiences - a bush dinner, a balloon flight or a dhow sunset rather than a full programme
  • A direct flight where one exists - an extra connection costs more than money on a honeymoon

Couples should also decide early whether they are buying a safari or buying a honeymoon with some safari in it. Both are valid; they just build different routes. Private African safaris → Luxury African safaris →

Romantic safari experiences for couples

Six experiences account for most of what couples actually remember. Pick several, not all of them.

Private game drives

A private safari vehicle gives couples control over their days. There is no need to leave a sighting because another guest wants to return to camp, and the guide can adapt the day around photography, wildlife interests, a picnic breakfast or a long sunset drive. For honeymooners, private guiding also builds a more personal relationship between the couple and their guide, which changes the tone of the whole week rather than just the logistics of it.

Bush dinners under the stars

A private bush dinner is one of the classic honeymoon experiences. Instead of dining only at the lodge restaurant, a camp may arrange a table in a carefully chosen wilderness setting, often with lanterns, a fire and a tailored menu. Depending on the property and local regulations, the setting might be:

  • An open savannah viewpoint
  • A dry riverbed
  • A private lodge terrace
  • A secluded bush clearing
  • A deck overlooking a waterhole
  • A private beach after the safari

Bush dinners are not automatically available at every lodge, so they should be requested when the itinerary is being designed rather than assumed once you arrive.

Private plunge pools

A private plunge pool turns a safari suite into part of the honeymoon rather than somewhere to sleep. After an early game drive, couples can return to the room, have breakfast, swim, read or simply watch the light change before the afternoon safari. Look for:

  • Private plunge pools
  • Private verandas
  • Wildlife or landscape views
  • Outdoor showers
  • Outdoor baths
  • Private dining areas
  • Secluded suites

Private pools are also available at selected luxury beach properties, which makes it possible to keep the same level of privacy after leaving the safari.

Sundowners for two

A sundowner is the simplest romantic experience to include. The vehicle stops before sunset, drinks and snacks appear, and the couple watches the landscape change as the sun goes. The experience varies dramatically by destination: a Serengeti sundowner looks across open grassland, an Okavango sundowner sits beside channels and floodplains, and in Zanzibar the same idea continues on the water with a private sunset dhow cruise.

Star beds and sleeping under the sky

For couples who want something more adventurous, a star bed gives a night under the African sky without giving up the comfort and service of a safari camp. This is available at selected properties and should be checked against the specific camp and season, because availability and safety rules vary considerably.

Hot-air balloon safaris

A sunrise balloon flight gives an entirely different perspective of the landscape. It is particularly associated with the Serengeti and the Maasai Mara, where flights provide aerial views of wildlife and the surrounding plains. A balloon safari can be followed by a bush breakfast, depending on the operator and location. Hot-air balloon safaris → Game drives →

Honeymoon safari destinations in Africa

Africa offers very different honeymoon experiences depending on the country - and the right choice has more to do with the two of you than with the wildlife list.

Some destinations focus on classic Big Five viewing, others on remote wilderness, desert landscapes, primates, or simply how easily the safari connects to a beach. The nine countries below cover almost every realistic honeymoon brief, and the sections that follow set out what each one is genuinely good at.

  • Tanzania - the clearest bush-to-beach contrast, with Zanzibar a short flight from the Serengeti
  • Kenya - the Maasai Mara, private conservancies and a direct coast link to Diani
  • Botswana - water-based wilderness, small camps and very few other vehicles
  • South Africa - safari plus city, wine and coast, with the fewest internal flights
  • Namibia - desert scenery, road-trip freedom and exceptional night skies
  • Zambia - walking, canoeing and river lodges for active couples
  • Zimbabwe - Victoria Falls as a dramatic finale alongside Hwange and Mana Pools
  • Rwanda - gorilla trekking, forest lodges and Lake Kivu
  • Uganda - gorillas, chimpanzees, boat safaris and lakes in one country

Four honeymoon safari shapes

Before choosing a country, decide which shape the trip should take. The shape usually matters more than the map.

Bush to beach

Safari first, ocean second. This is the most popular structure and the easiest to pace: several demanding mornings in the wilderness, then three to five nights where there is nowhere in particular to be. Tanzania with Zanzibar, Kenya with Diani and South Africa with Mozambique all follow this pattern.

Wilderness and water

The Okavango Delta, the Chobe riverfront and Victoria Falls replace the beach with water of a different kind. Mokoro channels, boat safaris and elephants at the river give a honeymoon that feels remote rather than resort-like. Boat safaris →

Active and adventurous

South Luangwa and the Lower Zambezi, Mana Pools, walking safaris, canoeing and, for some couples, gorilla or chimpanzee trekking. This shape suits couples who would rather earn the view than be driven to it. Walking safaris →

Desert and sky

Namibia offers dunes at Sossusvlei, the gravel plains of Damaraland, Etosha's waterholes and some of the darkest skies on the continent. It is the least conventional honeymoon and frequently the most memorable. Sossusvlei → Etosha →

Tanzania honeymoon safari

Particularly well suited to couples who want safari and beach in the same honeymoon.

Safari and beach honeymoons in Africa

For many couples the ideal honeymoon has two clear chapters: bush first, beach second.

The safari provides adventure, wildlife and shared experiences. The beach provides several days where there is nowhere else to go and nothing that has to be done. The order matters: putting the beach first tends to make the safari feel like work by comparison, whereas ending in the water gives the trip a natural decompression.

That said, either order can work depending on flights and travel dates, and some couples deliberately start slowly, go hard in the middle and finish quietly.

The combinations that work best

  • Tanzania and Zanzibar - Serengeti → Ngorongoro → Zanzibar. One of the most natural safari-and-beach pairings in Africa.
  • Kenya and Diani - Maasai Mara → Diani Beach. A classic bush-and-beach structure with a short final flight.
  • South Africa and Mozambique - Greater Kruger → Mozambique. Big Five safari, then Indian Ocean.
  • South Africa and Mauritius - Cape Town → Winelands → Greater Kruger → Mauritius. City, wine, wildlife and beach in one trip.
  • Botswana and Victoria Falls - Okavango Delta → Chobe → Victoria Falls. A bush-and-waterfall honeymoon rather than a beach one.
  • Zambia and Mozambique - South Luangwa → Lower Zambezi → Mozambique. Active safari, then an ocean finale.
  • Kenya and Zanzibar - Maasai Mara → Zanzibar. An alternative for couples who prefer an island to the Kenyan coast.

Where to finish: the beach options

Six finishes, each with a different character. Two of them are not beaches at all.

Zanzibar

The default for East African honeymoons and for good reason: it is a short flight from the Serengeti, the water is warm, and the island offers private villas, swimming, diving, snorkelling, sunset dhow cruises, spa treatments, Stone Town and private beach dinners. The north and east coasts are quieter than the west, and the best properties are usually a little removed from the main road.

Diani Beach

The most geographically direct Kenyan coast extension after the Maasai Mara. Reef, long sand and enough infrastructure to make the last three nights effortless.

Mozambique

Indian Ocean beaches with a slower, less developed feel than the better-known islands. Pairs well with Greater Kruger or the Lower Zambezi, and works best when the transfer is genuinely direct rather than routed through Johannesburg unnecessarily.

Mauritius and the Seychelles

The polished end of the spectrum: shorter flights from South Africa for Mauritius, longer ones for the Seychelles, and a level of service that makes the beach block feel like a proper hotel stay rather than an extension of the safari.

Lake Kivu and Lake Bunyonyi

Neither is the ocean, and both are better for some couples. Rwanda's Lake Kivu and Uganda's Lake Bunyonyi give a quiet, green, inland finish that costs no extra flights and keeps the honeymoon in the region where the wildlife was.

How long should a honeymoon safari be?

Duration should be set by the rhythm you want, not by the number of stamps in the passport.

5 to 7 days

Suitable for one safari destination: a short Maasai Mara honeymoon, a short Kruger honeymoon, a short Botswana safari or a gorilla-focused Rwanda itinerary. Workable when the wedding and travel dates are tight, and better than stretching a single destination across ten days with nothing new to see.

8 to 10 days

Useful for Tanzania plus Zanzibar, Kenya plus Diani, Cape Town plus Kruger, Victoria Falls plus Hwange, or Rwanda plus Lake Kivu. This is the sweet spot for most honeymoons because it fits a two-week window with travel time on either side.

11 to 14 days

Better for two or three safari regions, safari plus beach, safari plus Cape Town, Botswana plus Victoria Falls, or Kenya conservancy plus beach. Long enough that the itinerary can include a rest day without losing anything.

14 days and longer

Worth it when combining multiple countries, safari plus beach plus city, Kilimanjaro plus safari plus Zanzibar, Namibia plus Botswana, Zambia plus Zimbabwe plus beach, or gorilla trekking plus a traditional safari.

The important principle is not to overpack the itinerary. A honeymoon should have time for the couple to enjoy the accommodation and the destination rather than spending every day travelling between them. How many days is a safari → When to travel →

How to choose honeymoon safari accommodation

Do not choose a property because it is described as luxury. Look at what the couple will actually experience.

Star ratings describe the fixtures. They do not describe whether the suite is secluded, whether the plunge pool is genuinely private, whether the guide will take you back early, or whether the chef will lay a table in the bush at nine in the evening. Those are the things a honeymoon actually turns on.

The questions worth asking

  • Privacy - is the suite secluded, and how close are the neighbouring rooms?
  • Views - does it overlook wildlife, forest, river, ocean or open landscape?
  • Private pool - is the plunge pool genuinely private, or shared with the next suite?
  • Outdoor bathroom - is there an outdoor shower or bath, and is it screened?
  • Dining - can private meals be arranged, and where would they be set?
  • Safari activities - are game drives private or shared, and can they be timed to you?
  • Location - how far is the property from the main wildlife areas?
  • Beach location - for Zanzibar, Diani or another coastal extension, is it directly on the beach?
  • Transfers - how long will it take to reach the lodge or beach after landing?

These details matter more to honeymooners than the star classification alone, and most of them can be answered with a single email before anything is booked. Where to stay →

Honeymoon accommodation types

Five property styles cover almost every honeymoon, and they behave very differently once you are in them.

Lodge

A permanent building with a restaurant, a bar and usually a pool. Lodges are the most predictable option and the easiest for couples who want service, a proper kitchen and reliable wifi. The trade-off is that the larger the lodge, the less private the experience tends to feel.

Tented camp

Canvas suites on platforms, often in the best positions in the wildlife area. Luxury tented camps offer the same beds and bathrooms as a lodge but sit closer to the sounds of the bush, which is exactly the point for most honeymooners. Seasonal tented camps move with the wildlife and are at their best during the migration.

Private villa or exclusive-use property

Multiple bedrooms, a private guide, a private vehicle, a chef and a schedule that belongs entirely to you. This is the strongest option for couples who want total control, and it is often better value than it first appears when two couples travel together.

Star bed

A raised sleeping platform in the open, with a mosquito net, a lantern and usually a guide within call. It is the most adventurous option and works best as one night inside a comfortable camp rather than as the only accommodation on the trip.

Beach villa

The finish, not the safari. Look for a private plunge pool, a deck and a stretch of sand that is not shared with a beach bar - the same privacy criteria apply as they do in the bush, and the transfer time from the airport matters more than the room size. Private safari vehicle vs shared safari

For a honeymoon, a private vehicle is usually worth considering before anything else on the list.

The case is straightforward. A shared vehicle runs on somebody else's timetable and somebody else's interests, which is fine on a normal safari and quietly corrosive on a honeymoon. With a private vehicle the couple controls the day.

  • Game-drive times are more flexible
  • The couple does not share the vehicle with strangers
  • Photography can be tailored to their interests
  • Longer wildlife sightings are easier to accommodate
  • Picnic breakfasts can be arranged around the couple
  • The guide can adjust the pace without negotiating
  • Special occasions can be incorporated more easily

What it does not require

A private safari does not necessarily require the most expensive lodge. Couples can prioritise a private vehicle while choosing a comfortable mid-range or premium property, and that trade tends to improve the trip far more than moving up a room category does. Where the budget is fixed, the vehicle is usually the better place to spend it. Private African safaris → Private safari in Tanzania → Private safari in Kenya →

Planning a romantic safari without overdoing it

A honeymoon does not need a romantic moment every few hours. It needs several that genuinely count.

The commonest mistake is treating the trip like a checklist: a bush dinner, then a balloon, then a spa, then a private dinner, then a dhow. By day six the couple is scheduling romance, which rather defeats the purpose. Choose four or five experiences across two weeks and leave the rest of the calendar alone.

A sample fortnight might look like this:

  • Day 2 - private bush dinner
  • Day 4 - balloon safari at dawn
  • Day 6 - a deliberately empty lodge day
  • Day 8 - fly to Zanzibar
  • Day 9 - beach and private pool
  • Day 10 - sunset dhow cruise
  • Day 11 - a free beach day

The rest of the time can simply be spent together. What actually makes a honeymoon safari feel romantic is not the number of arranged experiences - it is unhurried mornings, a vehicle with nobody else in it, and an afternoon with nothing scheduled at all.

Honeymoon safari itinerary design

A strong honeymoon itinerary follows a rhythm rather than simply listing destinations.

The rhythm alternates intensity with space, and it ends on the quietest note. In practice a well-built fortnight moves through five phases:

  • Adventure - early game drives, wildlife encounters, walking or trekking
  • Romance - sundowners, private dinners and a special room
  • Relaxation - a late breakfast, pool time, a spa treatment or a free afternoon
  • Adventure - another game drive or a signature activity
  • Beach - several uninterrupted nights beside the ocean

For a Tanzania honeymoon the final transition reads: Serengeti → Zanzibar → beach villa → sunset dhow → free beach day. That change of pace is not decoration - it is the part of the trip where the safari actually lands.

Three design rules

  • Anchor the fixed points first. Fix the international flights, the beach block and anything with an age or permit limit, then build the safari around them.
  • Never schedule two demanding days back to back. A dawn balloon on the day after a long transfer is a mistake even when it looks efficient on paper.
  • Leave one full day unscheduled. The day nobody plans is usually the one the couple remembers. → When to travel

Three budget levels

Same itinerary, three different ways of spending against it.

Comfort-focused honeymoon

Mid-range safari lodges, a private vehicle, one or two selected romantic experiences and quality beach accommodation. This tier usually produces the best honeymoon per dollar, because the private vehicle and a good beach hotel deliver more than a marginal room upgrade would.

Luxury honeymoon

Luxury tented camps throughout, a private vehicle, a plunge-pool suite, a bush dinner and a premium beach villa. This is the tier most couples picture when they start planning, and it is where the route is usually trimmed to four bases to afford it.

Ultra-luxury honeymoon

Private concession access, a luxury suite, charter flights between camps, private dining every night, a plunge pool and an exclusive beach villa. The difference at this level is space and exclusivity rather than a longer activity list.

All three are fully customizable around the couple's priorities and budget, and a good proposal will show the same route priced at two or three levels so the trade-offs are visible. Luxury African safaris → Budget African safaris →

Best time for an African honeymoon safari

There is no single best month for every African honeymoon destination - and the safari half and the beach half rarely peak at the same time.

The right travel period depends on wildlife expectations, the safari region, the beach destination, rainfall, accommodation availability, budget and any special activity with a fixed season. That is why the starting question should be the wedding date rather than the country.

Tanzania and Zanzibar

The safari and beach portions should be considered separately, because the conditions that produce the best wildlife do not always coincide with the best beach weather. The dry months concentrate animals around water; the shoulder months bring fewer vehicles and greener scenery.

Kenya and Diani

The Maasai Mara and the Kenyan coast have different seasonal patterns, so both halves should be checked when choosing dates. The migration in the Mara moves through the year rather than sitting in one place, and the coast is warm and humid through most of it.

Botswana

The Okavango changes substantially through the year. Floodwaters arrive later than the rains, and different water levels produce genuinely different safaris rather than a better or worse version of the same one.

South Africa

Regional climate diversity makes year-round itinerary planning possible, although wildlife conditions in the Lowveld differ considerably between wet and dry periods. The winter dry season is the classic wildlife window; summer is greener, hotter and better value.

Namibia

The dry season is particularly useful for wildlife viewing around Etosha, while the desert landscapes remain attractive throughout the year. Summer heat in the desert is real and should be planned around rather than ignored.

Rwanda and Uganda

Trekking conditions should be considered alongside general safari weather. Forest trails are harder after heavy rain, and permits are limited whatever the month. Best time for an African safari → January migration safaris → March migration safaris →

Responsible honeymoon safaris

A romantic safari should still respect the place it is romantic in.

The luxury of a honeymoon does not need to come at the expense of the environment or the communities around the park. These are the practical points worth raising with an operator before booking:

  • Wildlife viewing distances and how consistently they are followed
  • Lodge conservation policies and energy or water use
  • Community partnerships and whether benefits reach local households
  • Local guides and how much of the guiding is done by residents
  • Waste reduction, plastic reduction and how rubbish leaves the camp
  • Ethical wildlife interactions - no feeding, no touching, no baiting
  • Conservation fees and where the money actually goes
  • Locally owned tourism businesses where a choice exists

Couples who ask these questions tend to end up at better camps, because the properties that answer them clearly are usually the ones that run well. Safari safety →

Flights, transfers and paperwork

The least romantic part of the planning, and the part most likely to undo a good itinerary.

Arrival and internal flights

Most honeymoons start with an international arrival in Nairobi, Dar es Salaam, Kilimanjaro, Entebbe, Kigali, Windhoek or Johannesburg, followed by an internal flight to the first safari base. Schedules are limited, so the international flight should be chosen first and the internal one built around it rather than the reverse.

Charters and road transfers

Remote camps are reached by light-aircraft charter, and charters often fly only on certain days of the week. Road transfers of four hours or more are worth replacing with a flight on a honeymoon, because the day spent driving is a day of the safari gone.

Visas, insurance and health

Visa requirements differ by passport and change often, so they should be checked against current official advice rather than a blog post. Travel insurance with medical evacuation cover is essential for remote camps, and any vaccination or medication question belongs with a travel clinic rather than an operator. Honeymoon safari FAQs

Is Africa good for a honeymoon?

Yes. Africa can combine wildlife, private accommodation, adventure, fine dining, wilderness experiences and beach destinations within one journey, which is exactly what most couples are looking for.

How many days do you need for a honeymoon safari?

Around 8 to 14 days works well for most safari-and-beach itineraries. Shorter single-destination safaris and longer multi-country honeymoons are both possible.

Is Tanzania good for a honeymoon?

Yes. Tanzania combines Serengeti, Ngorongoro and Tarangire with Zanzibar's beaches, so safari and beach sit inside a single itinerary without a long transfer between them.

Can we combine a Tanzania safari with Zanzibar?

Yes, and it is one of the most natural pairings in Africa. The typical route is Arusha, Tarangire, Ngorongoro, Serengeti and then Zanzibar, usually by a short domestic flight.

Is Zanzibar good for a honeymoon?

Zanzibar provides several days of beach relaxation after a safari, with private villas, diving, snorkelling, sunset dhow cruises, spa treatments, Stone Town and private dining all available.

Should we do the safari or the beach first?

Many couples prefer safari first and beach second, because the beach becomes a natural period of rest after early mornings. Either order can work depending on flights and dates.

Can we have a private safari vehicle?

Yes. Private vehicles can be built into honeymoon itineraries in most safari destinations and give far more flexibility and privacy than a shared game drive.

Can we have a private bush dinner?

Often, depending on the lodge, camp, location and local regulations. Ask for it during itinerary planning rather than assuming it will be available on arrival.

Which African safaris have private plunge pools?

Selected camps and lodges in Tanzania, Kenya, Botswana, South Africa, Zambia and Namibia offer them, along with a number of Zanzibar and Indian Ocean beach properties.

Can we combine Kenya with Zanzibar?

Yes, although Diani is the more geographically direct Kenyan coast extension after the Maasai Mara. Zanzibar adds a flight and a different character.

Is a luxury safari necessary for a honeymoon?

No. A couple can prioritise a private vehicle, intimate accommodation, a special bush dinner and a quality beach stay without booking the highest category everywhere.

What is the most romantic safari experience?

It depends on the couple. Some prefer a private bush dinner, others a plunge-pool suite, a balloon flight, a star bed, a walking safari, a sunset boat trip or several quiet days in Zanzibar.

What is a good honeymoon safari budget?

It is driven mainly by season, accommodation category, private versus shared vehicle, internal flights and length. Decide where you want to spend more before asking for quotes.

As a working number, honeymoon safaris start near US$600 per person per day for strong mid-range and pass US$1,200 for private-concession luxury; seven to ten nights plus a Zanzibar finish typically total US$7,000-18,000 per couple depending on flights. The two decisions that move the total most are season and whether the internal flights are scheduled or private charters.

How far in advance should we book?

Earlier than a normal trip. The best honeymoon suites and balloon flights are limited, and peak-season camps can be held a long way ahead.

Which countries are best for a first safari honeymoon?

Tanzania and Kenya for classic wildlife plus a beach, South Africa for safari with city and wine, and Botswana for remote wilderness with very few other vehicles.

Can we include gorilla trekking?

Yes. Rwanda and Uganda both combine gorilla trekking with chimpanzees, traditional safari and lakeside time, but permits are limited and should be secured first.

For honeymoon routing the practical choice is Uganda: one permit line instead of Rwanda's premium, and Bwindi pairs with Queen Elizabeth and the coast without border-jumping. Timewise, put the trek mid-honeymoon (days 4-6) rather than at the end - if a transfer slips, the beach absorbs it instead of the flight home.

Are hot-air balloon safaris worth it?

For couples who value the aerial perspective, usually yes. They are most associated with the Serengeti and the Maasai Mara and are often followed by a bush breakfast.

Do we need a visa for Tanzania or Kenya?

Requirements depend on your passport and change regularly, so check current official advice before booking flights. Most nationalities need an eTA or eVisa of some kind.

Two different systems. Kenya replaced visas with the eTA on 1 January 2024: US$32.50, filed online at least three days before departure, with visa-free entry for South Africa, Singapore, Ethiopia, Namibia, Botswana and most African passports. Tanzania still sells visas on arrival - US$50, US$100 for United States passports - runs its own eVisa, and exempts Kenya, Rwanda, Uganda and the SADC block for 90 days. A Kenya-Tanzania circuit needs both authorisations, because the East African Tourist Visa covers Kenya with Uganda and Rwanda but not Tanzania. These rules moved twice in two years, so recheck your nationality before every booking.

What is the best time of year for a honeymoon safari?

There is no single best month for every destination, and the safari and beach halves rarely peak together. Start with the wedding date, then choose the region.

Is a honeymoon safari safe?

With a licensed operator and sensible preparation it is a well-established form of travel. Camps brief guests on the basics and the rules in the bush are straightforward.

Can we take children on a honeymoon itinerary?

The itinerary itself is flexible, but many honeymoon properties and some camps have minimum ages for privacy reasons. Tell us at the start if the trip is not just the two of you.

What should we pack?

Neutral lightweight clothing and a warm layer for dawn drives, plus swimwear, one smart-casual outfit for dinner and hiking shoes if trekking is on the route.

Can we fly between the safari and the beach?

Usually, and it is worth it. A direct flight replaces a long road day and keeps the transition between the two halves of the honeymoon short.

Can we add Victoria Falls?

Yes. Botswana, Zambia and Zimbabwe itineraries can all end at Victoria Falls, which works well as a dramatic finale where a beach finish does not suit you.
