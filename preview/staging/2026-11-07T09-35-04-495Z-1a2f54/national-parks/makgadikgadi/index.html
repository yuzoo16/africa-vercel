Makgadikgadi Pans

Botswana's great salt pans: migration zebra, flamingos and baobabs.

The Makgadikgadi Pans are among the most extraordinary landscapes in Botswana: an immense network of salt pans, grasslands, seasonal wetlands, fossil lake margins, sand dunes and baobab-studded islands occupying part of the Kalahari Basin. Rather than being a single park, Makgadikgadi is a broader landscape containing several protected areas and wildlife destinations, including Makgadikgadi Pans Game Reserve, Nxai Pan National Park and Nata Sanctuary. The wider Makgadikgadi area covers roughly 12,000 km², while the larger Makgadikgadi Pans landscape identified by UNESCO's tentative-list documentation extends across more than 30,000 km².

The defining feature is its extraordinary geological history. The pans are remnants of a much larger prehistoric lake system, and today the enormous salt surfaces can appear almost completely lifeless for much of the year. After sufficient rains, however, the landscape can transform dramatically as water reaches the pans, grasslands become productive, zebra and wildebeest move through the region, and flamingos gather in spectacular numbers.

This is a destination for travelers who want something very different from the waterways of the Okavango Delta or the elephant-rich Chobe Riverfront.

Best for: dramatic landscapes, zebra and wildebeest migration, flamingos, baobabs, photography, Kalahari wilderness, cultural and geological experiences, and travelers looking for a less conventional Botswana safari.

What makes Makgadikgadi different?

Makgadikgadi should not be approached simply as another wildlife park.

Its strongest attraction is the relationship between water, salt, grassland and wildlife.

For much of the year, the pans are intensely dry. Their vast white or pale-grey surfaces create a landscape with almost no visual scale: a person standing on the horizon can appear tiny against an enormous empty expanse.

Then the rains arrive.

Temporary water transforms selected pans and surrounding grasslands. Wildlife responds to the changing conditions, and areas that appeared almost deserted can become highly productive.

Botswana Tourism describes the Makgadikgadi as an area of approximately 12,000 km², part of the Kalahari Basin, and home to some of the world's largest salt pans.

The result is one of Botswana's most dramatic seasonal ecosystems.

The Makgadikgadi Pans landscape

The word Makgadikgadi does not refer to one enormous continuous salt flat.

It is a complex of pans, including the two largest: Ntwetwe Pan and Sowa Pan.

Other pans and associated areas include:

  • Nxai Pan
  • Kudiakam Pan
  • Kaucaca Pan
  • Numerous smaller pans and depressions

Between the pans are grasslands, sand dunes, rocky islands, desert terrain and woodland.

The wider landscape is also associated with the Boteti River, which provides an important ecological connection toward the western side of the Makgadikgadi system, while the ephemeral Nata River supplies water from the east in suitable years. UNESCO identifies these hydrological relationships as important components of the wider Makgadikgadi landscape.

Ntwetwe Pan

Ntwetwe Pan is one of the two great central salt pans of Makgadikgadi.

Its enormous scale is difficult to appreciate from photographs.

When dry, its salt-covered surface creates the impression of an almost endless white or pale-grey plain. Vegetation is largely absent from the most saline surfaces, while grasslands develop around the margins.

During sufficiently wet periods, the character changes dramatically.

The pan can hold shallow water, while surrounding grasslands become increasingly productive.

For photographers, the combination of enormous horizons, salt crust, reflections, clouds, sunrise, sunset and extreme minimalism can produce some of Botswana's most distinctive landscape images.

Sowa Pan

To the east lies Sowa Pan, another enormous salt depression and one of the most important seasonal destinations for waterbirds.

When conditions are right, Sowa becomes particularly important for flamingos.

Botswana Tourism reports that flamingo numbers across the Makgadikgadi system can reach tens of thousands and, in exceptional circumstances, hundreds of thousands.

This is one of the reasons Makgadikgadi can be spectacular for birdwatchers.

The experience, however, is highly dependent on rainfall and water conditions. A traveler visiting in a dry year should not expect the same spectacle as someone arriving after exceptional rains.

Nxai Pan National Park

Nxai Pan National Park forms part of the greater Makgadikgadi complex but should be treated as a distinct destination within it.

Botswana Tourism gives the park an area of approximately 2,100 km² and identifies Nxai Pan, Kgama-Kgama Pan and Kudiakam Pan as major pans within the park. These were once ancient salt lakes but are now largely grassed and dotted with acacia islands.

This makes Nxai Pan much more suitable for conventional game viewing than the bare salt surfaces of the central pans.

Wildlife can include:

  • Zebra
  • Wildebeest
  • Springbok
  • Impala
  • Gemsbok
  • Hartebeest
  • Giraffe
  • Lion
  • Cheetah
  • African wild dog
  • Brown hyena
  • Bat-eared fox
  • Elephant in some circumstances
  • Buffalo in some circumstances

Botswana Tourism notes that wildlife viewing in Nxai Pan is strongly seasonal and particularly dependent on rainfall.

Makgadikgadi Pans Game Reserve

The Makgadikgadi Pans Game Reserve is another distinct protected area within the broader landscape.

Botswana Tourism gives the reserve an area of approximately 3,900 km². It incorporates the western end of Ntwetwe Pan and contains extensive grasslands and acacia woodland.

The reserve is particularly important during the wet season, when zebra and wildebeest begin moving toward the Boteti region.

Other wildlife includes gemsbok, eland, red hartebeest, kudu, bushbuck, duiker, giraffe, springbok, steenbok, elephant and brown hyena.

Predators follow the herbivore concentrations, creating opportunities for lion and other carnivore sightings.

The zebra and wildebeest migration

One of the major reasons to visit the Makgadikgadi region is its seasonal movement of zebra and wildebeest.

This is not simply a smaller version of the Serengeti-Mara migration.

The animals respond to rainfall, grass and the availability of water across the wider Kalahari landscape.

Botswana Tourism notes that the wet season brings large herds of zebra and wildebeest into the Makgadikgadi Pans Game Reserve as they begin moving westward toward the Boteti region.

UNESCO's tentative-list documentation describes the wider Makgadikgadi landscape as supporting one of the world's notable zebra-wildebeest migrations, including movements associated with the Boteti River and Chobe region.

This makes timing particularly important.

A traveler visiting primarily for migration should plan around rainfall patterns and recent wildlife conditions rather than assuming that a specific calendar date guarantees the herds will be in one location.

Flamingos of Makgadikgadi

Flamingos are among Makgadikgadi's most spectacular seasonal attractions.

When suitable shallow water and food conditions develop, greater and lesser flamingos can gather in extraordinary numbers.

The wider Makgadikgadi landscape is recognized by UNESCO as an important flamingo breeding and feeding area.

This is one reason the region is particularly valuable to birdwatchers.

The sight of thousands of flamingos against a vast salt-pan horizon is fundamentally different from a conventional safari experience.

However, flamingo conditions can be highly unpredictable.

Rainfall determines whether water reaches the appropriate pans, while breeding and feeding conditions influence where birds concentrate.

Wildlife in Makgadikgadi

Zebra

Zebra are among the most important large herbivores in the Makgadikgadi system.

Seasonal movements can bring large numbers into productive grasslands.

Their presence also influences predator activity.

Wildebeest

Wildebeest move through the region with seasonal changes in grass and water availability.

They are particularly important to the wider migration system associated with the Boteti and Makgadikgadi.

Gemsbok

Gemsbok are perfectly adapted to the Kalahari environment and can survive in dry conditions.

They are one of the species that give the Makgadikgadi landscape its distinctly arid character.

Springbok

Springbok can be seen across suitable grassland areas, particularly where rainfall creates productive grazing.

Giraffe

Giraffe occur in the woodland and scrub areas around the pans rather than on the most barren salt surfaces.

Eland

Eland are another important large antelope associated with the broader landscape.

Predators

Predators may include lion, cheetah, brown hyena, spotted hyena, African wild dog and jackal.

Predator viewing depends heavily on the movement of prey and seasonal conditions.

This is particularly important when planning a safari: Makgadikgadi is not a destination where every wildlife species is equally visible throughout the year.

Brown hyena

One of the most interesting species to look for is the brown hyena.

Brown hyenas are particularly well adapted to the dry environments of southern Africa and are much less frequently encountered than spotted hyenas.

Botswana Tourism specifically mentions brown hyena among the species that patient wildlife viewers may encounter in the Makgadikgadi Pans Game Reserve.

Because they are elusive and often active at night, sightings are never guaranteed.

Meerkats

Meerkats are one of the region's most popular smaller mammals.

Some specialist safari experiences around the Makgadikgadi landscape offer guided encounters with habituated meerkat groups.

These experiences should be approached responsibly, with animal welfare and natural behavior taking priority over close-up photographs.

Makgadikgadi for birdwatching

Makgadikgadi is particularly valuable for birding when seasonal water is present.

Potential highlights include:

  • Greater flamingo
  • Lesser flamingo
  • Pelicans
  • Ducks
  • Geese
  • Raptors
  • Ostrich
  • Kori bustard
  • Other grassland and wetland species

Nata Sanctuary can be especially rewarding when water reaches its pans.

Botswana Tourism describes Nata as a community-based conservation initiative where flamingos, pelicans, ducks and geese can gather when water conditions are favorable.

Nata Sanctuary

Nata Sanctuary is not a national park or game reserve.

It is a community-based conservation area established by residents of Nata, Maphosa, Sepako and Manxotae.

Botswana Tourism identifies it as Botswana's first community-based conservation project and notes that tourism proceeds are shared among the four communities for development projects.

The sanctuary covers approximately 250 km² and combines grasslands and pans.

Its significance goes beyond wildlife.

It demonstrates how tourism can be connected to local communities while conserving habitat.

When the pans contain water, the bird spectacle can be exceptional.

Baines' Baobabs

One of the most recognizable landmarks in the Makgadikgadi region is Baines' Baobabs.

The site consists of seven enormous baobab trees overlooking Kudiakam Pan.

They were named after the 19th-century explorer and artist Thomas Baines, who painted them during his travels through the region.

Botswana Tourism notes that the view remains remarkably similar to the scene Baines recorded more than a century ago.

For photographers, sunrise and sunset are particularly attractive times to photograph the trees against the enormous open landscape.

The baobabs also provide a powerful connection between the modern safari experience and the historical exploration of southern Africa.

Chapman's Tree

The wider Makgadikgadi landscape is also associated with Chapman's Tree, a famous historic baobab.

Botswana Tourism notes that the tree served as an important landmark for travelers crossing the pans and is associated with the journeys of David Livingstone and other 19th-century explorers. The tree is believed to be several thousand years old.

These historic trees demonstrate how important isolated vegetation and natural landmarks were for people crossing an otherwise extraordinarily open landscape.

The geological history of Makgadikgadi

The modern salt pans are remnants of a much larger ancient lake.

UNESCO's tentative-list documentation describes the area as the remains of one of the world's former great inland seas, with the ancient lake system once extending over an enormous area.

The geological history explains the flatness and salinity of the landscape.

Over immense periods, changes in climate and drainage caused the ancient lake to contract.

What remained were vast depressions containing salts and sediments.

Today, these ancient lakebeds form the extraordinary Makgadikgadi landscape.

This geological story is one of the reasons Makgadikgadi should be considered more than a wildlife destination.

When is the best time to visit Makgadikgadi?

The answer depends strongly on what you want to see.

December to March: green season

Summer rainfall can transform the region.

Grasslands become productive and zebra and wildebeest movements can intensify.

Bird activity can also increase substantially.

This period can be excellent for migration, green landscapes, birding, flamingos where water conditions are suitable, dramatic thunderstorms and photography.

The downside is that rainfall can make some roads difficult and wildlife can spread over larger areas when water becomes available in multiple locations.

April to May: transitional season

The landscape begins drying after the rains.

Grass remains productive in many areas while roads may become easier than during the wettest periods.

This can be an attractive compromise for travelers seeking both wildlife and changing landscapes.

June to October: dry season

The dry season creates the stark Makgadikgadi landscapes most travelers imagine.

The salt pans become extremely dry and the surrounding grassland loses much of its green color.

Visibility can improve as vegetation becomes shorter.

Botswana Tourism generally identifies April through October as favorable months for weather and game viewing across Botswana, although the exact experience varies considerably by destination.

For the pans themselves, however, dry conditions are part of the attraction.

November: heat and transition

November can be extremely hot as the landscape waits for the rains.

The first storms may begin transforming the environment, but their timing is unpredictable.

This is an interesting period for travelers seeking dramatic weather and fewer visitors, but not necessarily the safest choice if a specific wildlife spectacle is the main reason for the trip.

Makgadikgadi in the wet season

The phrase wet season can be misleading because rainfall is highly variable.

A strong rainy season can transform the ecosystem.

An average or poor rainy season may produce much less dramatic flooding.

The most important point is that Makgadikgadi's wildlife and bird spectacles are rainfall-dependent.

This is why a responsible safari operator should avoid guaranteeing flamingo numbers, pan flooding, exact migration location or particular wildlife concentrations.

Instead, itineraries should be planned around seasonal probabilities.

Safari activities in Makgadikgadi

Game drives

Game drives are the main wildlife activity in the protected game areas.

They are particularly rewarding when zebra, wildebeest and other herbivores concentrate following rainfall.

Pan excursions

Exploring the salt pans themselves is one of the defining experiences.

The experience changes dramatically according to conditions.

A completely dry pan offers stark geological scenery.

A flooded pan becomes a temporary wetland.

Quad biking

Quad biking is offered in some Makgadikgadi areas and provides a very different way of experiencing the vast salt landscape.

It should be undertaken through responsible operators that respect wildlife and sensitive areas.

Meerkat experiences

Guided meerkat experiences can provide an opportunity to observe these social animals at close range.

The quality of the experience depends heavily on how the animals are habituated and managed.

Birding

Nata Sanctuary and flooded pan areas can be especially rewarding for birdwatchers.

Sundowners

The huge horizon makes Makgadikgadi particularly suitable for sunset experiences.

A sundowner here is not about overlooking a dense concentration of wildlife.

It is about standing on an enormous horizon as the light changes across the salt and grassland.

Stargazing

The region's low population density and limited artificial lighting create excellent conditions for night skies.

Makgadikgadi for photography

Few Botswana destinations offer such strong opportunities for minimalist photography.

The landscape naturally creates enormous negative space, strong horizons, silhouettes, reflections, salt textures, baobab compositions, dramatic clouds and wildlife against open backgrounds.

The same location can look completely different depending on rainfall.

During the dry season, think white salt, blue sky, long shadows and baobabs.

During the green season, think green grass, storm clouds, zebra, wildebeest and temporary wetlands.

With flooded pans, think reflections, flamingos and enormous skies.

This seasonal transformation is one of Makgadikgadi's greatest photographic strengths.

Makgadikgadi for families

Makgadikgadi can work exceptionally well for families, particularly when accommodation offers flexible activities.

Children can learn about prehistoric lakes, salt pans, desert adaptation, animal migration, tracking, stars, baobabs and San/Kalahari cultural history.

For younger children, travel distances and heat should be considered carefully.

A family itinerary should avoid trying to cover too much territory in a short period.

Makgadikgadi for honeymoons

Makgadikgadi offers a different type of honeymoon experience.

Rather than focusing exclusively on luxury suites and water-based activities, it offers remote landscapes, private camps, extraordinary sunsets, stargazing, private dinners, intimate wildlife experiences and enormous empty horizons.

A particularly strong honeymoon combination is the Okavango Delta and Makgadikgadi.

The Delta provides water and lush wildlife scenery, while Makgadikgadi provides dramatic salt-pan landscapes.

Makgadikgadi vs Central Kalahari

These destinations are sometimes grouped together because both are part of Botswana's broader Kalahari environment.

But they provide different experiences.

Makgadikgadi is dominated by salt pans with a strong geological identity, the zebra and wildebeest migration, flamingos in suitable conditions, Ntwetwe and Sowa Pans, Baines' Baobabs and extraordinary landscape photography.

The Central Kalahari is characterized by grassland, scrub and fossil valleys, a strong wilderness and predator identity, Kalahari predators and desert-adapted species, Deception and Passarge valleys, black-maned lions and wildlife photography.

For a longer Botswana itinerary, both can be worthwhile.

Makgadikgadi vs Okavango Delta

The contrast is even stronger.

The Okavango Delta is defined by permanent and seasonal wetlands, channels, lagoons, floodplains, mokoro, boat safaris and high habitat diversity.

Makgadikgadi is defined by salt pans, grasslands, ancient lakebeds, seasonal wetlands, zebra and wildebeest movement, flamingos, baobabs and vast open landscapes.

Travelers who want to understand the diversity of Botswana should consider combining the two.

Makgadikgadi vs Chobe

Chobe is stronger for travelers specifically seeking elephants, buffalo, riverfront game viewing, boat safaris and high concentrations of large mammals.

Makgadikgadi is stronger for salt-pan landscapes, zebra and wildebeest, flamingos, geological scenery, baobabs and solitude.

They serve very different safari purposes.

Access to Makgadikgadi

The region is relatively accessible compared with some of Botswana's more remote wilderness areas.

Botswana Tourism identifies Nata as an important crossroads for travelers heading toward Maun and Kasane, approximately 300 km from both Maun and Kasane.

Nxai Pan National Park is particularly accessible from the Nata to Maun road, with Botswana Tourism placing the park approximately 50 km from the road.

This makes Makgadikgadi and Nxai Pan useful components of an overland Botswana itinerary.

Depending on accommodation and itinerary, travelers can also use air access to safari camps.

Conservation and community importance

Makgadikgadi is not only a wilderness destination.

People have occupied the broader region for thousands of years.

Botswana Tourism notes evidence of human occupation dating back to the Stone Age, while UNESCO documents archaeological evidence of long-term human habitation and traditional land use around the pans.

Nata Sanctuary provides an important modern example of community-based conservation.

The sanctuary was established through cooperation between local communities and conservation interests, with tourism income supporting the participating communities.

This makes community involvement an important part of the region's conservation story.

Responsible tourism in Makgadikgadi

The pans are environmentally sensitive.

Visitors should:

  • Stay on designated routes where required
  • Avoid driving across fragile salt-pan surfaces unnecessarily
  • Never approach wildlife aggressively for photographs
  • Avoid feeding animals
  • Respect bird breeding areas
  • Follow local guide instructions
  • Support community-owned conservation initiatives
  • Avoid littering
  • Use water responsibly

The most important principle is simple: the landscape should remain more important than the photograph.

Makgadikgadi Safari FAQs

What are the Makgadikgadi Pans?

The Makgadikgadi Pans are a vast system of salt pans and surrounding grasslands, dunes, islands and seasonal wetlands in Botswana's Kalahari Basin.

The two largest pans are Ntwetwe and Sowa, surrounded by numerous smaller pans. They are remnants of a much larger prehistoric lake system.

The term Makgadikgadi therefore describes a broader landscape rather than one individual protected area.

Are Makgadikgadi Pans a national park?

Not exactly.

Makgadikgadi describes a broader geographical and ecological landscape containing several different protected areas and tourism destinations.

The principal protected areas include Makgadikgadi Pans Game Reserve and Nxai Pan National Park, while Nata Sanctuary is a separate community conservation area.

This distinction is important when planning accommodation and activities because the rules, access and wildlife experience differ between locations.

When is the best time to visit Makgadikgadi?

It depends on your priority.

For zebra and wildebeest movement, green landscapes and seasonal bird activity, the rainy period can be very rewarding.

For dry landscapes, easier general travel conditions and classic salt-pan scenery, the dry season is more suitable.

Flamingo viewing is particularly dependent on whether sufficient water reaches the appropriate pans, so no responsible operator should guarantee large flamingo concentrations for a specific date.

Can I see flamingos in Makgadikgadi?

Yes, when conditions are right.

Sowa Pan and Nata Sanctuary can attract very large numbers of flamingos when appropriate water and food conditions develop.

Botswana Tourism notes that flamingo numbers can reach tens of thousands and, in exceptional circumstances, hundreds of thousands across the Makgadikgadi system.

The important qualification is that flamingo concentrations are highly seasonal and rainfall-dependent.

Where are the largest Makgadikgadi salt pans?

The two major pans are Ntwetwe Pan and Sowa Pan.

They form part of a much larger network of smaller pans and associated grasslands.

Is Makgadikgadi good for wildlife?

Yes, particularly during productive seasonal periods.

The Makgadikgadi Pans Game Reserve can provide sightings of zebra, wildebeest, gemsbok, springbok, giraffe, eland and other antelope, together with predators such as lion, cheetah and hyena.

However, wildlife density changes considerably with rainfall and migration.

It should not be compared directly with Chobe's dry-season elephant concentrations or the Okavango's highly productive wetland habitats.

Can I see the zebra migration in Makgadikgadi?

You can experience the seasonal zebra and wildebeest movements associated with the Makgadikgadi and Boteti ecosystem.

The exact timing and location depend on rainfall and grazing conditions.

This is why a safari planned around the migration should allow flexibility rather than relying on a fixed assumption that the animals will be in one particular location on one particular date.

What is the difference between Makgadikgadi and Nxai Pan?

Nxai Pan is a national park within the greater Makgadikgadi complex.

Its former salt pans are now largely grassed, creating an environment that can support substantial wildlife concentrations, including zebra, wildebeest, springbok, giraffe and predators.

The broader Makgadikgadi landscape is more strongly associated with the enormous salt pans, especially Ntwetwe and Sowa.

Is Makgadikgadi suitable for families?

Yes.

Families can combine wildlife drives with educational experiences involving geology, wildlife adaptation, astronomy, baobabs and, where responsibly operated, meerkat or cultural activities.

The main consideration is heat and driving time. A well-paced itinerary with suitable accommodation is much better than trying to cover the entire region quickly.

Is Makgadikgadi good for a honeymoon?

Yes, particularly for couples who prefer unusual landscapes and wilderness over conventional resort experiences.

The salt pans create exceptional sunset and stargazing opportunities, while private camps can provide a high degree of privacy.

Combining Makgadikgadi with the Okavango Delta can create an especially varied honeymoon.

How many days do I need in Makgadikgadi?

Three nights is a good starting point.

Four or five nights are preferable if the destination itself is a major focus of the trip.

If you are combining it with Okavango, Chobe or Moremi, two to three nights can work well as a contrasting component.

Can you visit Makgadikgadi and Okavango Delta on the same safari?

Yes, and the combination makes excellent ecological sense.

The Okavango provides a wetland environment shaped by permanent and seasonal water, while Makgadikgadi demonstrates the opposite extreme: ancient salt pans and seasonally flooded grasslands.

Together they show two dramatically different expressions of the wider Botswana landscape.

What are Baines' Baobabs?

Baines' Baobabs are a group of seven large baobab trees overlooking Kudiakam Pan.

They were named after explorer and artist Thomas Baines, who recorded and painted them during his travels through the region. Botswana Tourism notes that the landscape remains remarkably similar to his historic depiction.

Is Makgadikgadi good for photography?

It is one of Botswana's strongest destinations for landscape photography.

The enormous horizons, salt textures, baobabs, reflections, wildlife silhouettes and dramatic seasonal skies provide compositions that are very different from conventional safari photography.

The dry and wet seasons can produce almost completely different photographic environments.

Can I drive on the salt pans?

Access depends on the specific pan, conditions and local regulations.

Travelers should never assume that every part of a salt pan is safe or appropriate for driving. Wet salt surfaces can be fragile and vehicles can become seriously stuck.

Always follow local guide and park-management instructions.

What should I combine with Makgadikgadi?

The strongest combinations include the Okavango Delta for wetlands, Moremi for diverse wildlife habitats, Chobe for elephants and riverfront wildlife, the Central Kalahari for Kalahari predators and wilderness, and Nxai Pan for wildlife and seasonal pans.

For first-time Botswana visitors, the Okavango and Makgadikgadi combination is particularly effective because the ecological contrast is so strong.
