Moremi Game Reserve

Botswana's Big Five reserve in the Okavango: game drives, self-drive camping and water safaris.

Moremi Game Reserve is the protected heart of the Okavango Delta in northern Botswana. Proclaimed by the Batawana people in 1963, it safeguards around 40% of the UNESCO World Heritage property and combines floodplains, lagoons, woodland and dryland within a single landscape. It is also one of Botswana's best-known self-drive destinations and, following the reintroduction of black and white rhinos, a Big Five address.

Wildlife in Moremi

Moremi's mixture of habitats supports a dense concentration of wildlife, from large herds on the floodplains to predators in the woodland.

African wild dog

African wild dogs are one of the most exciting species for experienced safari travelers.

The species is endangered, and the wider Okavango ecosystem is an important stronghold for wild dogs. UNESCO identifies African wild dog among the threatened large mammals supported by the Delta.

Wild dogs are highly mobile and can cover substantial distances. Finding a pack therefore depends heavily on current movements.

When encountered, however, their social behavior can produce extraordinary sightings, particularly when a pack is preparing for or returning from a hunt.

Cheetah

Cheetahs occur in the Moremi ecosystem, particularly where more open habitat provides suitable hunting conditions.

Compared with lions and leopards, cheetahs have a very different hunting strategy. They depend on speed and therefore benefit from open terrain where they can see and pursue prey.

Rhinoceros in Moremi

Rhino conservation has an important history in Moremi.

Botswana Tourism states that black and white rhinos have been reintroduced, contributing to Moremi's status as a Big Five destination.

The reintroduction programme has a much longer history than the current tourism description suggests. A major Botswana rhino recovery project began in 2001, with white rhinos introduced to Moremi and subsequent black rhino translocations. The programme involved the Botswana government, Department of Wildlife and National Parks and conservation partners.

This makes a rhino sighting in Moremi more than simply a Big Five encounter: it represents part of a longer conservation effort to restore species that had been severely depleted in Botswana.

Red lechwe: a Moremi specialist

The red lechwe is one of the antelope species particularly associated with the Okavango environment.

Unlike many antelope, red lechwe are adapted to spending considerable time around water. They can move through shallow flooded areas and marshland with remarkable agility.

Seeing lechwe moving through floodwater is one of the experiences that distinguishes Moremi from more conventional dry-savannah reserves.

Hippos and crocodiles

The Delta's permanent waterways support both hippos and crocodiles.

These animals are particularly interesting when observed from boats because water provides their natural context. A boat safari can reveal behavior that may not be visible from a vehicle on land.

Hippos can be heard before they are seen, while crocodiles may lie almost completely motionless along the edges of channels.

Birdwatching in Moremi

Moremi is an excellent destination for birdwatchers. Botswana Tourism records over 400 bird species in the reserve, including migratory and endangered species.

The mixture of wetlands, woodland, grassland and forest provides many ecological niches. Possible sightings include:

  • African fish eagle
  • Kingfishers
  • Herons
  • Egrets
  • Storks
  • Bee-eaters
  • Hornbills
  • Jacanas
  • Wattled crane
  • Southern ground hornbill
  • Various raptors
  • Waterbirds

Birding is particularly rewarding during boat and mokoro excursions because the traveler moves slowly through habitats where birds concentrate.

The best safari activities in Moremi

Moremi is valuable because it supports more than one type of safari.

Game drives

Game drives are the core activity for most visitors. The reserve's network of tracks allows guides to explore different habitats in search of predators, herbivores and birds.

Early morning and late afternoon drives are particularly productive because temperatures are generally cooler and many animals become more active.

Boat safaris

Where water conditions and location permit, boat excursions offer a completely different perspective. Rather than searching primarily through woodland and grassland, travelers can explore:

  • Channels
  • Lagoons
  • Papyrus-lined waterways
  • Floodplains
  • Islands

Boat safaris are particularly useful for observing:

  • Hippos
  • Crocodiles
  • Waterbirds
  • Elephants near water
  • Aquatic landscapes

Mokoro excursions

Mokoro excursions are among the most iconic activities associated with the Okavango.

The traditional-style canoe moves quietly through shallow channels, providing an almost silent way of exploring the wetland. The greatest value of a mokoro is not necessarily seeing the largest animals. Instead, it offers a close encounter with:

  • Water plants
  • Reeds
  • Lily-covered channels
  • Birds
  • Smaller animals
  • Reflections
  • The structure of the wetland itself

Whether a mokoro excursion is possible depends on the specific area and prevailing water conditions.

Walking safaris

Walking experiences can be available in appropriate areas and under professional guidance.

Walking changes the purpose of a safari. From a vehicle, travelers often concentrate on large animals. On foot, the guide can interpret:

  • Tracks
  • Droppings
  • Plants
  • Insects
  • Birds
  • Animal behavior
  • Signs of predators
  • The relationship between wildlife and habitat

Because Moremi is genuine wildlife habitat, walking should only be undertaken under the conditions and supervision permitted by the relevant operator or authority.

Self-drive safari in Moremi

Moremi is one of Botswana's best-known destinations for self-drive camping. Botswana Tourism specifically identifies Moremi as a top destination for self-drive campers.

However, this is not a conventional road trip. Botswana Tourism warns that self-drive travelers may encounter:

  • Remote terrain
  • Deep sand
  • Rough tracks
  • Limited fuel
  • Limited food supplies
  • Limited water
  • Unfenced campsites
  • Wildlife around campsites

The tourism authority recommends substantial preparation, including adequate water, fuel, spare tires and vehicle parts.

For inexperienced off-road drivers, a guided safari is generally a much more comfortable option.

Camping in Moremi

Camping provides one of the most immersive ways to experience the reserve. Campsites inside wildlife areas may not be fenced, which means travelers are camping within genuine wildlife habitat.

That creates an extraordinary atmosphere - but it also means normal camping rules are not optional. Travelers should:

  • Never walk around camp casually at night
  • Follow ranger and camp-staff instructions
  • Never approach wildlife
  • Keep food properly stored
  • Avoid leaving waste outside
  • Use designated facilities
  • Carry adequate supplies when self-driving

The attraction of Moremi camping is precisely that the wilderness is not completely separated from the campsite.

The Third Bridge area

Third Bridge is one of Moremi's well-known camping areas. Botswana Tourism describes the campsite as being near the Sekiri River and surrounded by papyrus vegetation.

Its location provides the kind of landscape many travelers associate with Moremi: water, papyrus, woodland, wildlife and open wilderness.

For self-drive travelers, Third Bridge can form part of a multi-night Moremi route rather than simply being a place to spend one night.

Best time to visit Moremi

Moremi can be visited throughout the year, but different seasons produce noticeably different safari experiences.

May to August

This period combines Botswana's dry season with the developing Okavango flood. Conditions are generally cooler and drier, while water becomes increasingly important to wildlife.

This is one of the most popular periods for classic Botswana safari travel.

September to October

The landscape becomes progressively drier and hotter. Water becomes increasingly concentrated in permanent channels and remaining wet areas.

This can create excellent opportunities for wildlife viewing because animals may become increasingly dependent on reliable water sources. However, temperatures can become very high, particularly in October.

November to March

The summer rainy season changes the appearance of Moremi. Vegetation becomes greener, thunderstorms become more common and many animals enter breeding periods. Bird activity can be particularly rewarding.

The downside is that rain can make some tracks difficult, while wildlife may be more dispersed because water is available across a larger area.

April

April is a transitional month. The landscape begins moving from the rainy season toward the dry season, while the Okavango flood cycle becomes increasingly important. Conditions can vary considerably from one year to another.

Moremi during high water

High water does not mean that every road becomes inaccessible. However, water levels can significantly change road conditions and access routes.

This is not theoretical. In March 2026, severe flooding caused a strategic road into Moremi to close for several weeks, while some routes and airstrips were also affected. The road reopened in April, although authorities reported that some routes remained impassable because water levels were still high.

This illustrates an important planning point: Moremi is a living wetland, not a static safari landscape. Access conditions can change with water levels.

How many nights do you need in Moremi?

2 nights

Two nights are enough for a short introduction. You can experience several game drives and potentially one water-based activity if the area and season permit.

3 nights

Three nights provide a much better introduction. There is enough time to explore multiple habitats and avoid making the entire experience feel rushed.

4 nights

Four nights are excellent for travelers whose primary goal is wildlife. A longer stay also provides more opportunities for finding elusive species such as leopard and wild dog.

5+ nights

Longer stays make sense when combining Moremi with another part of the Okavango or when traveling slowly. Rather than simply adding more nights in exactly the same place, consider combining:

  • Moremi + Khwai + Okavango Delta
  • Moremi + Savuti + Chobe

Moremi safari for families

Moremi can be an extraordinary family destination, but the itinerary must be designed carefully. Families should consider:

  • Children's ages
  • Camp age restrictions
  • Length of game drives
  • Availability of family rooms
  • Swimming-pool facilities
  • Child-friendly meals
  • Walking restrictions
  • Flight luggage allowances
  • Transfer times

Young children may find long game drives tiring. Older children, however, can become deeply engaged when guides explain animal tracks, insects, birds and conservation rather than focusing only on the Big Five.

Moremi honeymoon safari

Moremi works exceptionally well as part of a Botswana honeymoon. The combination of remote camps, waterways, wildlife, private decks, sunset experiences, bush dinners, small camps and fly-in transfers creates a very different honeymoon atmosphere from a conventional beach resort.

A particularly strong honeymoon combination is Moremi → Okavango private concession → Victoria Falls, or Moremi → Khwai → Chobe → Victoria Falls.

Moremi vs Okavango Delta

This is one of the most important distinctions for travelers. Moremi is a protected game reserve of approximately 3,900 km² where game drives are central and the landscape contains floodplains and dryland. Self-drive is possible, and Moremi is part of the Okavango.

The Okavango Delta is a large wetland ecosystem and a much larger one at that, where land and water experiences combine. It includes the wider Delta system, many remote areas are fly-in only, and it includes Moremi.

Moremi is therefore part of the Okavango story, not a separate ecosystem from it.

Moremi vs Chobe

Moremi and Chobe make an excellent combination. Moremi's strengths include:

  • Delta wetlands
  • Floodplains
  • Diverse habitats
  • Predators
  • Wild dogs
  • Water-based activities
  • Remote safari atmosphere

Chobe is particularly renowned for:

  • Large elephant concentrations
  • Chobe River wildlife
  • River cruises
  • Northern Botswana's dry woodland and river ecosystem

Combining them creates a more varied northern Botswana itinerary.

Moremi vs Khwai

Moremi and Khwai are geographically and ecologically connected but offer different tourism experiences.

Moremi is a formal game reserve. Khwai is associated with community-based wildlife management and tourism outside the reserve.

For travelers interested in conservation and community tourism, combining the two can provide a broader understanding of how northern Botswana manages wildlife beyond traditional national parks and reserves.

Conservation and community importance

Moremi's conservation story begins with the community. The reserve was established because local residents recognized that wildlife was being depleted and took action to protect it.

Today, Moremi forms part of a larger conservation landscape in which government authorities, communities and private tourism operators all play different roles.

The broader Okavango World Heritage property consists of multiple management systems, including Moremi, Wildlife Management Areas and community/private concession arrangements.

The current Okavango Delta Management Plan also emphasizes stronger stakeholder engagement and community-based conservation. UNESCO's latest conservation decision specifically welcomes progress in implementing the 2021-2028 management plan and calls for continued attention to tourism pressure, pollution, water use, human-elephant conflict, wildfires and invasive species.

Moremi's role in the wider Okavango ecosystem

Moremi should not be isolated from the rest of the Delta. Wildlife does not recognize reserve boundaries.

Elephants, buffalo, predators and other animals move between protected areas, community lands and concessions depending on water, food, breeding and seasonal conditions. This makes the wider landscape approach particularly important.

UNESCO notes that much of the Okavango's large fauna moves beyond the boundaries of the World Heritage property and that maintaining ecological connectivity is important to the system's long-term integrity.

How to get to Moremi

By air

Flying is the easiest option for many international safari travelers. Travelers normally connect through Maun and then continue by light aircraft to an airstrip serving their camp.

This is particularly useful for luxury travelers staying in remote lodges.

By 4x4

Moremi can also be reached overland. This is particularly popular among self-drive travelers and mobile safari operators. However, road conditions vary significantly and can change according to flooding and rainfall.

Moremi Game Reserve FAQs

Is Moremi Game Reserve inside the Okavango Delta?

Yes. Moremi occupies part of the Okavango Delta and represents one of the principal protected areas within the wider wetland ecosystem.

UNESCO states that around 40% of the Okavango World Heritage property is protected within Moremi Game Reserve.

This is why Moremi and Okavango are often mentioned together, although they are not technically the same thing.

Is Moremi a national park?

No. Moremi is a game reserve.

This distinction matters because Botswana has several different types of protected and managed wildlife areas, including national parks, game reserves, Wildlife Management Areas and private/community concessions.

Moremi was originally proclaimed as a game reserve by the Batawana people in 1963.

Can I see the Big Five in Moremi?

Yes, Moremi is now promoted by Botswana Tourism as a Big Five destination, following the reintroduction of black and white rhinoceros.

But Big Five status should not be interpreted as a guarantee that every traveler will see all five animals.

Elephants, buffalo and lions are generally much more likely encounters than rhinos, while leopard sightings depend heavily on timing and luck.

Is Moremi good for self-drive safaris?

Yes, but it is for experienced and properly equipped 4x4 travelers, not a casual road trip.

Botswana Tourism specifically identifies Moremi as a leading self-drive camping destination but warns that travelers may encounter deep sand, rough terrain, limited supplies and unfenced campsites.

If you have little off-road experience, a guided safari is safer and considerably easier.

Can I do a mokoro safari in Moremi?

Potentially, depending on the area, water levels and operator.

Mokoro activities are strongly associated with the Okavango ecosystem, but not every section of Moremi has identical water conditions.

This is why an itinerary should specify the actual area and camp rather than simply promising a mokoro safari in Moremi.

Can I take a boat safari in Moremi?

Yes, where waterways and local operating conditions permit.

Boat trips are particularly useful for exploring the aquatic side of the ecosystem and observing hippos, crocodiles, birds and elephants near water.

The availability of boating depends on the specific area and seasonal water conditions.

What is the best month for Moremi?

There is no single best month for every traveler.

June to August is particularly attractive for travelers wanting cooler dry-season conditions combined with the developing Okavango flood.

September and October can be excellent for terrestrial wildlife because the landscape becomes increasingly dry, but temperatures rise considerably. November to March provides greener scenery, summer rains and strong birding opportunities.

The best month therefore depends on whether your priority is water, predators, general wildlife, birding, photography or weather.

How many days do I need in Moremi?

Three nights is a good starting point. Four nights provides more time to explore different habitats and improves your chances of finding elusive wildlife.

If Moremi is part of a larger Botswana safari, two or three nights may be sufficient before continuing to Khwai, Savuti or Chobe.

Is Moremi suitable for a honeymoon?

Yes. Moremi can provide a highly private wilderness experience, particularly when paired with an intimate lodge or private concession.

For honeymoon travelers, the strongest itineraries usually combine wildlife activities with enough downtime to enjoy the camp rather than filling every hour with game drives.

Is Moremi suitable for families?

Yes, provided the accommodation and activities are selected around the children's ages. Some camps have age restrictions, particularly for walking activities.

Families should therefore check the exact camp policy before booking.

Older children often enjoy Moremi particularly well because the variety of wildlife and habitats gives guides plenty to explain beyond simply looking for lions.

Is Moremi better than the Serengeti?

Neither is universally better. They provide different safari experiences.

The Serengeti is particularly famous for enormous open plains and the Great Migration. Moremi is defined by the meeting of Delta waterways, floodplains, woodland and dryland habitats.

Choose Moremi if you want a wetland-focused Botswana safari with game drives and potential water activities. Choose Serengeti if your priority is vast savannah landscapes and the Great Migration.

Is Moremi better than Chobe?

They are complementary. Moremi is stronger for travelers wanting the combination of Delta wetlands, predators, diverse habitats and water-based safari experiences.

Chobe is particularly strong for elephants and river-based wildlife viewing. A northern Botswana itinerary can easily include both.

What is the difference between Moremi and Chief's Island?

Chief's Island is a major geographical feature within Moremi Game Reserve. Moremi is the protected area; Chief's Island is one of its best-known islands.

Chief's Island is surrounded by Okavango wetland habitats and is particularly associated with high-quality wildlife viewing and exclusive safari camps.

Does Moremi have rhinos?

Yes. Both black and white rhinos have been reintroduced into the Moremi/Okavango landscape as part of Botswana's longer-term rhino conservation programme. Botswana Tourism identifies the reintroduced rhinos as one reason Moremi is now considered a Big Five destination.

However, rhinos remain considerably less predictable to see than elephants or buffalo.

Is Moremi affected by flooding?

Yes, and flooding is an important part of the ecosystem. The same water that makes Moremi exceptionally productive can also affect roads, tracks and airstrips.

In 2026, severe flooding caused a major Moremi access road to close temporarily, demonstrating how strongly water conditions can affect tourism logistics.

Travelers should therefore confirm current road and access conditions when planning self-drive or road-based safaris.
