Why this 8-day Botswana safari itinerary

8 days on the Botswana circuit: Okavango Delta, Moremi / Khwai, Chobe. Every day below is a real day on the ground, not a transfer with a game drive attached.

Why Okavango Delta comes first

The flood arrives when the surrounding country is driest, so the delta is the ecological centre of the trip - and that is where the route opens.

Chobe gets 4 nights

Chobe holds 4 of the 8 days, and that is deliberate: river days and game-drive days are different things, and consecutive nights let both happen.

What 8 days buys

8 days moves past the point of rushing: whole days rather than stops, and enough slack that weather or wildlife can bend the plan without breaking it.

Getting around Botswana

Botswana moves by light aircraft between camps, because the road distances between the delta and the Chobe riverfront would eat the safari. The delta itself is boat and mokoro rather than road, and the concessions around Khwai and Moremi allow night drives and off-road tracking that the national parks do not.

Best time to book

Botswana runs on two seasons. The one you pick changes what you see, what the camps cost and how far ahead you need to commit.

Dry season

May to October is the dry season and the classic window: animals concentrate on the permanent water, the delta flood arrives from the north, and the bush is at its most open. It is also the high season for both price and camps.

Green season

November to April is the green season, when the rains transform the pans, the birding peaks and the newborn animals arrive. Rates fall substantially and the parks are close to empty.

What actually drives the timing

The Okavango flood is the exception to every seasonal rule: it arrives from Angola during the dry months, so the delta is fullest from June to August precisely when the surrounding country is driest. Makgadikgadi works the other way, with the summer rains turning the pans green from December.

How far ahead to book

June to August and the Christmas window want six to twelve months of notice, because delta camps are small and fly-in seat counts are fixed. Green-season Botswana often works on two to four months.

How do we get the best price without hurting the trip?

What is the best month for this trip?

What vaccinations and malaria prevention do we need?

Yellow fever certificates are required when arriving from endemic countries; malaria prophylaxis is recommended for most safari regions - discuss options with your doctor 6 to 8 weeks out. We brief every trip on water, sun and insect precautions.

CDC guidance for the region is precise. Yellow fever vaccination is recommended for all safari travelers and becomes a certificate requirement when you arrive via an endemic country such as Rwanda or Uganda. Routine boosters, hepatitis A, typhoid and hepatitis B round out the travel clinic list. Malaria transmission covers every game park below roughly 2,500 meters, the parasite is chloroquine resistant, and the preventatives that work are atovaquone-proguanil, doxycycline, mefloquine or tafenoquine, started before arrival and finished after. Book the clinic six to eight weeks out, and pack repellent with 20 percent DEET plus long sleeves for dusk.

What photography gear should we bring?

A 100 to 400mm zoom covers 90 percent of sightings; add a wide second body for landscapes. Beanbags beat tripods in vehicles. We provide charging setups and dust management briefings - tell us your kit and targets.

How do internal flights and luggage limits work?

Bush flights allow 15 to 20kg in soft duffels; lodges store hard cases free. We route connections with realistic margins and meet you at every airstrip - missed-connection cover is built into fly-in trips.
